# Secret Society True Origins

The founding stories are propaganda. Here's what really happened—the theft, betrayal, and murder at the heart of each organization.

---

## The Problem with Origin Stories

Every secret society tells a noble founding story. The Inheritors preserved knowledge for humanity. The Order of Dawn arose to protect the innocent. The Veil Keepers formed to expose truth.

These stories are useful. They attract recruits. They justify actions. They create identity.

They're also lies.

The truth is messier. Every society was founded on betrayal. Every noble purpose masks an original sin. The founders weren't heroes—they were thieves, murderers, and opportunists who became heroes later because they wrote their own histories.

---

## The Inheritors of Blood

### The Official Story

*"Founded in Year 812 by scholars who discovered vampire knowledge and swore to preserve it for humanity's benefit. They have protected dangerous secrets for four hundred years, preventing catastrophe while advancing understanding."*

### The True Origin

#### Year 789: The Theft

The Inheritors weren't founded by scholars. They were founded by thieves.

**The Founders**:
- Aldric Shadowmend: A tomb robber who specialized in vampire ruins
- Sera Nighthollow: A forger who created fake provenance documents
- Marcus Veil: A murderer fleeing prosecution in Valdris

These three had been raiding vampire sites for years, selling artifacts to private collectors. In Year 789, they found something different: Sel'Naroth.

#### The Sel'Naroth Discovery

Sel'Naroth wasn't just a ruin—it was a Mind covenant research facility with intact records. The three thieves realized they'd found something worth more than any artifact: **knowledge**.

But they had a problem. The records were in vampire script. They couldn't read them.

#### The Scholar Murders

Aldric recruited scholars to translate—three academics from minor institutions who wouldn't be missed. They paid well. The scholars worked for months, translating the most accessible documents.

Then they learned what they'd found: instructions for blood magic, artifact creation, Bloodweb fragments. Dangerous, valuable, and illegal.

The scholars wanted to go public. "This belongs to everyone," they said.

Aldric disagreed.

Three scholars entered Sel'Naroth. None left. The official story says they died in a cave-in. The cave-in happened—after they were already dead.

**Victims**:
- Scholar Merik Ashford, specialist in vampire linguistics
- Scholar Denna Thornwood, specialist in magical theory
- Scholar Aldric Vane, specialist in pre-Collapse history

#### Year 789-812: The Building

With the scholars dead, the three thieves held the only translations. But they couldn't use them—they weren't mages or scholars. They needed people who could apply the knowledge.

So they recruited. Carefully. They found mages who wanted power, scholars who wanted truth, soldiers who wanted purpose. They created the Inheritors.

The new recruits were told a simple story: the founders had discovered this knowledge and wanted to preserve it. Noble purpose. Clean hands.

Only the three founders knew the truth. When Aldric died in Year 834, he passed the secret to one successor. When Sera died in Year 856, she did the same. Marcus lived until Year 891, the last original founder.

By then, the Inheritors believed their own mythology.

#### The Year 812 "Discovery"

The Year 812 discovery wasn't really a discovery—it was a reveal. The Inheritors had known about Sel'Naroth for twenty-three years. They "discovered" it publicly when they needed legitimacy.

Why Year 812? Because a rival group was getting close. The Inheritors had to claim the site officially or lose it. So they staged a discovery, filed the claims, and established themselves as legitimate researchers.

The stasis vampire they "found"? They'd known about it since Year 789. They just weren't ready to wake it. They still aren't sure if it was the same one or a different chamber.

### The Implications

**The Inheritors are built on murder**. Three scholars died so the founders could keep their monopoly. Every Inheritor since has benefited from that crime.

**The knowledge was stolen, not preserved**. Sel'Naroth belongs to everyone—or no one. The Inheritors took it and kept it.

**The Year 812 discovery was staged**. The Northern Kingdom's "legitimate claim" is based on a lie.

**Do current Inheritors know?** Some senior members suspect. The leadership chain passes the truth from Vera Nighthollow (current leader) back to Aldric Shadowmend. She knows. She doesn't share.

### The Descendants

The three murdered scholars left families. Those families still exist in Year 1200—and they're closer to the truth than Vera wants to admit.

**The Ashford Family**:
Helena Ashford is a senior Veil Keeper archivist. She's already suspicious of the Inheritors (she's been copying their sealed texts). She doesn't know her ancestor was murdered by them—yet. When she finds out, she'll have both the motive and the resources to destroy them.

**The Thornwood Family**:
Marcus Thornwood is a Valdrian Academy historian specializing in the Lost Centuries. His family has always believed Denna died in a "tragic accident" at Sel'Naroth. He's been petitioning for access to the site for twenty years. The Inheritors keep blocking him. He thinks they're protecting dangerous knowledge. He's half right.

**The Vane Family**:
Lady Cassandra Vane is a minor noble in the Maritime Federation. Her family lost status after Aldric Vane's "disappearance"—they were suspected of involvement in illegal excavation. She's spent her life trying to restore her family name. She believes clearing Aldric's reputation requires proving the Inheritors falsified the cave-in story. She's right.

**The Convergence**:
None of the three families know the others exist. If they discovered their connection—three scholars, one expedition, all dead—they would compare notes. The pattern would be obvious.

Vera Nighthollow knows about all three families. She has people watching them. If any of them gets too close to the truth, she'll have to decide: silence them like her ancestors did, or let the Inheritors' founding crime destroy everything they've built.

## The Order of Dawn

### The Official Story

*"Founded in Year 634 by Aldric the Lightbringer after his family was killed by vampire cultists. The Order arose to protect humanity from vampire corruption, hunting the remnants of the blood lords' evil."*

### The True Origin

#### Year 631: The Massacre

Aldric's family wasn't killed by vampire cultists. They were killed by Aldric.

**The Situation**:
Aldric Dawnson was a minor noble in what would become the Valdrian Empire. His family had a secret: they'd found a vampire artifact in Year 580 and had been using it. The artifact granted long life and vitality in exchange for blood—not vampire blood magic, but close enough.

For fifty years, the Dawnson family quietly thrived. They lived longer, stayed healthier, won more battles than they should have. They became powerful.

They also became dependent.

#### The Artifact's Toll

The artifact demanded more blood as time passed. First animal blood. Then criminal blood. Then... the family started looking at servants differently.

Aldric saw what his family was becoming. His father had killed three servants. His brother was eyeing the village children. His mother spoke about "acceptable sacrifices."

Aldric decided to end it.

#### The Night of Fire

On the winter solstice of Year 631, Aldric burned his family home with everyone inside. Parents, siblings, servants, guards—anyone who knew about the artifact.

He took the artifact into the wilderness and tried to destroy it. He failed. Vampire artifacts don't break easily.

So he buried it. Deep. He's the only one who knew where—and he never told anyone.

Then he created a story: vampire cultists attacked his family. He alone survived. He swore vengeance.

#### Year 631-634: The Transformation

Aldric spent three years transforming himself from patricide to prophet. He studied vampire lore. He learned what arguments would work. He practiced his story until he believed it.

In Year 634, he emerged as Aldric the Lightbringer with a mission: destroy all vampire corruption. Hunt the cultists. Protect humanity.

People flocked to him. He was noble, grieving, righteous. He'd lost everything to vampire evil. Who better to lead the fight?

#### The Order's Foundation

The Order of Dawn wasn't founded to protect humanity. It was founded to **ensure no one else found what Aldric had found**.

He needed people searching for vampire artifacts. He needed to know where they were. He needed to control them—and destroy them when possible.

The Order does hunt vampire corruption. That part is real. But the founding purpose was cover-up, not protection.

#### The Artifact's Location

Aldric buried the artifact somewhere in what's now the Northern Kingdom. The exact location died with him in Year 687.

But the artifact is still there. Still buried. Still demanding blood.

Some Order members have noticed patterns: disappearances near certain Northern villages, livestock deaths, children going missing. They attribute it to vampire remnants.

They're right. But not the way they think.

### The Implications

**The Order was founded by a mass murderer**. Aldric killed dozens of people, including his own family. Everything that followed was to cover that crime.

**An active vampire artifact is still buried**. It's been drawing blood for 500+ years. Something has been feeding it—or it's been taking what it needs.

**The Order hunts what its founder created**. If the artifact is found and traced to Aldric, the Order's entire identity collapses.

**Do current members know?** No. The secret died with Aldric. But some senior members have noticed that original documents about the founding are suspiciously incomplete. Brother Aldric Valorian has been quietly investigating.

## The Veil Keepers

### The Official Story

*"Founded in Year 423 by scholars who believed truth should be preserved, not hidden. They have documented history's secrets for eight hundred years, maintaining records that powerful forces want destroyed."*

### The True Origin

#### Year 423: The Cover-Up

The Veil Keepers weren't founded to expose truth. They were founded to **bury it**.

**The Context**:
Year 423 fell during the Silent Years (423-445)—the period with almost no surviving records. The Veil Keepers know why those records don't exist. They destroyed them.

#### The Second Collapse

Something happened during the Silent Years that terrified the powers of that era. The records call it the "Second Collapse"—not a repeat of Year 0, but something that threatened similar devastation.

The details are lost, but fragments suggest:
- Dimensional instability from The Scar
- Attempted reconstruction of vampire magic
- Mass deaths in multiple kingdoms
- Something that emerged and was barely contained

The powers of that era—ancestors of current Valdrian nobility, early Church, proto-Northern Kingdom—agreed that the truth was too dangerous. If people knew what had happened, panic would destroy civilization.

So they erased it.

#### The Founders

The Veil Keepers were originally the **Keepers of the Veil**—the veil being the cover-up, not the hidden truth.

**The Original Members**:
- Court historians ordered to destroy records
- Church scribes who altered documents
- Noble archivists who burned family papers

These were the people who executed the cover-up. They weren't preserving truth—they were destroying it.

#### The Transformation

Over generations, the organization changed. The original members died. New recruits joined who didn't know the founding purpose. The name was reinterpreted: they weren't keeping the veil in place—they were keeping track of what was behind it.

By Year 600, the Veil Keepers had reversed their mission. They were now trying to recover the truth their founders had destroyed.

The irony is sharp: they search for records their own organization burned.

#### What They Destroyed

The Veil Keepers' founders destroyed records of:
- The Second Collapse's true cause
- Mass casualties (possibly millions)
- Who was responsible
- What emerged from The Scar
- How it was contained
- What failed containment would mean

Some of these records were the only copies. The truth is permanently lost.

Other records were copies—originals might exist in sealed archives, dwarven vaults, or elven libraries. The current Veil Keepers desperately seek these, not knowing their predecessors are why they're needed.

### The Implications

**The Veil Keepers destroyed what they now seek**. The organization that champions truth was founded to suppress it.

**The Silent Years cover-up was organized, not accidental**. The missing records were deliberately erased by the very people now trying to find them.

**The Second Collapse may repeat**. If the original threat wasn't truly eliminated—if it was just contained or suppressed—it could emerge again. The Scar is growing.

**Do current members know?** Senior Keeper Helena Ashford found a fragment in Year 1178 that suggested the original purpose. She's been investigating quietly, but hasn't shared her findings. She's afraid of what it means for everything they've built.

## Connections Between Societies

### The Triangle of Lies

Each society knows one of the others' secrets but not their own:

**The Inheritors** have documents proving the Order of Dawn was founded on a massacre. They use this as leverage.

**The Order of Dawn** has evidence that the Veil Keepers destroyed records during the Silent Years. They use this to discredit Keeper revelations.

**The Veil Keepers** have fragments suggesting the Inheritors murdered scholars at Sel'Naroth. They've been investigating.

None of them know their own true origins—or they know and guard the secret fiercely.

### The Shared Guilt

All three societies:
- Were founded on betrayal or murder
- Serve purposes their founders would hate
- Have leadership that knows or suspects
- Would collapse if members learned the truth

They need each other. If one society's true origin is exposed, the others become vulnerable. Mutually assured destruction keeps everyone silent.

### The Convergence

The three societies are converging on the same secrets from different angles:

**Inheritors** want Vessel Seven to unlock vampire knowledge—including Sel'Naroth's deeper vaults they've never accessed.

**Order of Dawn** wants to destroy Vessel Seven as vampire corruption—and might find the buried artifact in doing so.

**Veil Keepers** want to document Seven's awakening—and might finally uncover what happened in Year 423-445.

Each investigation threatens to expose another society's origin. The next few years will either destroy all three organizations or bind them together in shared cover-up.

## The Current Leadership

### Vera Nighthollow (Inheritors)

Direct descendant of Sera Nighthollow, one of the original murderous founders. She knows everything. She's used the knowledge to maintain power within the organization.

**Her secret fear**: The scholars' descendants are still out there. The Ashford, Thornwood, and Vane families. If they learned the truth, they'd have righteous vengeance claims.

### Brother Aldric Valorian (Order of Dawn)

Named after the founder but suspicious of the founding story. He's found gaps in the records, inconsistencies in the narrative. He doesn't know about the massacre yet, but he's looking.

**His secret fear**: What if Aldric the Lightbringer wasn't what they think? What would the Order be if its founder was a monster?

### Helena Ashford (Veil Keepers)

Senior Keeper who found the fragment about the original purpose. She's a descendant of the Ashford family—the same family whose scholar was murdered by the Inheritors' founders.

**Her secret fear**: Her organization destroyed the truth. Her family was killed for knowledge. Everything she's worked for is tainted.

## Implications for the Story

### When Seven Awakens

Each society will try to control him:

**Inheritors** want his knowledge but fear what he might reveal about Sel'Naroth and Year 812.

**Order of Dawn** wants him destroyed but risk exposing their founder's crime if they dig too deep into vampire artifacts.

**Veil Keepers** want to document everything but risk revealing their own destructive past.

Seven is a living connection to the past all three societies have corrupted. His memories might contain the truth about Sel'Naroth, the Year 423-445 events, or even what really happened to Aldric's family.

### The Exposure Risk

If Seven reveals:
- The scholar murders → Inheritors lose legitimacy
- The Dawnson massacre → Order loses moral authority
- The record destruction → Veil Keepers lose credibility

Every society has reason to control what he says. Every society has reason to silence him. And every society is watching the others for signs of betrayal.

### The Redemption Question

Each society has become something better than what it was founded to be:

- Inheritors do preserve knowledge (even if they stole it)
- Order of Dawn does protect people (even if founded on murder)
- Veil Keepers do expose truth (even if they destroyed records)

Do the current members deserve to suffer for founders' sins? Does good work redeem bad origins?

Seven might be the one who has to decide.
