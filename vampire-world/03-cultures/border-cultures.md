# Border Cultures

## Overview
Where empires meet, unique hybrid cultures emerge over generations.

---

## Northern-Valdrian Marches

### Location
Strip of territory along North Pass, claimed by both empires.

### Culture
- **Language**: Mix of Northern directness and Valdrian formal vocabulary
- **Religion**: Church presence but pragmatic interpretation
- **Economy**: Smuggling as legitimate profession
- **Identity**: "We're neither—we're Marchers"

### Characteristics
- Play both empires against each other for advantage
- Intermarriage common (Northern discipline + Valdrian ambition)
- Bilingual; switch registers depending on audience
- Military traditions from both (Northern tactics, Valdrian equipment)

### Political Status
- Technically Valdrian but Northern-influenced
- First to experience any war
- Constant low-level tension and raiding
- Potential fifth column for either side

## Silverwood Border Humans

### Location
Frontier settlements along Silver Stream.

### Culture
- **Language**: Archaic human dialect with elven loan words
- **Religion**: Syncretic (Church rituals with elven seasonal timing)
- **Economy**: Waiting; historically traded with elves, now nothing
- **Identity**: "We remember when borders were open"

### Characteristics
- Older residents remember elven trade (pre-Year 1050)
- Younger generations know only the closed border
- Some families claim elven ancestry (not verified)
- Maintain elven-influenced architecture and festivals

### Political Status
- Valdrian territory but economically depressed
- 150 years without trade has impoverished region
- Growing resentment toward Silverwood and Valdrian center
- Potential recruitment ground for anyone promising change

## Broken Lands Marchers

### Location
Frontier communities bordering goblin territories.

### Culture
- **Language**: Human with goblin trade pidgin
- **Religion**: Church (fervent—they see goblins as threat)
- **Economy**: Raiding back, black market trade
- **Identity**: "We know goblins—you don't"

### Characteristics
- Unofficial trade with goblins despite embargo
- Know goblin customs, some speak goblin languages
- First to die in goblin raids; bitter and practical
- Often hired as goblin experts by empires

### Political Status
- Marginal citizens in their own empires
- Suspected of collaboration by authorities
- Actually most knowledgeable about goblin culture
- Potential intermediaries if anyone wanted peace

---

## Dwarven Border Communities

### Location
Human settlements near major mountain holds.

### Culture
- **Language**: Heavy dwarven vocabulary for technical terms
- **Religion**: Church but with dwarven ancestor reverence practices
- **Economy**: Service industry for dwarven trade
- **Identity**: "Humans who work with the mountain folk"

### Characteristics
- Generational relationships with dwarven trading families
- Understand dwarven custom better than most humans
- Intermarriage rare but not unknown (controversial)
- Technical skills influenced by dwarven training

### Political Status
- Valuable as intermediaries in dwarven negotiations
- Suspected of split loyalties by both sides
- Actually loyal to whoever pays best
- First to know dwarven political shifts
