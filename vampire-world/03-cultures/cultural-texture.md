# Cultural Texture

Songs, sayings, customs, holidays, and everyday practices that make the world feel lived-in.

---

## Linguistic Contamination

### Trade Words (Used by All)
- **Krath** (dwarven): Standard precious metal weight
- **Assay** (dwarven): Quality verification
- **Folio** (vampire via elven): Document format
- **Protocol** (vampire: pro-tokol): Formal procedure
- **Archive** (vampire: arkith): Records storage

### Vampire Survivals in Common Speech
Words everyone uses without knowing origin:
- **Covenant** - binding agreement (from vampire political units)
- **Dominion** - controlled territory
- **Ascension** - rising to power
- **Vessel** - container (also consciousness housing)

### Forbidden Words
Known but not spoken:
- Vampire personal names
- Bloodweb technical terms
- Feeding terminology
- Covenant-specific honorifics

Using these marks you as either a scholar or dangerously interested.

### Slurs That Reveal History
- **"Collar-neck"** - implies vampire-era service
- **"Dust-drinker"** - implies consuming vampire remains
- **"Dawn-blind"** - too zealous to see truth
- **"Forge-bought"** - implies dwarven corruption

## Common Sayings

### About the Past

**"Don't dig too deep."**
- Meaning: Leave vampire ruins alone; curiosity is dangerous
- Origin: Mining accidents in collapsed vampire sites
- Usage: Warning against asking too many questions
- Who says it: Everyone

**"Blood remembers."**
- Meaning: The past affects the present; inherited guilt is real
- Origin: Goblin saying about genetic memory
- Usage: Explaining grudges, family feuds, instinctive reactions
- Who says it: Goblins primarily, but it's spread

**"Dust before the dawn."**
- Meaning: Something will be destroyed; inevitable ending
- Origin: Reference to vampires burning in sunlight
- Usage: Fatalistic acceptance of coming failure
- Who says it: Common in Valdrian territories

**"Older than the Scar."**
- Meaning: Very old; ancient; from before everything
- Origin: The Scar is 1,200 years old—older than memory
- Usage: Describing traditions, families, grudges
- Who says it: Everyone

### About Truth and Knowledge

**"Written in vampire."**
- Meaning: Something that's dangerous to understand; forbidden knowledge
- Origin: Vampire script = forbidden texts
- Usage: Warning about information, usually half-joking
- Who says it: Scholars, common people near ruins

**"Three versions and no truth."**
- Meaning: Every story is biased; no one knows what really happened
- Origin: Unknown—probably Dark Ages
- Usage: Cynical commentary on competing narratives
- Who says it: Scholars, cynics

**"The Church has one book."**
- Meaning: Someone is closed-minded, refuses other perspectives
- Origin: Church reliance on single doctrine
- Usage: Insulting someone's thinking
- Who says it: Free Cities intellectuals (quietly elsewhere)

### About Power and Politics

**"Crowns on corpses."**
- Meaning: Power is built on death; rule costs lives
- Origin: Year 1-50, when warlords literally crowned themselves
- Usage: Dark observation about politics
- Who says it: Cynics, reformers

**"Valdrian promises."**
- Meaning: Empty promises; agreements that won't be kept
- Origin: Various treaties Valdris broke
- Usage: Insulting Valdrians
- Who says it: Everyone except Valdrians

**"Dwarven hospitality."**
- Meaning: Grudging tolerance; you're allowed to stay but not welcome
- Origin: Dwarven isolationism
- Usage: Describing someone being coldly polite
- Who says it: Humans, elves

### About Danger

**"Smells like a ruin."**
- Meaning: Situation is dangerous in a vampire-related way
- Origin: Vampire ruins have distinctive smell (old blood, decay)
- Usage: Warning about something feeling wrong
- Who says it: Relic hunters, soldiers near ruins

**"Feeding time."**
- Meaning: You're in trouble; someone's about to take advantage
- Origin: Vampire feeding practices
- Usage: Warning about predatory situations
- Who says it: Common people

**"Dawn's coming."**
- Meaning: The Order of Dawn is nearby; watch what you say
- Origin: Order's reach and willingness to arrest
- Usage: Warning to stop a conversation
- Who says it: Anyone who fears the Order

## Songs and Ballads

### The Collapse Ballad (Traditional)

*Sung in taverns, usually when drunk. Multiple versions.*

**First Verse**
> The towers fell at breaking day
> The blood ran cold, then ran away
> The masters died, the servants wept
> In borrowed beds, we finally slept

**Chorus**
> One thousand years beneath the yoke
> One moment and the binding broke
> We're free, we're free, the sun is high
> But freedom came when millions died

**Third Verse**
> My father served, my mother too
> They wore the chains that vampires drew
> I wear my skin and call it mine
> But in my blood runs older wine

*The song is about liberation, but it's melancholy. Freedom came at terrible cost. People don't sing it happily—they sing it drunk.*

---

### The Cassandra's Song (Valdrian)

*About the Burner. Sung differently depending on politics.*

**Hero Version**
> She burned the rot that fed on us
> She burned the lies they told
> She burned until the land was clean
> And we could finally hold
> Our heads up high, our children safe
> The past was ash and char
> Cassandra held the cleansing flame
> And showed us who we are

**Monster Version**
> She burned the innocent with guilty
> She burned without a trial
> She burned until she couldn't tell
> The virtuous from vile
> She burned her brother screaming
> She burned and called it right
> Cassandra held the cleansing flame
> But couldn't stand the light

*You can tell someone's politics by which version they sing.*

---

### The Digger's Warning (Dwarven, translated)

*Sung by dwarven miners to warn about vampire ruins.*

> When the stone goes smooth, boy, stop your swing
> When the air goes cold, boy, don't touch a thing
> When the lights still glow after all these years
> When the silence sings, that's your warning, hear?
>
> They built down deep, boy, deeper than we go
> They built it strong, boy, stronger than we know
> They built it waiting, patient in the dark
> Leave it where it lies, boy, and walk back through the dark

---

### The Goblin's Lament (Goblin traditional)

*Rarely heard outside Broken Lands. Translated from Goblin.*

> We were people before
> We had names before
> We had children who looked like us before
>
> They took our blood and made it new
> They took our children and made them too
> They took our memory and now we don't know
> What we were before
>
> You call us monsters
> You hunt us down
> You fear the face that they gave us
> But we didn't choose this
> We didn't choose this
> We were people before

*Humans who hear this usually get uncomfortable. That's the point.*

## Holidays and Observances

### Liberation Day (1st of Sowing)

**What It Is**
- Anniversary of the collapse (approximately)
- Day the vampires died
- Beginning of freedom

**How It's Observed**
- Public feasts
- Bonfires (burning the past)
- Telling liberation stories
- Avoiding any vampire mention

**The Problem**
- Also the anniversary of millions of deaths
- Some families mourn instead of celebrate
- Tension between "liberation" and "catastrophe"

**Regional Variations**
- Valdris: Military parades, nationalistic
- Sanctified: Religious services, solemn
- Free Cities: Parties, irreverent
- Northern: Quiet observance
- Goblins: Day of mourning

---

### Day of Remembrance (15th Deepwinter)

**What It Is**
- Remembering those who died in the aftermath
- The 40 million dead
- The purges, famines, wars

**How It's Observed**
- Candles in windows
- No feasting, simple food
- Visiting graves
- Telling family stories

**The Controversy**
- Some want to remember collaborators killed
- Some say collaborators don't deserve remembrance
- Some say the purges saved us
- Some say the purges damned us

**It's a quiet day. And a sad one.**

---

### Saint Mercy's Day (20th Harvest)

**What It Is**
- Feast day of Sister Mercy, Church founder
- Celebration of Divine Light
- Thanksgiving for salvation from vampires

**How It's Observed**
- Church services
- Donations to poor
- White clothing worn
- Meat avoided

**The Tension**
- Mandatory in Sanctified States
- Celebrated in Northern, Valdrian
- Ignored in Free Cities
- Mocked by some scholars

**Who Doesn't Celebrate**
- Goblins (they're the "demons" in Church theology)
- Elves (who don't share human religion)
- Dwarves (who have their own observances)
- Academics (who resent Church)

---

### Forging Day (Dwarven: 5th Midwinter)

**What It Is**
- Commemoration of dwarven liberation
- Founding of Ironpeak Confederation
- Honor to Grimjaw

**How It's Observed**
- Forge fires lit in every hold
- Metalwork gifts exchanged
- Stories of Grimjaw told
- Ale. A lot of ale.

**Human Equivalent**
- Humans not invited
- Some humans celebrate ironically
- Dwarves find this offensive

---

### Long Night (Elven: Midwinter)

**What It Is**
- Elven remembrance of vampire era
- 1,000 years of darkness
- Honoring those who didn't survive

**How It's Observed**
- Unknown—elves don't share
- Presumably solemn
- Silverwood goes darker than usual
- No trade on Long Night

## Social Customs

### Greeting Rituals

**The Valdrian Handshake**
- Open palms shown first
- Then grip
- Origin: showing you hold no weapon
- Shows you're not a threat

**The Northern Nod**
- Brief eye contact, single nod
- No physical contact unless intimate
- Origin: cold climate, layers of clothing
- Don't reach toward a Northerner

**The Dwarven Fist**
- Closed fist to chest
- Origin: showing you're not armed for combat
- Don't bow to a dwarf (it's condescending)

**The Goblin Tooth-Show**
- Brief display of teeth
- NOT a threat—submission gesture
- Shows you're not going to bite
- Humans often misread this badly

### Hospitality Rules

**Universal**
- Share food with guests
- Don't discuss business first night
- Guest doesn't pay for first meal

**Valdrian Addition**
- Wine must be poured by host
- Guest should compliment the vintage
- Bad wine is an insult

**Northern Addition**
- Salt offered first (prevents poisoning)
- Guest should accept all food (refusing is insult)
- Never comment on the cold

**Dwarven Addition**
- Guest given tour of the forge
- Must compliment the metalwork
- Asking prices is rude

### Death Customs

**Human (Common)**
- Burial or cremation depending on region
- Three days of mourning
- Black clothing
- Don't say the name for a month

**Sanctified Addition**
- Light must be present at death (soul guidance)
- Body sprinkled with sacred water
- No burial in shadow

**Dwarven**
- Burial in stone
- Body returned to mountain
- Hammer placed in hands
- Name carved in hall of ancestors

**Elven**
- Unknown—they don't share
- They don't die the same way humans do
- "Fading" not discussed

**Goblin**
- Blood rite (details unknown)
- Something survives in the blood of descendants
- Humans find this disturbing

## Gestures

### Positive

**Open Palm** (universal)
- "I'm not armed"
- "I'm peaceful"
- "I greet you"

**Hand Over Heart** (human)
- "I swear it's true"
- "On my honor"
- Used in oaths

**Closed Fist** (dwarven)
- Respect
- Solidarity
- "You're strong"

### Negative

**Two Fingers Extended** (human)
- Obscene gesture
- Different meanings in different regions
- Generally: "I reject you"

**Showing Neck** (to vampire reference)
- Mockery
- "Are you going to bite me?"
- Very insulting

**Dawn Sign** (religious)
- Crossing gesture
- Warding off evil
- Done when passing vampire ruins, hearing bad news

### Dangerous

**The Old Wave** (vampire greeting gesture)
- Curling fingers downward
- Vampires used this among themselves
- Extremely offensive now
- Doing this can get you killed in Sanctified territories

## Food and Drink

### Common Foods

**Valdrian Staples**
- Wheat bread, wine, beef
- Heavy sauces
- Root vegetables

**Northern Staples**
- Rye bread, ale, game
- Preserved fish
- Cabbage, turnips

**Dwarven Staples**
- Mushrooms, cave-grown grains
- Meat (what kind unclear)
- Strong ale

**Goblin Staples**
- Blood (not always animal)
- Swamp vegetation
- Things humans don't want to know about

### Drinks

**Valdrian Red**
- Wine, obviously
- Status symbol
- Old vintages = wealth

**Northern Black**
- Strong ale, almost opaque
- Served warm
- "It fights back"

**Dwarven Stone-Ale**
- Fermented underground
- Extremely strong
- Humans can rarely handle it

**Free Cities Mix**
- Everything
- Coffee is popular
- Tea from elven trade
- Wine from Valdris
- Spirits from Northern

### Forbidden Foods

**Blood (for humans)**
- Eating blood is taboo
- Association with vampires
- Blood sausage is acceptable (processed)
- Raw blood is suspect

**Vampire-Era Foods**
- Some foods were vampire specialties
- Even if humans can eat them
- Association makes them forbidden
- Mostly forgotten anyway

## Superstitions

### About Vampires

**"They're not all dead."**
- Many believe vampires survive somewhere
- Twilight sanctuaries
- Stasis chambers
- Underground

**Reflection Check**
- Mirrors placed at town entrances
- Vampires supposedly don't reflect
- Actually a myth—vampires had reflections
- But the practice continues

**Salt Lines**
- Salt at thresholds
- Supposedly keeps vampires out
- Actually a myth
- But people feel safer

### About Ruins

**"Don't take anything."**
- Curse belief
- Items from ruins bring bad luck
- Conveniently ignored by relic hunters
- Believed by everyone else

**"They're still watching."**
- Belief that ruins are aware
- Vampire security systems interpreted as spirits
- Some ruins DO have active systems
- So it's not entirely wrong

### About Blood

**"Blood tells."**
- Belief that blood reveals truth
- Ancestry, guilt, nature
- Basis for goblin fear
- Also for family pride

**"Never mix blood."**
- Taboo against blood transfusion
- Vampire association
- Also genuine medical risk (they don't understand blood types)
- People die from this superstition

## Insults

### Universal Insults

**"Collaborator's spawn"**
- Your family served vampires
- The worst insult
- Fighting words

**"Dust-drinker"**
- You're low, scavenger
- You'd drink vampire remains
- Extremely offensive

**"Dawnless"**
- You're evil
- No light in you
- Religious insult

### Regional Insults

**Valdrian on Northern**
- "Frozen-brained"
- "Snow-speaker" (implying they talk to no one)

**Northern on Valdrian**
- "Grape-soft"
- "Promise-breaker"

**Everyone on Goblins**
- Multiple slurs
- Most are about blood
- All are dehumanizing

## How to Use This

### In Dialogue

Characters should:
- Use appropriate sayings
- Make cultural references
- Have regional speech patterns
- Reveal background through idiom

### In Scene-Setting

Describe:
- What people are eating/drinking
- What holidays are approaching
- What gestures people make
- What superstitions they follow

### For Conflict

Cultural differences cause:
- Misunderstandings
- Insults (intentional and unintentional)
- Customs violated
- Trust broken

### For the Protagonist

Vessel Seven knows vampire culture. He doesn't know:
- Human sayings
- Post-collapse holidays
- New taboos
- What gestures mean now

**His learning these things is a plot point.**

## Intermarriage and Mixed Populations

### Human-Elf Relationships
- **Frequency**: Rare but documented throughout history
- **Children**: Possible; inherit mixed traits (longer life, magical sensitivity)
- **Status**: Tolerated in Maritime, scandalous in Sanctified, irrelevant in Silverwood (none leave)
- **Cultural Identity**: Usually identify as human (elven communities won't accept them)

### Human-Dwarf Relationships
- **Frequency**: Uncommon; size differences create challenges
- **Children**: Possible; inherit strength and technical aptitude
- **Status**: Accepted in border communities, surprising elsewhere
- **Cultural Identity**: Often become intermediaries between communities

### Human-Goblin Relationships
- **Frequency**: Extremely rare; extreme taboo
- **Children**: Unknown if possible; no documented cases
- **Status**: Social death in human communities; execution in some
- **Reality**: Probably occurs in border regions but never acknowledged

### Historical Shifts
- **Year 0-300**: Forbidden entirely (post-collapse trauma)
- **Year 300-700**: Began occurring in Maritime (cosmopolitan tolerance)
- **Year 700-1200**: Gradually more accepted in urban areas
- **Present**: Urban tolerance, rural hostility

### Regional Variation
- **Maritime Republic**: Most accepting (profit over purity)
- **Valdrian Empire**: Depends on class (nobility forbids, commoners tolerate)
- **Northern Dominion**: Practical acceptance (anyone who fights is accepted)
- **Sanctified States**: Strict prohibition (religious law)
- **Ironpeak**: Rare (dwarves prefer their own)

*A world isn't real until people have words for things. They need sayings their grandmothers taught them. They need songs they sing when drunk. They need gestures that mean something. They need food that tastes like home and food that tastes like somewhere else. The protagonist enters a world with 1,200 years of these things accumulated—and he knows none of them. He's a stranger in the deepest sense. Learning to understand these things is how he becomes part of the world. Or doesn't.*
