# Elven Historical Archives

What Silverwood preserved, why they won't share it, and how someone might access it.

---

## The Preservation Mandate

When vampires fell, the elves made a choice: preserve everything.

Other races burned vampire records, destroyed artifacts, erased histories. The Church declared vampire knowledge dangerous. Kingdoms competed to eliminate evidence of their collaboration. Within a century, most vampire-era documentation was gone.

The elves kept theirs.

---

## What They Have

### Category 1: Elven Records

Documentation created by elves during vampire rule:
- Population records (all elves for 1,200 years)
- Political correspondence with vampire authorities
- Trade agreements and economic data
- Cultural observations and analyses
- Records of the "optimization" programs

**Location**: Central Archive, Silverwood Council chambers
**Access**: Council members and designated scholars
**Condition**: Perfect—elven preservation magic

### Category 2: Vampire Documents

Materials obtained from vampire sources:
- Official communications with elven leadership
- Technical documents about elven "optimization"
- Treaty texts and legal agreements
- Research reports that mentioned elves
- Maps, surveys, population studies

**Location**: Sealed Archive, beneath the Council tree
**Access**: Three designated elders only
**Condition**: Good—preserved but not magically maintained

### Category 3: Collapse Documentation

Records from Year 0 and the immediate aftermath:
- Communications during the Collapse (what elves witnessed)
- Refugee accounts (elves who escaped vampire territories)
- Council deliberations on response
- Intelligence on what happened to other races
- The decision to seal the borders

**Location**: Restricted Archive, elder access only
**Access**: Current High Council only
**Condition**: Excellent—considered critical records

### Category 4: Silent Years Records

The only complete documentation of Year 423-445:
- What elves observed during that period
- Communications with other powers
- Why they participated in the coordinated erasure
- What they're still hiding

**Location**: Sealed chamber, location known only to three elders
**Access**: Theoretically none—even elders debate whether to open it
**Condition**: Unknown—may be magically sealed beyond preservation

### Category 5: Twilight Sanctuary Records

Information about Sel'Varis sanctuary:
- Design documents (elves provided the location)
- Construction records
- Occupant lists
- Current status reports

**Location**: Highest security
**Access**: High Elder only
**Condition**: Active—updated as recently as Year 1195

## Specific Documents of Interest

### The Optimization Files

Complete records of what vampires did to elven physiology:
- Enhancement techniques
- Dependency creation
- Reversal possibilities

**Why It Matters**: Could explain elven magical abilities and limitations in Year 1200. Could reveal whether elven isolation is biological necessity or political choice.

**Who Wants It**: Everyone. The elves won't share because it would reveal how much they were changed—and how much they can't change back.

### The Collapse Timeline

Hour-by-hour account of what elves observed during the Collapse:
- When they knew something was wrong
- What they saw from Silverwood's borders
- Why they didn't help other races
- What they know about Neth'Korath's final moments

**Why It Matters**: Most detailed surviving account of Day 0. Could resolve questions about what actually happened.

**Who Wants It**: Inheritors (understanding the Collapse), Veil Keepers (exposing truth), scholars everywhere.

### The Silent Years Truth

Why elves participated in erasing Year 423-445:
- What really happened during those years
- What elves did that they're ashamed of
- What they know about the "Second Collapse"
- Why they've maintained the silence for 800 years

**Why It Matters**: The elves are the only race with complete records. They could end the mystery. They choose not to.

**Who Wants It**: Everyone researching the Lost Centuries. The elves have killed people who got too close.

### The Sel'Varis Status Reports

Current condition of the Twilight sanctuary beneath elven mountains:
- Is anyone alive?
- Have they emerged?
- What are the elves doing about it?
- Why won't they tell anyone?

**Why It Matters**: Could be thousands of vampire survivors. Could be alliance opportunity. Could be existential threat.

**Who Wants It**: Inheritors desperately. Northern Kingdom (who found Mor'Thelas) would trade significant concessions. The Church would burn Silverwood to get it.

## Why They Won't Share

### Political Reasons

Sharing records would reveal:
- Elven collaboration with vampire rule (embarrassing)
- Elven "optimization" details (shows they were slaves too)
- Elven actions during Silent Years (possibly criminal)
- Elven knowledge of current vampire survivors (explosive)

Knowledge is power. The elves have more historical knowledge than any other race. Sharing it would reduce their advantage.

### Cultural Reasons

Elven identity is built on superiority:
- "We remember everything" (but won't tell you)
- "We've always been free" (not entirely true)
- "We don't need other races" (isolation is partly forced)

Sharing records would undermine these myths. Elves would have to admit they were optimized servants, not independent allies. Their current abilities came from vampire modification, not inherent superiority.

### Security Reasons

The archives contain:
- Information that could restart wars
- Technology that could be weaponized
- Secrets that would get people killed
- Truths that would destabilize current politics

The elves may genuinely believe some knowledge is too dangerous to share. They may be right.

### They Made Promises

Elves live long enough to honor commitments across centuries. They may have promised:
- Twilight covenant to protect sanctuary information
- Other powers to maintain Silent Years erasure
- Someone to keep specific secrets forever

Breaking promises matters to elves in ways shorter-lived races don't understand.

## How to Access the Archives

### Method 1: Elven Permission (Nearly Impossible)

**Requirements**:
- Approach through official diplomatic channels
- Demonstrate legitimate research purpose
- Offer something elves want in return
- Accept heavy restrictions on use

**Success Rate**: Near zero. No human has received archive access in 400 years. Dwarves occasionally get specific technical documents. No one gets Silent Years material.

**Best Target**: Optimization files—elves might share if you could offer reversal research.

### Method 2: Thandril Connection (Possible)

**Requirements**:
- Convince Thandril that access serves elven interests
- Demonstrate trustworthiness through actions
- Help elves with something they need
- Accept that he'll control what you learn

**Success Rate**: Low but non-zero. Thandril operates outside normal channels. He might facilitate access for the right person with the right request.

**Best Target**: Collapse documentation—could frame as "correcting human misunderstanding."

### Method 3: Outcast Network (Risky)

**Requirements**:
- Contact elven outcasts (those exiled from Silverwood)
- Find one with archive knowledge
- Pay their price (high and possibly immoral)
- Risk elven retaliation

**Success Rate**: Moderate for fragments. Some outcasts took documents when they left. None have complete collections. All are hunted.

**Best Target**: Whatever you can get. Outcasts don't specialize; they grabbed what they could.

### Method 4: Break In (Suicidal)

**Requirements**:
- Enter Silverwood (nearly impossible—sealed borders)
- Reach the archives (multiple security layers)
- Find specific documents (vast collection)
- Escape alive (elven response is lethal)

**Success Rate**: Zero. No one has successfully infiltrated Silverwood's central archives and returned.

**Best Target**: N/A—you won't survive.

### Method 5: Wait for Collapse (Long-term)

**Requirements**:
- Something forces elves to open archives
- War, crisis, or internal change
- Elves need outside help badly enough to trade secrets

**Success Rate**: Unknown. Elves have maintained isolation for 1,150 years. But they're fortifying for something. When that something arrives, they may need allies.

**Best Target**: Everything—if elves break isolation, they might share comprehensively.

## Seven's Options

### Direct Request

If Seven identifies as a Vessel, elves might be interested:
- He's proof the Collapse could have been survived
- His existence affects their Sel'Varis calculations
- They might trade information for information

**Risk**: They might capture him for study instead.

### Offer Value

Seven has knowledge elves don't:
- First-hand memories of vampire civilization
- Technical knowledge implanted during creation
- Connection to Vel'Thoras and its secrets

He could trade this for archive access.

**Risk**: He might reveal more than he learns.

### Use Thandril

Thandril is the most accessible elf. If Seven can demonstrate he's useful to elven interests, Thandril might advocate for limited access.

**Risk**: Thandril has his own agenda. "Limited access" might mean exactly what elves want Seven to know—no more.

### Find Outcasts

Elven outcasts might be sympathetic to Seven:
- They know what it's like to be rejected by your people
- They might have useful fragments
- They're less likely to capture him

**Risk**: Outcasts are outcasts for reasons. Some are criminals, traitors, or worse.

## What Access Would Reveal

### About the Collapse

- Complete timeline of Day 0
- What elves saw from outside the disaster zone
- What they know about why it happened
- What they chose not to share with other races

### About Seven

- Elven observations of the Sanguine Vessels Initiative
- Whether elves knew about the project before the Collapse
- Any elven involvement in the research
- What optimization techniques might apply to him

### About Year 1200

- Current status of Sel'Varis sanctuary
- What elves are preparing for
- What they fear is coming
- What they know about The Scar's growth

### About Everything Else

The elves have 1,200 years of documentation that no one else has. Access to their archives would rewrite the history everyone thinks they know.

That's why they'll never share it voluntarily.
