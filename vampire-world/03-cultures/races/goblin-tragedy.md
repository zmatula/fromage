# The Goblin Tragedy

*What "suffering worst" actually meant*
*Scenes from the vampire era and its aftermath*

---

## Purpose

The goblin experience during vampire rule wasn't a footnote. It was systematic dehumanization, cultural erasure, and the transformation of a people into resources. These scenes show what that meant for individuals who lived through it.

---

## Scene 1: The Breeding Facility

**Year -500 | Facility 12, Eastern Province**
**POV: Kaela, Female Goblin, Breeding Stock**

---

They came for her children at birth.

Kaela had learned not to look. The first time—her first child, fifteen years ago—she'd fought. She'd bitten the handler's arm to the bone. They'd beaten her until she couldn't stand, and they'd taken the baby anyway.

Now she didn't fight. She didn't look. She heard the infant's first cry, felt the afterbirth, and then the handler took it and that was the end.

"Female," the handler noted. "Good coloration. Tag and transfer to Growth Sector 7."

Kaela stared at the ceiling. Gray stone, same as always. Same as for the past thirty years in this facility.

The supervisor entered. Kaela knew him—Overseer Valdrin, vampire, three hundred years old. He inspected the records tablet the handler passed him.

"Kaela 12-7," he read. "Seventeenth pregnancy. Fourteen surviving offspring." He looked at her. "You've been productive."

She didn't answer. There was no answer to give.

"Your genetic markers are exceptional. Strong shamanic affinity, high adaptability, superior regeneration." He made a note. "We're moving you to the premium program. Better conditions. Better diet. You'll be bred with selected males only."

*Better conditions.* She almost laughed. Better conditions meant a larger cell. Better diet meant enough food that her body could handle more pregnancies. Selected males meant—

"Optimal genetic combinations," Valdrin continued. "Your offspring will be assigned to enhanced roles. Research subjects. Dimensional tests. The important work."

*Important work.* She knew what that meant. She'd seen the goblins who came back from "important work," the ones who came back at all. Missing limbs. Missing minds. Missing parts of themselves that couldn't be named.

Her children would be cut open and studied. Dissected while alive to see how shamanic magic flowed. And she would keep producing more of them, because her "genetic markers" were exceptional.

"Do you understand?" Valdrin asked.

"Yes, Overseer."

"Good. Transfer tomorrow."

He left. The handler finished the records and followed.

Kaela lay on the birthing table and thought about her mother.

Her mother had told her stories. Before the vampires, before the facilities, when goblins were free. The Great Swamp, where tribes lived by their own laws. Shaman-Kings who spoke to spirits. Children who grew up knowing their parents, knowing their names, knowing they belonged to something.

Kaela had never seen the Great Swamp. She'd been born in Facility 8, transferred to Facility 12 when she reached breeding age. She would die in a facility. Her children would die in laboratories.

Her grandchildren wouldn't even have stories. They'd be third generation—bred for specific traits, raised for specific purposes, knowing nothing of what goblins had been. The erasure would be complete.

She thought about killing herself. She thought about it often. But she always stopped at the same place: if she died, they'd just use another breeder. The system didn't need her specifically. It just needed bodies.

*We were people*, she thought. *We were free people.*

She couldn't remember what that felt like.

---

**Historical Note**: Goblin breeding programs operated for nearly a thousand years. An estimated 200,000 goblins passed through them. Survival rates for offspring assigned to research were approximately 15%. The programs were considered efficient and cost-effective by vampire administrators. Kaela died in Year -485 during her twenty-third pregnancy. She never learned any of her children's names.

## Scene 2: The Experiment

**Year -250 | Research Complex, Neth'Korath**
**POV: Thren, Male Goblin, Experimental Subject**

---

They told him it would help his people.

"Your shamanic abilities are remarkable," the researcher had said. "If we understand how they work, we can develop techniques that will benefit goblins for generations. You'll be a pioneer."

Thren was young—barely twenty—and he wanted to believe.

They strapped him to the table. The restraints were soft, almost comfortable. The room was clean and bright. Nothing like the horror stories he'd heard.

"We're going to open your chest cavity," the researcher explained. "The spirit-channels run through your torso. We need to observe them directly while you channel energy. You'll be awake throughout—we need you to actively use your abilities."

"Will it hurt?"

"Some discomfort. We'll manage it."

They opened him up.

Thren learned what "some discomfort" meant in vampire terms. It meant he could feel everything—the cutting, the spreading of his ribs, the cold air on his exposed organs—but the pain was muted somehow, pushed to a distance where he could observe it without being consumed by it.

"Now," the researcher said, "call on the spirits."

Thren tried. He reached for the connection he'd felt since childhood—the link to something ancient, something that lived in the blood and the earth and the darkness. The spirits of his ancestors. The powers that had protected goblins for millennia.

Nothing answered.

"Again," the researcher said. "Concentrate."

He concentrated. He poured everything he had into the calling, every technique his mother had taught him, every secret he'd learned.

Silence.

The researcher frowned. "The spirit-channels are active. I can see the energy moving. But nothing's responding." He made a note. "Interesting. The subjective experience of spiritual contact doesn't correlate with actual dimensional interface. Try again."

Thren tried. For hours. His chest open, his organs visible, his soul crying out for something to answer. Nothing came.

"Fascinating," the researcher said eventually. "The shamanic 'connection' appears to be self-generated hallucination. The goblin believes spirits are responding, but objectively, there's no external contact. The power is internal, interpreted through a spiritual framework that doesn't reflect reality."

*No.* Thren wanted to scream it. *The spirits are real. They're just not—they can't—*

He understood. The spirits wouldn't come here. Into this place, this room, this situation. They wouldn't be observed and measured and studied. They had abandoned him to the vampires because that was all they could do.

*Or they were never real at all.*

"Again," the researcher said. "We need more data."

---

They ran the experiment eight times. On the eighth, Thren's heart gave out.

His last thought was not about spirits or ancestors or his people. It was simple and clear: *I believed them. I believed it would help.*

---

**Historical Note**: Vampire researchers never definitively determined the nature of goblin shamanic abilities. The experiments suggested internal psychic power interpreted through cultural framework, but some results remained unexplained. Approximately 40,000 goblins were used as experimental subjects during the vampire era. Thren's contribution to the research was noted in a summary report as "Subject 227, inconclusive results." His name was not recorded.

## Scene 3: Cultural Erasure

**Year -300 | Hidden Grotto, Southern Province**
**POV: Elder Shaman Vrix, Last Keeper of the Old Songs**

---

She taught in darkness because light meant death.

"Again," Vrix whispered. "The Calling of Blood."

Her apprentice—Skeela, fourteen years old, stolen from a breeding facility three years ago—repeated the words. She got most of them right. Most wasn't enough.

"The third passage. 'Blood calls to blood across the void.' Not 'blood speaks to blood.' Calls. The distinction matters."

"I'm sorry, Elder."

"Don't apologize. Learn." Vrix had been teaching for forty years, ever since she'd escaped her own facility. She'd had a hundred apprentices. Most were dead. "The songs are the memory of our people. If you don't learn them exactly, they change. They corrupt. A hundred years of small errors and they mean nothing."

"Why do they matter?"

Vrix looked at the girl. Fourteen years old, born in captivity, knowing nothing of what goblins had been. How could she explain?

"Before the vampires," Vrix said, "the songs were our history. Our law. Our connection to the spirits and the ancestors. When a shaman sang the Calling of Blood, she could find any goblin anywhere in the world. When she sang the War-Chant of Grist, she carried his rage into battle. The songs were power."

"But they don't work anymore."

"They work. I've felt them work. But we're so few now, and we've lost so much..." Vrix shook her head. "The songs work. We just need goblins strong enough to sing them."

A sound outside. Footsteps.

"Hide," Vrix commanded.

Skeela went to the back of the grotto, to the crevice that would hide her if she stayed silent. Vrix stayed where she was.

The patrol entered. Three vampire soldiers, routine sweep of the area.

"Goblin," the leader said. "Alone?"

"Yes, lord."

"What are you doing here?"

"Resting. I'm old. The walk from the work camp tired me."

The soldier studied her. Vrix kept her face empty. An old goblin taking a rest—nothing suspicious. Nothing worth reporting.

"Get back to your camp," the soldier said.

"Yes, lord."

They left. Vrix waited until their sounds faded, then whistled for Skeela.

The girl emerged. Her face was white.

"If they'd found me—"

"They didn't." Vrix took her hands. "This is why the songs matter. This is why we hide, why we risk, why we teach in darkness. Because if we don't keep the old ways, they disappear. And if they disappear, we're not goblins anymore. We're just... things the vampires made."

Skeela nodded slowly.

"Now. Again. The Calling of Blood."

The girl began to sing.

---

They found them three months later. The patrol had reported the old goblin after all, and Security had been watching.

Vrix died in the interrogation. She wouldn't name other teachers, other students, other hidden places where the old ways survived.

Skeela was returned to a breeding facility. She tried to teach the songs to other girls, but she found she couldn't remember them clearly. The third passage—was it "calls" or "speaks"? She wasn't sure anymore.

The Calling of Blood survived in fragments. The War-Chant of Grist was lost entirely. A hundred years of small errors, and the songs meant nothing.

Just as Vrix had feared.

---

**Historical Note**: By Year 0, an estimated 90% of goblin traditional knowledge had been lost. The songs, the rituals, the names of spirits and ancestors—erased through active suppression and passive decay. What survived did so in fragments, passed down by individuals like Vrix who risked death to remember. When goblins emerged from the Collapse, they found they didn't know who they had been. They had to rebuild their identity from scraps.

## Scene 4: The Rage Preserved

**Year 50 | Broken Lands, Hidden Camp**
**POV: Shaman Kreth, Teacher of Blood Magic**

---

"Why do we use their magic?"

The question always came. Kreth had answered it a hundred times.

She looked at her student—Varak, sixteen, born after the Collapse into a world of chaos and freedom. He'd never known vampire rule. He'd never seen a facility or a laboratory or a patrol. He didn't understand.

"Their magic is in our blood," Kreth said. "The vampires put it there. A thousand years of experiments and breeding—they wanted to understand our power, so they changed us. Made us carry techniques we were never meant to have."

"Then we should reject it. It's their corruption."

"We should *use* it." Kreth's voice sharpened. "They gave us weapons. They meant them to serve vampire purposes. Now we turn them around."

"Against who? The vampires are dead."

"Are they?"

Varak hesitated.

"The vampires are dead," Kreth said. "But the humans who hunted us aren't. The elves who refused to meet our envoys aren't. The dwarves who sealed their gates and left us to be conquered aren't." She stepped closer. "They watched us suffer. Some of them profited from it. They called us monsters and felt righteous while we were bred and butchered. And now they control the world again."

"So we take revenge?"

"We *survive*. The humans outnumber us a hundred to one. The elves have their forests and their magic. The dwarves have their holds and their weapons. All we have is what the vampires left us—blood magic, dimensional techniques, and rage."

Varak looked uncertain. Kreth understood. He was young. He wanted to believe the world could be fair.

"There's a story," Kreth said. "From before the vampires. The Great Gathering."

"I've heard it."

"Tell me."

Varak recited it—the envoys, the refusals, Vren Blood-Eye's head sent back to his father. The Oath.

"That was 1,100 years ago," Kreth said. "How long do you think it takes for humans to change? For elves to see us as people? For dwarves to open their gates?"

"I don't know."

"Neither do I. But I know this: while we're waiting to find out, we need to be strong. We need power. We need magic that can hurt them if they come for us again." She held up her hand, let blood-energy crackle across her fingers. "This is what we have. This is what we use. And if someday we don't need it—if someday there's real peace—then we can let it go. Until then, we practice. We train. We remember."

"Remember what?"

"Remember that they answered Vren with murder. Remember that no one helped us. Remember that when goblins are weak, goblins die."

Varak looked at her hand, at the blood magic, at the power the vampires had given them.

"Teach me," he said.

Kreth smiled. This was how the rage survived. This was how a people who'd been broken learned to stand again. Not with forgiveness. Not with reconciliation.

With power. With memory.

With the Oath.

---

**Historical Note**: Blood magic became central to goblin culture after the Collapse. What had been forced upon them by vampire experiments was reclaimed as heritage—power that was theirs now, to use as they chose. By Year 1200, every goblin shaman learned blood techniques. Most saw it as practical necessity. Some saw it as sacred rage. All saw it as goblin.

## The Thread to Year 1200

### Gritha Bloodsinger

A young goblin shaman sits in the Broken Lands and listens to the old stories.

She hears about the Great Gathering. About Vren Blood-Eye. About the Oath.

She hears about the breeding facilities and the experiments and the cultural erasure. About a people who were things before they were free.

She hears about the humans and elves and dwarves who watched, who refused to help, who called goblins monsters while profiting from their slavery.

She practices blood magic. She feels power in her veins—power that vampires gave her people, that goblins claimed as their own.

She looks at the world in Year 1200 and sees humans still hunting goblins, still calling them vermin, still controlling everything while goblins scrape survival in the wastelands.

She remembers the Oath.

*We will never ask again. We will never forget.*

She hasn't forgotten.
