# Artifact Power Sources

Which artifacts still work, which don't, and why.

---

## The Power Problem

Most vampire artifacts were designed to draw power from the Bloodweb. When the Bloodweb collapsed, these artifacts stopped working—or degraded to minimal function.

But some artifacts still work in Year 1200. Understanding why tells us what they can do, what they're worth, and what dangers they pose.

---

## Power Source Categories

### Category 1: Bloodweb-Dependent (Non-Functional)

**How they worked**: Drew power through Bloodweb connection
**Status in Year 1200**: Dead or minimal residual function
**Examples**: Most communication devices, transportation portals, city infrastructure

These artifacts are the majority. They're studied for design insights but can't be used. Some hold residual charges that discharge unpredictably—touching one might produce a single effect before it dies completely.

**Value**: Academic curiosity; occasional one-time use

### Category 2: Self-Powered (Functional)

**How they worked**: Internal power generation independent of Bloodweb
**Status in Year 1200**: Fully functional or degrading slowly
**Examples**: Mind Codex, certain weapons, some detection devices

These were rare even in vampire times—expensive and difficult to create. They were made for situations where Bloodweb access couldn't be guaranteed (exploration, military operations, research contingencies).

**Value**: Extremely high; functional artifacts are priceless

### Category 3: Ambient Collectors (Degraded)

**How they worked**: Drew ambient magical energy directly from environment
**Status in Year 1200**: Function at reduced capacity; slowly depleting
**Examples**: Crown of Seeing, some healing devices, wards

These can function without Bloodweb but need magical energy that's harder to collect post-collapse. They work, but less effectively than designed. Many are running down and will fail within decades or centuries.

**Value**: High; degraded function is better than none

### Category 4: Blood-Powered (Conditional)

**How they worked**: Draw power from vampire blood directly
**Status in Year 1200**: Non-functional without vampire user; devastating with one
**Examples**: Certain weapons, control devices, the Forge of Souls

These require vampire blood to activate. In Year 1200, they're inert—no vampires, no function. But if Vessel Seven touches one...

**Value**: Unknown until vampire activates; potentially extreme

## The Five Great Artifacts

### Crown of Seeing

**Power Source**: Ambient collector
**Function**: Enhanced perception; see distant places, hidden things, possible futures
**Status in Year 1200**: Functional at ~30% original capacity

The Crown still works, but its range is limited and visions are unreliable. The Veil Keepers have learned to use it carefully—demanding too much risks burnout.

**Why it still works**: Designed for use by seers operating alone, outside cities. Never needed Bloodweb.

### Forge of Souls

**Power Source**: Blood-powered
**Function**: Create artifacts; bind souls to objects; modify living beings
**Status in Year 1200**: Dormant

The Forge requires vampire blood to activate. The dwarves sealed it because they don't want anyone activating it—ever. If Vessel Seven or another vampire reached it, it would work exactly as designed.

**Why it's sealed**: Too dangerous to use; too valuable to destroy

### Mind Codex

**Power Source**: Self-powered
**Function**: Contains Mordecai's consciousness and knowledge
**Status in Year 1200**: Fully functional

The Codex was Mordecai's backup—created in case the collapse happened. He made it completely self-sustaining. It's been waiting 1,200 years for someone to access it.

**The danger**: Mordecai encoded his consciousness, not just his knowledge. Accessing the Codex might mean interacting with him—or being influenced by him.

### Blood Archives

**Power Source**: Self-powered (fragmented)
**Function**: Historical records; memories of ancient vampires
**Status in Year 1200**: Partially functional; incomplete

The Archives were split during the collapse. Different fragments work better than others. The Inheritors have portions that still play memories; other fragments are completely dead.

**Why they're fragmented**: Deliberately separated to prevent anyone having complete record

### Living Artifact

**Power Source**: Unknown—possibly self-generating
**Function**: Unknown
**Status in Year 1200**: Active

The Living Artifact is... alive. It's described as a thinking, active relic. It may not need power in the conventional sense—it may generate its own.

**The mystery**: This might be Vessel Eleven. If so, "it" is a person.

## Common Artifact Types

### Communication Devices

**Original function**: Telepathic links, message sending, remote viewing
**Power source**: Bloodweb-dependent
**Status**: Non-functional

Without the Bloodweb to carry signals, these are dead. Some can store messages that were being transmitted when the collapse happened—frozen in mid-send for 1,200 years.

### Weapons

**Original function**: Enhanced combat, magical attacks
**Power sources**: Various (self-powered, blood-powered, ambient)
**Status**: Varies

Combat weapons were often designed for independent operation. Many still work:
- Self-powered weapons function normally
- Ambient collectors work at reduced effect
- Blood-powered weapons are dormant until a vampire touches them

### Medical Devices

**Original function**: Healing, life extension, modification
**Power sources**: Bloodweb-dependent or ambient
**Status**: Mostly non-functional

These were usually Bloodweb-connected for precision work. Some ambient-powered healing devices still work but are unreliable—you might heal, or you might be transformed in unexpected ways.

### Wards and Protections

**Original function**: Barriers, alarms, defenses
**Power sources**: Ambient collectors (usually)
**Status**: Degraded but functional

Most ruins have partially-functional wards. They work well enough to be dangerous but not well enough to be reliable. The Year 720 Vel'Thoras expedition was killed by wards that activated inconsistently—sometimes they passed, sometimes they died.

### Transportation Devices

**Original function**: Teleportation, portals, fast travel
**Power sources**: Bloodweb-dependent
**Status**: Non-functional

Portals required enormous power that only the Bloodweb could provide. No portable power source can activate them. The few that still have residual charge are extremely dangerous—they might send you somewhere, but probably not where you intended.

## Artifact Degradation

### The Timeline

**Year 0-100**: Artifacts with residual charges still function intermittently
**Year 100-500**: Ambient collectors begin significant degradation
**Year 500-1000**: Most artifacts reach minimal function
**Year 1000-1200**: Final degradation; many die completely
**Year 1200+**: Only self-powered and dormant blood-powered artifacts remain viable

### Why It Matters

Artifacts found and used in Year 300 would have been more functional than the same artifacts in Year 1200. This affects:
- Historical accounts of artifact use
- Why certain artifacts are "more powerful" in old records
- Expectations vs. reality when finding artifacts

## Reactivation Possibilities

### If Seven Handles Blood-Powered Artifacts

Blood-powered artifacts will recognize vampire blood and activate. Seven could potentially:
- Use weapons designed for vampire warriors
- Activate devices that have been dormant for 1,200 years
- Access the Forge of Souls (if he reached it)
- Power systems designed for vampire operators

The Inheritors want Seven partly for this reason—he's a key to artifacts no one else can use.

### If Someone Rebuilds Bloodweb Components

Theoretically possible to create small-scale Bloodweb nodes:
- Dwarves have technical fragments
- Inheritors have theoretical knowledge
- Some sanctuaries might have functional examples

A rebuilt node could power Bloodweb-dependent artifacts locally. This would be enormously valuable—and dangerous if it recreated the dependency.

### If The Scar Opens Further

The dimensional breach at The Scar is related to the power that fed the Bloodweb. If the breach expands, ambient magical energy might increase. This could:
- Enhance remaining ambient collectors
- Reactivate some Bloodweb-dependent artifacts
- Create new dangers from powered-up relics

## Strategic Implications

### Who Wants What

**Inheritors**: Functional artifacts for study; blood-powered artifacts to use with Seven
**Order of Dawn**: All artifacts destroyed
**Veil Keepers**: Artifacts that reveal truth (Crown, Archives)
**Valdrian Empire**: Military artifacts for army
**Northern Dominion**: Any artifacts that shift power balance
**Dwarves**: Keep Forge sealed forever

### The Power Balance

Functional artifacts are so rare that possessing one shifts political power. The Crown of Seeing makes Veil Keepers influential despite small numbers. If the Empire found a cache of working weapons, it could change continental politics.

This is why artifact hunting is so intense—and so deadly.
