# Narrative Voice

How to write POV characters and maintain distinct voices.

---

## Core Approach

### Third Person Limited (Deep POV)

**Perspective**: Inside character's head, past tense
**Access**: Thoughts, feelings, perceptions—but only theirs
**Voice**: Adapts to POV character
**Reliability**: Character's biases color everything

---

## POV Mechanics

### What Character Perceives

**Include**:
- What they notice (selective attention)
- How they interpret it (personal filter)
- Physical sensations
- Emotional responses

**Exclude**:
- Things they don't see/hear
- What others are thinking
- Objective truth
- Information they don't have

### Example

**Wrong** (camera eye):
> The room was beautifully decorated with expensive tapestries.

**Right** (through protagonist):
> The tapestries on the walls looked expensive. Everything in this place looked expensive. He didn't recognize any of the patterns—1,200 years had erased whatever these images meant.

## Character-Specific Voices

### Protagonist (Vessel Seven)

**Background**: Vampire creation, 1,200 years isolated
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Formal, slightly archaic phrasing
- Clinical observation (trained)
- Emotional distance (conditioning)
- Growing confusion/wonder at new world
- Moments of unexpected feeling

**Vocabulary**:
- Precise technical terms
- Vampire-era references
- Gradually learns modern expressions
- Struggles with idioms

**Internal Focus**:
- Questions about identity
- Comparing past to present
- Evaluating threats
- Seeking connection

**Example**:
> They called this a city. The structures were adequate—stone and timber, functional enough—but where was the infrastructure? The energy conduits? The blood channels? This was not a city. This was an encampment that had forgotten to move.

---

### Elara Goldstone

**Background**: Valdrian noblewoman, political operative
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Calculating, strategic
- Reads people and situations
- Elegant but cold
- Justification of own actions

**Vocabulary**:
- Political terms
- Court language
- Economic concepts
- Euphemisms for ugly realities

**Internal Focus**:
- Political calculations
- Weighing costs/benefits
- Rationalizing means
- Tracking relationships

**Example**:
> Duke Aldric smiled at her across the ballroom, but his eyes were counting her allies. She smiled back, letting him count. Let him think he knew her numbers. The ones who mattered wouldn't be visible—that was rather the point.

---

### Marcus Ashford

**Background**: Order of Dawn commander, devout hunter
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Certain, righteous
- Military precision
- Religious conviction
- Growing doubt (later)

**Vocabulary**:
- Military/tactical terms
- Religious language
- Absolutes (good/evil, pure/corrupt)
- Fewer absolutes as doubt grows

**Internal Focus**:
- Mission focus
- Religious framework
- Judgment of others
- Identity questioning (later)

**Example**:
> The vampire-thing looked up at him, and for a moment Marcus saw fear in its eyes. Good. It should fear. The Light's judgment had found it after twelve hundred years, and the Light was not known for mercy. Neither was Marcus.

---

### Vera Nighthollow

**Background**: Inheritor scholar, researcher
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Academic, analytical
- Detached observation
- Classification impulse
- Gradually more personal

**Vocabulary**:
- Scientific terms
- Research methodology
- Categorization language
- Struggling with emotional terms

**Internal Focus**:
- Information assessment
- Classification of subjects
- Research implications
- Ethical questions (growing)

**Example**:
> Subject Seven presented fascinating contradictions. His physiology suggested enhanced regenerative capacity, yet his psychological profile indicated standard trauma responses. He was not merely vampire—he was something in between. She needed to understand what that meant. She needed him to let her understand.

---

### Kira Frost

**Background**: Military intelligence, double agent
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Compartmentalized thinking
- Constant threat assessment
- Emotional suppression
- Exhaustion underneath

**Vocabulary**:
- Intelligence jargon
- Military terms
- Careful, guarded language
- Different vocabularies for different contexts

**Internal Focus**:
- Balancing loyalties
- Operational security
- Stress management
- Longing for simplicity

**Example**:
> The Veil Keeper's instructions contradicted the King's orders. Again. Kira filed both in their appropriate mental compartments and considered her options. Option one: obey the King, betray the Keepers. Option two: obey the Keepers, betray the King. Option three: find a way to satisfy both. There was never an option four.

---

### Gritha Bloodsinger

**Background**: Goblin elder shaman, keeper of rage
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Ancient perspective
- Bitter, fierce
- Poetic in anger
- Loving toward her people

**Vocabulary**:
- Goblin cultural terms
- Blood magic concepts
- Ancestral references
- Contempt for humans

**Internal Focus**:
- Her people's suffering
- Historical grievances
- Plans for revenge
- Love for young goblins

**Example**:
> The humans called her kind savages. This from a people who had murdered goblin children in their beds, burned goblin libraries, and called it justice. Let them call her savage. She would show them what savagery really meant. She would show them with their own children's blood.

---

### Thandril Shadowleaf

**Background**: Elven agent, long-term placement
**Voice Characteristics**:
- Patient, observational
- Detached from events
- Uncertain about purpose
- Growing investment

**Vocabulary**:
- Elven terms (occasionally)
- Formal, slightly alien syntax
- Observational language
- Fewer contractions

**Internal Focus**:
- Following orders
- Long-term thinking
- Cultural observations
- Questions about mission

**Example**:
> One hundred and fifty years among the quicklings had taught Thandril patience. They lived, they died, their kingdoms rose and fell—all in the time it took an elf to master a single discipline. And yet. This one, this vampire remnant—he was not quickling. He was something else. And for the first time in a century and a half, Thandril's orders did not tell him what to do with something else.

## Voice Transitions

### How Voice Changes

**Within Scene**:
- Emotion intensifies → voice shifts
- Stress changes word choice
- Physical state affects perception
- Focus narrows or widens

**Across Arc**:
- Character grows → voice evolves
- New knowledge changes references
- Emotional development shows
- Vocabulary shifts

### Example: Protagonist Evolution

**Early**:
> They did not behave as servants should. They looked at him with fear, yes, but also hatred. This was not right. Servants were grateful for their roles.

**Late**:
> They looked at him with fear. He understood now—their ancestors had looked at his creators the same way. And their ancestors had been right to be afraid.

## Technical Guidelines

### Tense
- Past tense throughout
- Present tense only in dialogue
- Consistency critical

### Person
- Third person limited
- "He/she" not "I"
- But deeply inside their head

### Internal Monologue
- Integrated into narration
- No italics for common thoughts
- Italics for emphasized thoughts
- Free indirect discourse

### Example:
> Marcus watched the creature flee into the darkness. He should pursue. That was his duty. But his legs didn't move. Why didn't his legs move? *Because you're afraid*, a voice whispered. *Afraid he might not deserve to die.*

## Common Errors

### ❌ Head-Hopping
- Stay in one POV per scene
- No switching mid-paragraph
- No knowing others' thoughts

### ❌ Camera Eye
- No neutral descriptions
- Everything filtered through POV
- Character's interpretation always present

### ❌ Author Intrusion
- Don't tell reader what to think
- Don't editorialize
- Let character's bias show character

### ❌ Inconsistent Voice
- Vocabulary must match character
- Education level must be consistent
- Cultural background must show
