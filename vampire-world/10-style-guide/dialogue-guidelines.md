# Dialogue Guidelines

How characters speak and what their dialogue reveals.

---

## Core Principle

Dialogue is not just what characters say—it's what they're trying to do. Every line of dialogue is an action.

---

## Dialogue Actions

### What Characters Do With Words

**Persuade**: Convince someone of something
**Deceive**: Make someone believe a lie
**Evade**: Avoid giving information
**Threaten**: Warn of consequences
**Seduce**: Create attraction/allegiance
**Interrogate**: Extract information
**Command**: Compel obedience
**Negotiate**: Find mutual benefit
**Provoke**: Cause emotional reaction

### Identifying the Action

Before writing dialogue, know:
- What does this character want?
- How are they trying to get it?
- What are they hiding?
- What do they want others to think?

---

## Subtext

### The Unspoken Layer

**Subtext**: What the dialogue really means beneath the surface words

**Example**:
> "I see you've made new friends," Elara said.
>
> *Translation: I know you've betrayed me and I'm deciding what to do about it.*

### Creating Subtext

- Characters don't say what they mean
- Power dynamics influence word choice
- Past history affects present meaning
- Context changes interpretation

### When to Use Direct Speech

- High emotion (breaks through)
- Major confrontation
- Final showdown
- After long buildup of subtext

## Character-Specific Speech

### Protagonist (Vessel Seven)

**Patterns**:
- Formal, slightly archaic
- Precise technical language
- Questions about things others take for granted
- Occasionally slips into vampire tongue under stress

**Example**:
> "Your 'king' rules by... consent? He was not bred for dominion?"

**Avoids**: Contractions (early), idioms, casual speech

---

### Elara Goldstone

**Patterns**:
- Politically careful
- Euphemisms
- Never commits directly
- Implications over statements

**Example**:
> "It would be unfortunate if Lord Vasken's concerns were to reach the wrong ears."

**Avoids**: Direct threats, emotional language, simple declaratives

---

### Marcus Ashford

**Patterns**:
- Military brevity
- Religious references
- Absolute language (early)
- More questions (later)

**Example**:
> "The Light demands justice. You obstruct the Light. The equation is simple."

**Avoids**: Doubt (early), nuance, lengthy explanation

---

### Vera Nighthollow

**Patterns**:
- Academic/clinical
- Classification language
- Questions rather than statements
- Thinking out loud

**Example**:
> "Fascinating. You exhibit emotional responses despite reported memory modification. Unless the modification was incomplete? Or did you develop these post-creation?"

**Avoids**: Emotional language, relationship terms, imprecision

---

### Kira Frost

**Patterns**:
- Guarded, careful
- Double meanings
- Different registers for different audiences
- Exhaustion sometimes shows

**Example**:
> "The situation is... complex. I'm assessing options."
>
> *Translation: I have no idea what to do and I'm terrified.*

**Avoids**: Commitment, singular loyalty, complete honesty

---

### Gritha Bloodsinger

**Patterns**:
- Poetic anger
- Ancestral references
- Bitter humor
- Raw emotion

**Example**:
> "You speak to me of law? Your grandfathers made laws against our breathing. Let us discuss law when the bones of my people are returned to our earth."

**Avoids**: Diplomacy, human conventions, restraint

---

### Thandril Shadowleaf

**Patterns**:
- Formal, few contractions
- Long view references
- Observer's detachment
- Occasional alien perspective

**Example**:
> "I have watched seven generations of your family. You are... different from your predecessors. This is neither compliment nor criticism. Merely observation."

**Avoids**: Human idioms, urgency, emotional commitment

## Dialogue Mechanics

### Attribution

**Simple is best**:
- "said" is invisible
- "asked" for questions
- Avoid: hissed, growled, snarled, etc.
- Action beats instead: He stood. "Get out."

**Beat Attribution**:
> Marcus set down his sword. "I'm not here to kill you."

### Interruption

**Show with em-dash**:
> "But I thought—"
> "You thought wrong."

**Show with incomplete thought**:
> "If you would just..." She stopped. What was the point?

### Trailing Off

**Ellipsis for trailing off**:
> "I used to believe..." He shook his head.

### Silence

**Sometimes the best dialogue is none**:
> "Will you help us?"
>
> The protagonist looked at her for a long moment. Then he turned and walked away.

## Power Dynamics

### Every Conversation Has Power

**Who has it**:
- Status (noble vs commoner)
- Information (who knows what)
- Need (who needs more from this)
- Position (host vs guest)

**How it shows**:
- Who speaks first
- Who asks questions
- Who sets tone
- Who gives ground

### Power Shifts

Watch power shift during conversation:
- New information changes balance
- Threat revealed
- Vulnerability shown
- Alliance formed

---

## Exposition in Dialogue

### The "As You Know, Bob" Problem

**Wrong**:
> "As you know, the vampires collapsed 1,200 years ago when the Ascension Experiment destroyed the Bloodweb."

**Right**:
> "Twelve hundred years," the scholar muttered. "And we still don't understand what they did. Maybe we're not meant to."

### How to Include Information

- Characters disagree about facts
- New character learns something
- Someone explains to someone who genuinely doesn't know
- Information serves character purpose

## Dialogue in Conflict

### Verbal Combat

**Escalation**: Each exchange raises stakes
**Weapons**: Information, threats, emotional hits
**Victory**: Someone gets what they want
**Cost**: Something is lost

### Example Exchange

> "I know what you did in Thornhaven," Elara said.
> "Do you." Vasken didn't flinch.
> "The bodies. The fire. All of it."
> "Then you know what I'm capable of."
> "I know what you're afraid of. Those aren't the same."

---

## Group Conversations

### Managing Multiple Speakers

- Limit to 3-4 distinct speakers
- Give each clear purpose
- Don't rotate mechanically
- Some characters stay quiet

### Creating Dynamics

- Alliances show
- Tensions show
- Side conversations
- Interruptions reveal relationships

---

## Common Errors

### ❌ Everyone Sounds the Same
- Each character needs distinct voice
- Education, background, culture matter
- Speech patterns are fingerprints

### ❌ Too Much Information
- People don't explain to each other what both know
- Dialogue is not info delivery
- Break up exposition

### ❌ Too Direct
- Real people evade, imply, misdirect
- Direct statements should be rare
- Climactic moments only

### ❌ Overly Clever
- Not every line should be a zinger
- Wit matches character
- Sometimes simple is right

### ❌ No Action
- Dialogue should do something
- Not just exchanging information
- Characters are trying to affect each other

---

## Dialogue Checklist

For each dialogue passage:
1. What does each character want?
2. What are they hiding?
3. Who has power?
4. Does it sound like this specific character?
5. Is there subtext?
6. Does the scene change because of this exchange?
