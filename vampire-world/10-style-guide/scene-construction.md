# Scene Construction

How to build scenes that work in GRRM style.

---

## Core Scene Principles

### Start Late, End Early

**Start Late**:
- Skip the setup
- Begin in the middle of action or tension
- Arrival, preparation, small talk—cut them
- Trust reader to catch up

**End Early**:
- Cut before resolution
- End on tension, question, or hook
- Don't show aftermath (save for next scene)
- Leave reader wanting more

### Example

**Wrong**:
> Marcus rode for three days to reach the city. He stabled his horse, found an inn, cleaned up, and then went to the meeting. After the meeting, he returned to his room to think about what he'd learned.

**Right**:
> "You're late," Lord Vasken said as Marcus entered.
> "I'm exactly on time for a dead man." Marcus dropped the severed head on the table.

## Scene Structure

### Scene vs Sequel

**Scene** (action):
- Goal
- Conflict
- Disaster (setback)

**Sequel** (reaction):
- Reaction
- Dilemma
- Decision

### How They Alternate

Scene creates new situation → Sequel processes it → Decision leads to new Scene

Not every scene needs full sequel, but major scenes should have processing.

---

## Every Scene Needs Conflict

### Types of Conflict

**Physical**: Combat, chase, survival
**Verbal**: Argument, negotiation, interrogation
**Internal**: Decision, doubt, moral struggle
**Social**: Status, manipulation, betrayal

### Conflict Layering

Best scenes have multiple conflicts at once:
- Physical fight while having an argument
- Political negotiation while hiding a secret
- Making a decision while being pursued

### Example

> Elara smiled at Duke Aldric [social conflict] while her mind raced through escape routes [internal conflict]. The assassin was somewhere in this room [physical threat]. She needed Aldric's support [political goal], but if she didn't leave now she'd be dead [survival conflict].

## Scene Goals

### Clear Goal

Every POV character enters scene wanting something. Reader should know what.

**Protagonist goals**:
- Learn something
- Escape someone
- Convince someone
- Obtain something

### Goal Obstacles

Something prevents easy achievement:
- Another character's opposing goal
- Information gap
- Physical barrier
- Internal conflict

### Goal Outcome

Scene ends with goal:
- Achieved (rare—too easy)
- Achieved at cost (good)
- Failed (good—creates next scene)
- Achieved but now worse (great)

## Tension Management

### Building Tension

- Delay resolution
- Add complications
- Raise stakes
- Narrow time
- Increase uncertainty

### Releasing Tension

- After major confrontation
- Brief quiet moments
- Humor (rare and earned)
- Character connection

### Tension Rhythm

Don't maintain maximum tension constantly—vary it:
- High → Brief rest → Higher
- Build through chapter
- Cliffhanger at chapter end

---

## Information Control

### What Reader Learns

Every scene reveals something:
- Plot information
- Character trait
- World detail
- Relationship dynamic

### Information Pacing

**Don't**:
- Reveal everything at once
- Withhold too long (frustrating)
- Info-dump

**Do**:
- One or two revelations per scene
- Balance mystery and clarity
- Let reader piece things together

### The "And Therefore" Test

Scenes should connect: "This happened AND THEREFORE this happened."

Not: "This happened AND THEN this happened."

## Chapter Structure

### One POV Per Chapter

- Entire chapter from single perspective
- Never switch mid-chapter
- Chapter is complete unit

### Chapter Length

- Variable (not all same length)
- 3,000-8,000 words typical
- Shorter for tension
- Longer for complexity

### Chapter Endings

**Hook Types**:
- Cliffhanger (physical danger)
- Revelation (new information)
- Decision (character commits)
- Question (what will happen?)

**Cut Away**:
- At moment of highest tension
- Before resolution
- To different POV
- Creates urgency to return

## Scene Types

### Action Scenes

**Focus**: Physical conflict, survival
**Pacing**: Fast, short sentences
**Details**: Immediate, sensory, visceral
**Goal**: Survive, win, escape

### Dialogue Scenes

**Focus**: Verbal conflict, information
**Pacing**: Medium, varied
**Details**: Subtext, body language
**Goal**: Convince, learn, deceive

### Political Scenes

**Focus**: Power dynamics, maneuvering
**Pacing**: Slow-medium, deliberate
**Details**: Social cues, implications
**Goal**: Gain advantage, form alliance

### Character Scenes

**Focus**: Internal development
**Pacing**: Slow, reflective
**Details**: Emotional, memory
**Goal**: Decision, understanding

## Scene Examples by Type

### Opening Scene (Hook)

- Immediate tension
- Question raised
- Character introduced in action
- World shown through detail

### Revelation Scene

- Setup with false assumption
- Evidence accumulates
- Truth emerges
- Consequences begin

### Confrontation Scene

- Characters with opposing goals
- Stakes clear
- Escalation
- Resolution or standoff

### Planning Scene

- Characters assess situation
- Options discussed
- Decision made
- Reader understands stakes

### Aftermath Scene

- Consequence processing
- Emotional reaction
- New understanding
- Next goal formed

## Pacing Within Scene

### Sentence Rhythm

**Fast action**: Short sentences. Fragments. Quick beats.

**Medium dialogue**: Varied lengths. Some short. Some extending into longer constructions that allow for complexity.

**Slow reflection**: Longer sentences that roll through multiple clauses, allowing the reader to sink into the character's contemplation.

### Paragraph Rhythm

- Short paragraphs: speed
- Long paragraphs: slow
- White space: pause
- Dialogue: varies

## Common Scene Errors

### ❌ No Conflict
- Every scene needs conflict
- Even quiet scenes have tension
- Internal conflict counts

### ❌ Goal Not Clear
- Reader should know what character wants
- Character should be trying for something
- Even if it's just understanding

### ❌ Nothing Changes
- Something must be different at end
- Information, relationships, status
- Scene must matter

### ❌ Too Much Setup
- Start in the action
- Trust reader
- Skip to interesting part

### ❌ Resolved Too Easily
- Complications should complicate
- Success should cost
- Easy wins are boring

---

## Scene Checklist

Before each scene, ask:
1. What does POV character want?
2. What's preventing them?
3. What conflict(s) drive the scene?
4. What information is revealed?
5. How does it end (hook)?
6. What changes by the end?
