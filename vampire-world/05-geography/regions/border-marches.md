# The Border Marches

Contested zones between major powers—flashpoints for conflict, refuges for the desperate, and the lawless spaces where the world's real business gets done.

---

## Overview

**Location**: Various borders between major powers
**Size**: ~100,000 square miles combined
**Climate**: Various (temperate to cold)
**Controlling Power**: Disputed, overlapping, or absent
**Population**: ~1 million (official); ~1.5 million (actual)

The Border Marches aren't a single place—they're the cracks between places. Where empires end and arguments begin. Where laws overlap, contradict, and stop existing. Where people go when they can't go anywhere else.

---

## The Four Major March Zones

### I. The Valdrian-Northern March

**Location**: Mountain passes between Valdrian Empire and Northern Dominion
**Size**: ~30,000 square miles
**Population**: ~300,000

The oldest and most militarized of the Border Marches. For 800 years, Valdris and the North have contested these mountain passes—and the vampire ruins within them.

#### Geography

**The Krell Passes**: Three major routes through the mountains. Control them and you control invasion routes. Both powers have fortresses on their sides, facing each other across narrow valleys.

**The High Ruins**: Vampire installations in the peaks, unreachable by large armies. Explored piecemeal by small teams. Artifacts come down; expedition teams often don't.

**The Valley Towns**: Settlements that have changed hands so many times they've stopped caring who rules them. They pay taxes to whoever shows up to collect.

#### Key Locations

**Fort Valdris**: Valdrian Empire's primary northern fortress. 3,000 garrison, massive walls, watching the passes. Commander General Torvan Blackwood—veteran of three border wars, seventy-two years old, refuses to retire because he's certain the North will attack the moment he leaves.

**Fort Aldric**: Northern Dominion's mirror fortress. Named for the current king's grandfather. 2,500 garrison, newer construction, better artillery. Commander Lord Marshal Vera Ironside—ambitious, forty-three, wants a war because that's the only way she'll get promoted.

**Crossing**: Neutral trading town in the central pass. Both powers have agreed (seventeen times, in seventeen different treaties) to leave it neutral. Population 8,000. Run by a merchant council that sells to both sides and trusts neither. Current council head: Brennan Goldsworth, who's bankrupted three fortunes and made four.

**The Hanging Ruins**: Vampire installation built into a cliff face, accessible only by a path that's collapsed twice. Jointly explored by Valdrian and Northern teams under a Year 1150 treaty that both sides claim the other has violated. Contains: unknown. No expedition has returned from the lower levels.

#### Current Tensions

**Year 1198**: Northern scouts found an artifact cache in the eastern reaches. Valdris claims the find was on their side of the border. The North claims the border was misdrawn. The cache is valuable—and both sides have troops positioned to seize it.

**Year 1199**: Border incident in the Krell Passes. A Valdrian patrol killed a Northern courier. Valdris says the courier was a spy. The North says it was murder. Ambassadors are exchanging threats.

**Year 1200**: King Aldric XII is planning something. Troop movements along the border. Supply caches being built. General Blackwood has sent three warnings to Valdris—and been told to stop warmongering. He's not wrong.

#### The Actual Business

Away from the fortresses, the Valdrian-Northern March is smuggling country. Goods move across the border with taxes paid to neither power. Artifacts come down from the ruins, sold to the highest bidder. Information flows both ways. The people who live here have learned that empires come and go, but the mountains stay.

**Notable Figures**:

- **Kira Thornwood**: Smuggler queen. Fifty-three years old, controls seven passes. Pays bribes to both empires. Has never been arrested because she knows too much about too many people.

- **Brother Malachai**: Church hermit, lives in a cave in the high passes. Supposedly holy. Actually a retired spy for the Veil Keepers, watching the ruins and reporting what comes out of them.

- **The Ghost**: Someone is killing Northern patrols in the eastern passes. Seven dead in two years. No one has seen the killer. The North blames Valdris. Valdris denies it. The Ghost keeps killing.

### II. The Silverwood Borders

**Location**: Forest edges around elven Silverwood
**Size**: ~25,000 square miles
**Population**: ~200,000

The elves sealed their borders in Year 47, and they've stayed sealed. The Silverwood Borders are the human settlements that grew up against those walls—resentful, suspicious, and constantly testing the limits.

#### Geography

**The Treeline**: Where elven forest meets human farmland. Looks natural. Isn't. The elves shaped this boundary over centuries, and they enforce it with arrows.

**The Buffer Zone**: Ten-mile strip that's technically unclaimed. Humans enter at risk. Elves patrol. "Accidents" happen.

**The Treaty Towns**: Human settlements just outside elven arrows range. Built against the forest they can't enter. Logging towns that can't log. Hunting towns that can't hunt. Angry.

#### Key Locations

**Thornwall**: Largest human settlement on the Silverwood border. Population 12,000. Built around a logging operation that was shut down by elven treaty in Year 780. The mills still stand, empty and rotting. The town survives on smuggled timber, trade with elven outcasts, and spite.

Mayor: Aldric Thornwall (yes, they named the town after his family). Fifth generation. Hates elves. His great-great-grandmother was killed by elven arrows when she stepped into the Buffer Zone—by accident, according to humans; deliberately, according to elves.

**The Watching Posts**: Elven guard stations along the border. Staffed by Silverwood rangers who see everything and allow nothing. They have standing orders to kill anyone who enters the forest without authorization. They follow those orders.

**The Old Shrine**: Human temple on the border, built over what was once an elven sacred site. The elves demanded it be removed in Year 430. Humans refused. Tensions escalated to the Twilight War. The shrine is still there. Elves still resent it. Humans consider it a symbol of defiance.

**The Black Market**: Somewhere in the Buffer Zone, there's a trading spot where elven outcasts meet human smugglers. Location changes. Password changes. But it's always happening. Elven goods—medicines, metalwork, enchantments—move into human markets. Human goods—iron, cloth, news—move into the forest. Both sides pretend it doesn't exist.

#### Current Tensions

**The Logging Pressure**: Valdris needs timber. Silverwood has the best. Every few years, Valdrian politicians propose "renegotiating" the treaty. Every time, the elves remind them what happened in the Twilight War. The proposal dies. The need doesn't.

**The Thandril Question**: Thandril Shadowleaf is elven, but he operates outside the forest, often in human territory. He's an official agent of the Silverwood Council, but humans see him as a spy. His presence on the border causes incidents.

**The Forest Changes**: Something is happening in Silverwood. Human observers report changes in the forest—more patrols, more fortification, strange lights at night. The elves aren't talking. They never talk. But something has them worried.

#### The Actual Business

The Silverwood Borders exist because humans want what elves have and can't take it by force. So they scheme, smuggle, and wait. Some want peaceful trade. Some want to burn the forest down. Most just want to survive in the shadow of an ancient, powerful, unknowable neighbor.

**Notable Figures**:

- **Mira Greenleaf**: Half-elven. Runs the largest smuggling operation on the border. Accepted by neither side, uses both. Has information networks in Silverwood that shouldn't exist. The elves haven't killed her yet, which means they're using her too.

- **Father Aldric Sunbrand**: Church priest in Thornwall. Preaches that elves are godless creatures who must eventually accept human faith or be "cleansed." Has a following. Has attracted Church attention. May be promoted or silenced.

- **The Forest Walker**: A human who enters Silverwood without being killed. Disappears for months, comes back with elven goods and no explanation. No one knows how he does it. The elves don't seem to mind him—which is the strangest part.

### III. The Goblin Frontier

**Location**: Multiple borders around the Broken Lands
**Size**: ~30,000 square miles
**Population**: ~400,000 (human); unknown (goblin)

Every major power borders the Broken Lands somewhere. The Goblin Frontier is where those borders meet—a patchwork of human fortresses, raiding zones, and the constant violence of a war that never ended.

#### Geography

**The Frontier Line**: Human fortifications facing goblin territory. Not a wall—too long, too expensive—but a network of forts, patrols, and cleared zones. Built to contain. Doesn't always work.

**The Raiding Grounds**: Territory between the fortifications and the Broken Lands proper. Both sides operate here. Humans hunt goblins. Goblins raid human settlements. No one controls it.

**The Dead Towns**: Human settlements destroyed during the Goblin Wars, never rebuilt. Ruins that goblins use as bases for raids. Dangerous to approach.

#### Key Locations

**Ironhold**: Largest fortress on the Goblin Frontier. Valdrian Empire's primary containment position. Garrison of 5,000. Commander: General Brennan Cole—third generation Ironhold commander, family has been fighting goblins for 200 years. Sees them as vermin to be exterminated.

**The Blood Wall**: Northern Dominion's contribution to goblin containment. Twenty miles of fortified wall, built with forced labor, defended by conscripts. Called "Blood Wall" because of construction casualties—and because of what happens when goblins breach it.

**Ash Creek**: Frontier town that exists despite military recommendations to evacuate. Population 2,000. Survives on bounties for goblin kills and salvage from dead towns. Everyone is armed. Everyone has lost someone to raids.

Sheriff: Mara Blackthorn, thirty-eight, lost her husband and son to a raid in Year 1192. Leads kill teams into the Raiding Grounds. Has a necklace of goblin teeth. Doesn't talk much.

**The Parley Ground**: Traditional neutral site where humans and goblins have occasionally negotiated. Last used in Year 980. Hasn't been used since because every negotiation fails. But it's still there, still recognized. Someone might use it again.

#### Current Tensions

**The Unrest**: Something is stirring in the Broken Lands. Goblin raids are increasing—up 40% since Year 1195. Scouts report more organization, more discipline, more shamans. The goblins are planning something.

**Gritha's Movement**: A goblin shaman named Gritha Bloodsinger is traveling the Frontier, preaching to goblins about "remembering what they were." She's crossing into human territory, contacting goblin slaves and workers. The Empire wants her dead. The Church wants her burned. She keeps moving.

**The Weapons**: Goblins are using better equipment. Iron weapons, armor that doesn't fall apart, even some enchantments. Where are they getting it? Smugglers? Theft? Or is someone arming them?

#### The Actual Business

The Goblin Frontier is where human civilization pretends to end and barbarism begins. But the reality is messier. Humans trade with goblins when it's profitable. Hire them for dirty work. Use them to undermine rival powers. The Frontier is as much about controlling the goblin threat as profiting from it.

**Notable Figures**:

- **Keller Graves**: Bounty hunter. Has killed more goblins than anyone on the Frontier—allegedly 400+. Works for whoever pays. Has no ideology, just a price list. The military hates him because he's better at killing than they are.

- **The Translator**: Anonymous figure who arranges goblin-human communication. Shows up when needed, disappears after. Works both sides. Essential to any negotiation. No one knows their name or race—some think they're half-goblin themselves.

- **Captain Vera Stoneheart**: Commands the worst posting on the Blood Wall—the section that's breached most often. Her troops have the highest casualty rate and the highest morale. She's been offered transfers. She refuses. She has something to prove.

### IV. The Maritime Hinterland

**Location**: Between Maritime Republic and Sanctified States
**Size**: ~15,000 square miles
**Population**: ~150,000

Where money meets faith. The Maritime Republic wants trade routes and profit. The Sanctified States want souls and obedience. The Hinterland is where they collide—and where the refugees from both end up.

#### Geography

**The Coast Road**: Primary trade route between the Republic and other markets. Runs through Sanctified territory. The Church claims authority over travelers. The Republic claims freedom of commerce. Fights are common.

**The Sanctuary Towns**: Settlements that have declared independence from both powers. Some recognized, some not. Where heretics, debtors, and the desperate flee.

**The Temple Line**: Sanctified fortifications guarding their border. Not military—religious. Inquisitors instead of soldiers. Worse.

#### Key Locations

**Freehaven**: Largest Sanctuary Town. Population 20,000. Officially independent, practically Republic-aligned. Run by a council of merchants who fled the Sanctified States for commercial heresy (charging interest). The Church wants it destroyed. The Republic wants it as a buffer.

Council Head: Aldric Coinsworth. Fifty-five years old. Made his fortune in Republic banking. Was excommunicated for usury. Doesn't regret it. Has built Freehaven into a refuge for everyone the Church hates.

**Temple Crossing**: Sanctified States border station. Every traveler is stopped, questioned, and inspected for heretical materials. Delays are deliberate. Bribes are expected. Run by Inquisitor Brennan Ashford—Marcus Ashford's superior, cousin, and enemy.

**The Burnt Villages**: Settlements destroyed during the Year 1150 persecution. Heretics lived here. The Church purged them. The ruins remain as warnings. People still live in some of them—quietly.

**The Smuggler Coast**: Section of shoreline where Republic ships land outside official ports. Goods move without Church inspection. People move without Church interrogation. The Republic officially disapproves. Unofficially profits.

#### Current Tensions

**The Persecution Intensifies**: High Confessor Brennan is escalating action against heresy. More arrests, more executions, more refugees fleeing to the Hinterland. The Republic is accepting them—which the Church sees as harboring criminals.

**Trade War**: The Church is imposing new tariffs on Republic goods, claiming they "corrupt the faithful." The Republic is rerouting trade through the Hinterland, bypassing Sanctified ports. Both sides are losing money and gaining anger.

**Elara's Search**: Elara Goldstone is searching the Hinterland for her daughter, taken by Church "rescuers" during a raid. Her search is becoming an investigation into systematic Church kidnapping of merchant children. She's getting close to truths that could start a war.

#### The Actual Business

The Maritime Hinterland is where two irreconcilable worldviews meet and grudgingly cooperate. The Republic wants profit. The Church wants control. Between them, a space exists where neither has full authority—and that space is worth a fortune to the right people.

**Notable Figures**:

- **Sister Vera Nighthollow**: Officially a Church missionary in the Hinterland. Actually recruiting for the Inheritors. Uses Church cover to access people and places. The Church doesn't know—or chooses not to.

- **Captain Brennan Saltwater**: Republic merchant captain who runs the Smuggler Coast. Has bribed every official in three countries. Provides passage to refugees, for a price. Has been excommunicated four times—keeps getting re-communicated when the Church needs a favor.

- **The Confessor**: Independent inquisitor, not Church-sanctioned. Wanders the Hinterland, punishing "heretics" by his own standards. Has killed forty people. The Church denies knowing him. The Republic has a bounty on him. He keeps moving.

## Common Characteristics

### Settlements

**Fortress Towns**: Military or fortified, centered on defense. Soldiers and their dependents. Suspicious of outsiders. Law is martial. Examples: Fort Valdris, Ironhold.

**Trade Towns**: Exist because of commerce, legal or otherwise. Merchants dominate. Law is negotiable. Examples: Crossing, Freehaven.

**Sanctuary Towns**: Refuges from one or more powers. Mixed populations, many with reasons to hide. Law is whatever keeps the peace. Examples: Freehaven, various unnamed.

**Frontier Towns**: Edge of civilization, barely controlled. Everyone armed, everyone dangerous. Law is survival. Examples: Ash Creek.

### Law and Order

The Border Marches have overlapping, contradictory legal authorities:

- **Imperial Law**: Valdrian claims jurisdiction over Valdrian subjects anywhere.
- **Kingdom Law**: Northern Dominion applies the same logic.
- **Church Law**: Sanctified States claim religious authority over all souls.
- **Treaty Law**: Various agreements about neutral zones, joint jurisdiction.
- **No Law**: Large areas where no authority reaches.

In practice: whoever has the most soldiers or the most money decides.

### Economy

**Legal Trade**: Goods moving between powers. Heavy taxation. Heavy regulation. Most merchants avoid it when possible.

**Smuggling**: The actual economy. Artifacts, weapons, drugs, people—everything moves through the Marches. Estimated value: equal to legal trade or higher.

**Military Supply**: Fortresses need food, weapons, everything. Supplying them is reliable business—if you can handle military contracts.

**Bounties and Salvage**: Goblin kills, artifact recovery, ruin exploration. Dangerous work, high margins. Attracts adventurers, treasure hunters, and fools.

**Mercenary Work**: Every power hires deniable assets. The Marches are full of people willing to do what soldiers won't.

## The Vampire Ruins

The Border Marches contain more unexplored vampire ruins than any other region—because no single power controls them.

### Why They're Here

**Strategic Locations**: Vampires built in defensible positions, which happen to be the same contested terrain that powers fight over.

**Avoided During Empire**: Empires don't explore ruins in disputed territory because rivals might seize discoveries.

**Accessible Now**: Less state control means more independent expeditions.

### Notable Sites

**The Hanging Ruins**: In the Valdrian-Northern March. Jointly claimed, jointly explored, jointly deadly. Levels below the cliff face remain sealed.

**The Forest Cache**: In the Silverwood Buffer Zone. Technically elven territory. Occasionally explored by humans who don't get caught. Contains: unknown.

**The Goblin Tombs**: In the Frontier. Vampire structures that goblins have occupied and fortified. Contain original artifacts, mixed with goblin modifications. Very dangerous.

**The Sunken Temple**: Off the Smuggler Coast. Underwater vampire installation, partially above sea level. Republic divers have been exploring for decades. Artifacts come out regularly. Divers come out occasionally.

### The Artifact Trade

Ruins in the Marches feed the black market. Without state oversight, artifacts move from discovery to buyer in days. The Inheritors have agents watching every site. So do the Veil Keepers. So does the Order of Dawn.

If something important comes out of a March ruin, everyone will know within a week.

## Why Characters Go Here

### Seven's Opportunities

**Anonymity**: The Marches ask few questions. A strange man with no history can find work, shelter, allies.

**Information**: Smugglers, explorers, and spies know things. The Marches are where secrets are traded.

**Access**: Want to reach Silverwood? Get into the North? Find goblin contacts? The Marches have ways.

**Money**: If Seven needs resources, the Marches have work—dangerous, often illegal, but available.

### POV Character Connections

**Kira Frost**: Operates in the Valdrian-Northern March. Smuggling routes, spy networks, military contacts.

**Elara Goldstone**: Searching the Maritime Hinterland. Refugee camps, sanctuary towns, Church investigations.

**Thandril Shadowleaf**: Moves through Silverwood Borders. Illegal crossings, outcasts, black market contacts.

**Gritha Bloodsinger**: Crosses the Goblin Frontier. Preaching, organizing, evading kill teams.

**Marcus Ashford**: May be assigned to Temple Crossing or Hinterland persecution. Confronting his own Church's methods.

## Year 1200 Situation

### Rising Tensions

Every Border March is hotter than it's been in decades:

- **Valdrian-Northern**: Troops massing, artifact dispute unresolved, Ghost still killing
- **Silverwood**: Elves fortifying, Thornwall agitating, something in the forest
- **Goblin Frontier**: Raids increasing, Gritha moving, weapons appearing
- **Maritime Hinterland**: Persecution escalating, trade war brewing, Elara investigating

### Why Now?

No single cause. Multiple pressures converging:

- **Succession crises** making powers unstable
- **Economic pressures** increasing competition
- **Religious persecution** driving refugees
- **Goblin organization** threatening containment
- **The Scar expanding** and everyone nervous

And underneath it all: the sense that something is about to happen. That the next few years will reshape the world. The Border Marches are where that reshaping will start—because the Marches are where the old order is weakest.

## Sensory Experience

### The Valdrian-Northern March

Cold wind through mountain passes. Snow half the year. The smell of woodsmoke and horse. Fortress walls blocking the sky. Soldiers everywhere, watching. The creak of supply wagons. The distant sight of ruins in the peaks. The knowledge that the other side is watching back.

### The Silverwood Borders

The forest looming, always present, impenetrable. Trees that seem to watch. The treeline sharp and unnatural. Human settlements pressed against an edge they can't cross. The smell of sawmills that aren't running. Resentment thick enough to taste.

### The Goblin Frontier

Smoke from burned settlements. Cleared zones where nothing grows. Fort walls stained with old blood. The sound of warning bells from watchtowers. Armed patrols every hour. The sense of something just beyond the perimeter, waiting.

### The Maritime Hinterland

Salt air and incense. Trade ships and temple bells. Checkpoints asking your faith. Refugees huddled in camps. Wealth and poverty side by side. The Church watching. The Republic counting. Money changing hands in every shadow.

*The Border Marches are the world's seams—where powers meet but don't merge, where laws overlap but don't apply, where the desperate go when everywhere else closes its doors. They're dangerous and lawless and full of opportunity. They're where empires send their problems and where problems escape from empires. When Seven wakes up in Vel'Thoras, the Marches will be his first choice if he wants to disappear—and his first danger if he doesn't.*
