# Warlord-King Aldric XII

## Basic Information
- **Role**: King of the Northern Territories, supreme military commander
- **Age**: 48
- **Location**: Frosthold, Northern capital

## Cultural DNA

### Background
Won the kingship through combat trial at age 23. Has held it through constant challenge, executing six rivals and defeating eleven in single combat. Born to a minor war-chief family, rose entirely through martial prowess. His queen is Valdrian (Princess Helena), a political marriage he's grown to respect. Rules the hardest people in the hardest land through sheer force of will.

### Values
- **Strength determines right** - weakness is moral failure
- **Action over deliberation** - thinking is for those who can't do
- **Loyalty to those who prove themselves** - blood means nothing, deeds mean everything
- **Direct confrontation** - subtlety is cowardice
- **Survival of the worthy** - hardship purifies

### Blind Spots
- Dismisses anything that can't be solved with force
- Cannot comprehend long-term economic planning
- Sees diplomacy as weakness disguised
- Ignores problems that require patience
- Assumes all peoples think like Northerners

## Motivations

### Primary Drive
Secure grain supplies for his people before they starve

### Secondary Concerns
- Maintain his position through continued victories
- Establish his children as heirs (combat trials threaten them)
- Expand Northern influence while neighbors weaken
- Find new weapons against ancient enemies

### Fears
- His people starving while he watches helplessly
- Being seen as weak by his war-chiefs
- His children failing combat trials
- The Northern Territories fragmenting after his death
- Dying in bed instead of battle

## Economic Position

### Wealth
Royal treasury: ~80,000 crowns (minimal by southern standards)
War spoils: ~30,000 crowns

### Income
Tribute from war-chiefs: ~25,000 crowns annually
Raiding profits: variable (~10,000-40,000 crowns)

### Stakes
The grain crisis threatens everything. Three failed harvests have emptied reserves. Without new supply sources, his people starve or scatter. He must act, regardless of consequences.

## Relationships

### Allies
- **War-chiefs** - bound by shared victories and combat honor
- **Princess Helena** - respects her intelligence and loyalty
- **Northern military** - personally loyal to the warrior-king

### Enemies
- **Valdrian Empire** - holds grain his people need
- **Order of Dawn** - religious fanatics who raid his borders
- **Time** - the grain crisis won't wait for politics

### Complicated
- **Dwarven kingdoms** - respects their strength, needs their neutrality
- **Seven** - potential weapon or potential threat
- **Commander Blackwood** - useful but too clever

## Knowledge

### Knows
- Military strength and disposition of all neighbors
- Exactly how much grain remains (weeks, not months)
- Which war-chiefs are loyal and which ambitious
- Northern terrain and how to fight in it
- That the Valdrian succession creates opportunity

### Believes
- Force solves all problems eventually
- Southern weakness will collapse under pressure
- His people are harder and therefore better
- Complexity is excuse-making for cowardice
- Taking what you need is morally neutral

### Doesn't Know
- How fragile Northern logistics actually are
- That Helena still reports to Valdrian intelligence
- The depth of Maritime banking manipulation
- How dwarven neutrality might shift
- That vampires exist beyond old stories

## Speech Pattern

### Markers
- Short, declarative sentences
- No qualifications or hedging
- Commands rather than requests
- Hunting and combat metaphors
- Dismissive of lengthy explanation

### Sample Lines
"We act. We win. Philosophy is for those with full bellies."

"Your borders are weak. Your granaries are full. This is not complicated."

"I took this throne with my hands. I will hold it the same way."

"Talk when the fighting's done. Until then, fight."

## Story Function

Aldric represents the tragedy of strength without flexibility. He is genuinely capable, genuinely protective of his people, and genuinely dangerous because he has only one tool. His invasion of Valdris for grain could trigger the continent-wide war.

He serves as:
- External military threat to Valdrian stability
- Force that accelerates succession crisis
- Potential employer or enemy for protagonist
- Contrast with southern political complexity
- Symbol of what happens when survival trumps civilization
- Source of military pressure and strategic directness
