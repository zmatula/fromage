## Core Principle

**Documentation tells. Drama shows.**

Every major historical event needs at least one scene showing it through a character's eyes. Readers should feel history, not just understand it.

## Phase 1: Pre-Vampire Political Complexity

**Goal**: Show functioning civilizations before conquest so readers understand what was lost.

### 1.1 New Document: Pre-Vampire Political Scenes

**Location**: `/home/user/fromage/vampire-world/02-timeline/era-breakdowns/pre-vampire-scenes.md`

**Content**:

#### Scene 1: Valdrian Succession Crisis (Year -1100)
- King Aldric III lies dying
- Two sons compete for throne: Marius (warrior) and Valdric (scholar)
- Council debates which is needed—strength or wisdom
- Marius wins by one vote; Valdric's supporters become permanent opposition
- **Purpose**: Shows human politics weren't simple; internal divisions existed

#### Scene 2: Merchant Principalities Trade War (Year -1080)
- Prince-Merchant of Seahaven proposes tariff against Portmere
- Debate in Council of Princes
- Argument about whether unity matters more than profit
- Decision to maintain unity—but resentment planted
- **Purpose**: Shows economic tensions that vampires later exploited

#### Scene 3: Holy Confederation Heresy Trial (Year -1070)
- High Priest Aldric Dawnward presides
- Scholar accused of "Dual Nature Heresy"—claiming Divine Light is force, not god
- Defender argues truth serves the Divine
- Guilty verdict; scholar executed
- **Purpose**: Shows religious rigidity that later became Church of Divine Light

#### Scene 4: Elven Moonspire Council (Year -1060)
- King Vaelin proposes military alliance with humans against "eastern threat"
- Elder Silvermoon argues isolation is safety
- Vote splits; alliance pursued half-heartedly
- **Purpose**: Shows elven divisions that made them vulnerable

#### Scene 5: Dwarven Succession Dispute (Year -1055)
- High King Borin II's son challenges cousin for throne
- Argument about whether High King should have real power
- Cousin wins; son's faction becomes permanent opposition
- **Purpose**: Shows dwarven resistance to central authority

## Phase 2: The Great Gathering

**Goal**: Make goblin historical trauma visceral and heartbreaking.

### 2.1 New Document: The Great Gathering

**Location**: `/home/user/fromage/vampire-world/02-timeline/era-breakdowns/great-gathering.md`

**Structure**:

#### Part 1: The Goblin Perspective

**Scene: Shaman-King Grist Blood-Eye's Council (Year -1051)**
- Grist argues they must seek alliance
- Elder shaman warns other races see goblins as monsters
- Young war-chief says fight alone or die
- Grist: "We will die alone if we do not try together"
- Decision to send envoys to all races

**Scene: Selecting the Envoys**
- Grist chooses his own son, Vren, to lead delegation to humans
- Vren's mother begs Grist not to send him
- Grist: "If I will not risk my son, why should others risk theirs?"
- Three envoys selected: Vren (humans), Skaela (elves), Threk (dwarves)

#### Part 2: The Missions

**Scene: Vren's Arrival at Valdrian Court (Year -1050)**
- Vren approaches with white banner
- Guards are hostile but let him speak
- Vren addresses King Marius II
- Explains vampire threat, proposes alliance
- Marius's advisor whispers: "Goblins are monsters. This is a trick."
- Marius orders execution
- Vren's last words: "You doom us both"
- Execution described—not gratuitous, but clear

**Scene: Skaela at Silverwood Border**
- Elven guards refuse to let her enter
- She waits three days
- Message comes: "Silverwood does not treat with goblins"
- Skaela returns home
- Brief: elves didn't even listen

**Scene: Threk at Ironpeak Gates**
- Dwarves let him in, hear his words
- Council debates
- Decision: "We will defend our holds. Goblins must defend theirs."
- Polite refusal
- Threk: "When they come for you, remember we asked"

#### Part 3: The Return

**Scene: Grist Receives News**
- Skaela and Threk return
- Messenger arrives with Vren's body—head removed, returned as "warning"
- Grist's reaction: grief, then cold rage
- "Remember this. Remember that we asked. Remember that they answered with my son's head."
- The Oath: "We will never ask again. We will never forget."

#### Part 4: The Consequence

**Brief flash-forward**:
- Goblins conquered alone (Year -1020)
- During vampire era, goblins treated worst
- Post-collapse, goblins preserve blood magic
- Year 1200: Gritha Bloodsinger looks at humans and sees the race that killed Vren

### 2.2 Integration

- Reference in `era-pre-vampire.md`
- Reference in goblin race file
- Reference in consequence chains (already exists, strengthen)

### 2.3 Success Criteria

- [ ] Reader feels Grist's choice to send his son
- [ ] Execution of Vren is gut-punch
- [ ] Other races' refusals feel real (not cartoonish)
- [ ] Connection to Year 1200 goblin rage is clear

## Phase 3: Golden Age Through Character Eyes

**Goal**: Show vampire civilization as lived experience, not museum exhibit.

### 3.1 New Document: Golden Age Scenes

**Location**: `/home/user/fromage/vampire-world/02-timeline/era-breakdowns/golden-age-scenes.md`

**Content**:

#### Scene 1: Bloodweb Dependency Discovery (Year -310)

**POV**: Elder vampire (unnamed, Twilight) realizes she can't survive without Bloodweb

- She tries to go three days without connecting
- Physical symptoms: weakness, disorientation, pain
- She connects and feels relief—then horror
- She reports to High Seer
- High Seer: "We know. We are preparing."
- Elder: "Does Mind know?"
- High Seer: "Mind knows. Mind doesn't care."

**Purpose**: Shows dependency as personal horror, not technical problem

#### Scene 2: The Artist's Dilemma (Year -350)

**POV**: Theron Bloodwright receives commission for "Eternal Dominion" fresco

- Warlord-Regent wants "our glory for eternity"
- Theron sees what Throne actually is: brutal, rigid, fearful
- He paints what they want
- That night, he begins secret work: "The Hollow Throne"
- Internal monologue about living a lie for centuries

**Purpose**: Shows not all vampires were blind; some saw truth and couldn't speak it

#### Scene 3: Lysara's Last Warning (Year -30)

**POV**: Lysara the Dissenter addresses Council of Savants

- She presents dependency data
- Shows Bloodweb failure projections
- Proposes safety protocols
- Council dismisses: "Progress cannot be stopped"
- Archon Mordecai: "Your concerns are noted"
- After meeting, colleague warns her: "They're going to silence you"
- Lysara: "Let them. The evidence exists."
- Colleague: "They'll destroy that too"

**Purpose**: Shows warnings existed and were deliberately suppressed

#### Scene 4: Servant Race Optimization (Year -330)

**POV**: Human administrator in optimization facility

- He oversees "enhancement" of human laborers
- Sees pain, hears screams, records data
- Tells himself it's for the greater good
- One subject looks at him: "You're one of us"
- He continues his work
- That night, he can't sleep

**Purpose**: Shows how vampires used collaborators; shows human complicity

#### Scene 5: The Pleasures of Eternity (Year -200)

**POV**: Young vampire attending a "sensation party"

- Blood symphonies, memory theater, extreme experiences
- She's initially excited
- Realizes the "entertainment" uses suffering servants
- Host: "They don't feel it like we do"
- She leaves
- Later learns staying silent makes her complicit
- She stays silent anyway

**Purpose**: Shows moral decay of civilization; shows how good individuals become complicit

### 3.2 Integration

- Cross-reference from `era-golden-age.md`
- Connect to named figures (Theron, Lysara)
- Show what present-day scholars can and can't know

### 3.3 Success Criteria

- [ ] Reader experiences Bloodweb dependency as horror
- [ ] Propaganda machine feels real and oppressive

## Phase 4: The Collapse Dramatized

### 4.1 New Document: The Collapse

**Location**: `/home/user/fromage/vampire-world/02-timeline/collapse-scenes.md`

**Structure**: Multiple POVs across days, showing collapse spreading

#### Day 1: The Experiment (14th Deepwinter, Year 0)

**POV 1: Archon Mordecai in Apex Spire**
- Final preparations
- Confidence that they will transcend
- Experiment begins
- Something goes wrong
- His last thought: "We were so close"

**POV 2: Velanna in secondary laboratory**
- Monitoring experiment remotely
- Sees readings spike
- Realizes what's happening
- Runs to protagonist's stasis chamber
- "You were always the real project"
- Seals him; energy wave hits
- Her death

**POV 3: Mind civilian in Neth'Korath street**
- Sees Apex Spire light up
- Beautiful at first
- Then pain—Bloodweb inverting
- Collapse
- Brief, brutal

#### Day 2-3: The Spread

**POV 4: Throne commander at northern border**
- Receives word from Neth'Korath: "catastrophe"
- Tries to rally forces
- Soldiers start dying—Bloodweb dependency
- Realizes there's no enemy to fight
- Orders evacuation
- Most die before they can move

**POV 5: Twilight sanctuary warden**
- Monitoring collapse spread
- High Seer Selene orders: "Seal the sanctuary"
- Warden: "There are still people outside"
- Selene: "If we open, we all die"
- He seals the door
- Can hear people pounding on the outside
- Pounding stops

#### Day 4-7: The Aftermath

**POV 6: Escaped vampire (Twilight)**
- Didn't make it to sanctuary
- Hiding in ruins
- Watching other survivors die
- Realizes he can live without Bloodweb—Twilight training
- But he's alone
- "We prepared to survive. We didn't prepare to live."

**POV 7: Human servant in Vel'Thoras**
- Watches masters die
- Initial reaction: relief
- Then: horror at scale
- Then: fear of what comes next
- Begins to loot—not greed, survival
- Other servants doing the same
- "We're free. Now what?"

- Rage becomes tradition

## Quality Standards

### Each Scene Must Have

1. **POV character** with name (or clear identity)
2. **Specific sensory details** (not just events)
3. **Internal experience** (thoughts, feelings)
4. **Stakes** (what's at risk)
5. **Connection** to larger history
6. **Emotional weight** (reader should feel something)

### Each Scene Must NOT Have

1. Info-dumps disguised as dialogue
2. Characters explaining things they would know
3. Perfect knowledge of situation
4. Neat resolution
5. Morally simple choices

### Tone Guidelines

- **Tragedy, not melodrama**: Understatement is more powerful
- **Specific, not general**: "She saw his face" not "she felt sad"
- **Moral complexity**: No pure villains or heroes
- **Earned emotion**: Build to moments, don't announce them
