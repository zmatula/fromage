# The Collapse: Competing Theories

Seven scholarly explanations for why vampire civilization died—each with evidence, each with agendas, none complete.

---

## The Problem of Evidence

The Collapse destroyed most records. The Scar makes investigation lethal. Survivors had reasons to lie. Every theory about what happened on Day Zero is built on fragments, and every fragment has been interpreted to serve someone's interests.

What follows are the major scholarly positions, the evidence they cite, and the political purposes they serve. One of them might be true. All of them are useful to someone.

---

## Theory 1: The Ascension Accident

### The Claim

Archon Mordecai's Ascension Experiment went catastrophically wrong. He attempted to open dimensional conduits to transcend vampire limitations, but the breach destabilized the Bloodweb, causing the inversion that killed 99% of vampires.

### The Evidence

**Physical**:
- The Scar's dimensional instability proves a breach occurred
- Bloodweb nodes show inversion damage consistent with energy reversal
- Timing matches Mordecai's announced experiment date

**Documentary**:
- Mind covenant records show escalating dimensional research
- Warnings from Vaelith about experimental risks (Year -60)
- Mordecai's own notes reference "acceptable losses"

**Testimonial**:
- Twilight sanctuary survivors report feeling the Bloodweb reverse
- Dwarven observers at Neth'Korath describe "the sky tearing open"
- Elven far-seers recorded the dimensional breach in real-time

### The Problems

- Why would Mordecai risk his own life?
- The experiment was supposed to save vampires, not kill them
- Some evidence suggests the Bloodweb was already failing before the experiment

### Who Benefits

**The Church**: Proves vampire hubris caused their downfall—divine punishment for playing god.

**Valdrian Empire**: The accident narrative means no one is currently responsible. The past is past.

**Inheritors**: They can study the technology without moral burden—it was misused, not evil.

### Scholar Champion

**Master Aldric Thorne**, Valdrian Academy of Natural Philosophy (Year 1156)

> "The evidence is overwhelming. Mordecai reached too far and killed his people. This isn't mystery—it's tragedy. The only question is whether he knew the risk and proceeded anyway, or whether he was simply wrong. Neither answer redeems him."

## Theory 2: The Covenant War Thesis

### The Claim

The Collapse wasn't an accident—it was an attack. Throne covenant, scheduled to launch their coup in Year 1, sabotaged Mordecai's experiment to destroy Mind covenant. The sabotage worked too well and killed everyone.

### The Evidence

**Political**:
- Throne planned a coup for Year 1 (documented)
- Throne had infiltrated Mind research facilities
- Cassius the Patient was known for elaborate long-term schemes

**Technical**:
- The Bloodweb inversion required specific frequency manipulation
- Only someone with deep system knowledge could cause it
- Throne had captured Bloodweb nodes and studied them

**Circumstantial**:
- Cassius died fighting during the Collapse—why fight if you caused it?
- Unless he was trying to limit damage he hadn't anticipated

### The Problems

- Throne would have killed themselves too
- No Throne records suggest sabotage capability
- The scale of destruction exceeds any tactical purpose

### Who Benefits

**Northern Kingdom**: Descended from Throne territories—if Throne was victim, not perpetrator, Northern legitimacy increases.

**Order of Dawn**: All vampires were equally evil, fighting each other to destruction.

**Veil Keepers**: Complicated truth is more interesting than simple accident.

### Scholar Champion

**Sera Nighthollow**, independent historian (Year 1189)

> "Cassius spent sixty years planning a coup. You don't plan that long and then let your enemy perform an experiment that might make the coup unnecessary. He sabotaged it. He just didn't understand what he was sabotaging."

---

## Theory 3: The Twilight Trigger

### The Claim

Twilight covenant caused the Collapse deliberately. They knew vampire civilization was doomed by Bloodweb dependency, so they accelerated the end to ensure their sanctuaries survived while their rivals died.

### The Evidence

**Preparation**:
- Twilight built sanctuaries starting Year -80
- Sanctuaries were specifically shielded from Bloodweb effects
- They selected survivors while others did nothing

**Motive**:
- Twilight was the weakest covenant militarily
- They couldn't win a war against Mind or Throne
- Destroying everyone else was their only path to dominance

**Timing**:
- Twilight completed their last sanctuary in Year -3
- The Collapse happened in Year 0
- They were ready; no one else was

### The Problems

- Twilight died too—only 25,000 of 3 million survived
- No evidence of Twilight sabotage capability
- Selene the Eternal disappeared—if she planned this, why not claim victory?

### Who Benefits

**Mind descendants**: The Inheritors can claim Mind was innocent victim, not perpetrator.

**Throne descendants**: Northern Kingdom can claim the same.

**Anyone suspicious of secret survivors**: If Twilight caused it, sanctuary survivors are guilty.

### Scholar Champion

**Brother Aldric**, Church of Divine Light (Year 1145)

> "Selene called herself 'the Eternal.' She built refuges while others built weapons. She selected survivors while others selected targets. And then everyone died except her chosen. You call that coincidence. I call it conspiracy."

## Theory 4: The Dwarven Refusal

### The Claim

The Collapse was caused by dwarven economic warfare. By refusing to supply Bloodstone and Soulforged materials, dwarves forced vampires into desperate measures. The Ascension Experiment was a last-ditch attempt to escape dependency—and dwarves knew it would fail.

### The Evidence

**Economic**:
- Dwarves cut off Bloodstone supply Year -200
- No new Soulforged items after Year -200
- Vampire infrastructure couldn't be maintained without these materials

**Technical**:
- Dwarves understood Bloodweb mechanics better than vampires
- They could have predicted cascade failure
- They had the knowledge to warn vampires—and didn't

**Political**:
- Dwarves emerged from the Collapse as major power
- They control the Forge of Souls
- They've profited from vampire absence for 1,200 years

### The Problems

- Dwarves didn't cause the experiment—Mordecai did
- Economic pressure isn't the same as murder
- Dwarves also lost trade partners and suffered in the chaos

### Who Benefits

**Anyone wanting to pressure dwarves**: If dwarves are guilty, they owe reparations.

**Human kingdoms**: Dwarven monopolies become illegitimate if based on genocide.

**Elves**: Deflects attention from elven withdrawal.

### Scholar Champion

**Merchant-Prince Valdris Ironhand**, Maritime Federation (Year 1178)

> "The dwarves knew exactly what they were doing. They strangled vampire civilization economically, then watched it choke. They didn't swing the sword, but they forged it and sold it to the killer. That's guilt enough."

---

## Theory 5: The Elven Withdrawal

### The Claim

Elves caused the Collapse by withdrawing from vampire society. Specifically, by cutting off Starweave silk, they made certain rituals impossible. Mordecai's experiment was a substitute for a process that would have worked if elves had cooperated.

### The Evidence

**Material**:
- Elves stopped Starweave exports Year -150
- Certain Mind rituals required Starweave components
- Mordecai's notes reference "inadequate materials"

**Knowledge**:
- Elves claim to remember everything
- They knew vampire dependency was fatal
- They withdrew at exactly the wrong time

**Behavior**:
- Elves sealed their borders at the Collapse
- They've never explained their withdrawal
- They still won't share Starweave

### The Problems

- Elves didn't force Mordecai to experiment
- Their withdrawal was gradual, not sudden
- They suffered too—isolation has costs

### Who Benefits

**Anyone wanting elven knowledge**: If elves are guilty, they should share secrets as reparation.

**Dwarves**: Deflects attention from their own withdrawal.

**Humans**: Elven isolation becomes aggression, not neutrality.

### Scholar Champion

**Lord Commander Aldric Frost**, Northern Kingdom (Year 1190)

> "The elves saw what was coming. They didn't warn anyone. They didn't help. They sealed their borders and watched fifteen million vampires die. That's not neutrality. That's complicity."

## Theory 6: The Entity Incursion

### The Claim

The Collapse wasn't caused by any vampire faction—it was caused by something from beyond. The dimensional breach didn't kill vampires; something came through and killed them. The Scar isn't a wound; it's a doorway.

### The Evidence

**The Scar**:
- Still dimensionally active after 1,200 years
- The Figures aren't ghosts—they're entities
- The whispers are communication attempts
- It's growing—wounds don't grow

**The Timing**:
- Mordecai's experiment opened a breach
- Something was waiting for exactly that
- The breach didn't close because something is holding it open

**The Phenomena**:
- The Pull draws people toward the center
- The Figures watch but don't attack—yet
- The Year 1195 incident: something emerged

### The Problems

- No direct evidence of entities
- Could be dimensional effects, not beings
- Easier explanation: Mordecai's mistake, not invasion

### Who Benefits

**The Church**: Proves there are real demons, real evil beyond mortal understanding.

**Inheritors**: If something came through, vampire knowledge is needed to fight it.

**Military factions**: External threat justifies military expansion.

### Scholar Champion

**Sister Helena**, Order of Sacred Observation (Year 1192)

> "I've stood at the Distortion Ring. I've heard the whispers. I've seen the Figures. Those aren't echoes of the past. They're something else. Something that was waiting for the door to open. And it's still there. Still waiting."

## Theory 7: The Deliberate Sacrifice

### The Claim

Mordecai caused the Collapse deliberately. He knew the Bloodweb was killing vampires slowly. He chose to kill them quickly instead—to end their suffering, to prevent a worse death, to make sure his research wasn't captured by enemies.

### The Evidence

**Mordecai's Character**:
- He was ruthless and pragmatic
- He killed Vessels 1-6 when they failed
- He would choose quick death over slow failure

**The Timing**:
- The experiment happened when the Bloodweb was already failing
- Node failures were killing vampires monthly
- Complete collapse was inevitable within decades

**The Mind Codex**:
- Mordecai encoded his consciousness before the experiment
- He expected to die
- He had a plan for after

### The Problems

- Why kill everyone instead of saving some?
- The Vessels could have survived—why not make more?
- This makes him a monster, not a tragic figure

### Who Benefits

**No one directly**: This theory makes everyone uncomfortable.

**Philosophically**: Some prefer a deliberate end to a meaningless accident.

**The Inheritors secretly**: If Mordecai had a plan, the Mind Codex contains it.

### Scholar Champion

**The Whispering Sage**, identity unknown (Year 1134)

> "Mordecai was not a fool. He knew what his experiment would do. He did it anyway. Not because he failed to calculate—because he succeeded. He calculated that quick death was better than slow. He was probably right."

## The Hidden Eighth Theory

Spoken only in whispers. No scholar claims it publicly.

### The Claim

The Collapse was orchestrated by someone outside the three covenants. Someone who wanted vampires dead and had the knowledge to make it happen. Someone who is still alive.

### The Possibilities

**Selene the Eternal**:
- Disappeared during the Collapse
- Had sanctuary access
- Knew Mordecai's research
- Motive: save her covenant by destroying the others

**Vessel Eleven**:
- Created as "improved" version of Seven
- May have been completed before the Collapse
- Had no loyalty to vampire society
- Motive: freedom

**An unknown party**:
- Someone with Bloodweb access
- Someone with dimensional knowledge
- Someone who benefited from vampire extinction
- Someone who's been hiding for 1,200 years

### The Evidence

There is none. That's what makes it frightening.

### Who Benefits

No one speaks this theory because no one wants to consider its implications. If someone deliberately killed fifteen million vampires and has been free for 1,200 years, they could do it again.

## The Synthesis Problem

Each theory explains some evidence. None explains all of it.

**What we know for certain**:
- The Ascension Experiment happened
- A dimensional breach opened
- The Bloodweb inverted
- Fifteen million vampires died
- The Scar remains

**What we don't know**:
- Whether the inversion was accident or sabotage
- Whether anyone knew what would happen
- Whether something came through the breach
- Whether anyone is still guilty

### The Uncomfortable Conclusion

The Collapse may have had multiple causes. The Bloodweb was failing. The covenants were fighting. The dwarves and elves had withdrawn. Mordecai was desperate. Cassius was plotting. Selene was preparing.

Everyone contributed. Everyone is guilty. And that's why no one theory ever wins—because accepting one means absolving the others.

The truth might be that vampire civilization didn't die from one wound. It died from a dozen wounds, all at once, any one of which might have healed but together were fatal.

That's not a satisfying answer. But it might be the right one.

## Implications for Seven

When Vessel Seven awakens, he'll encounter all these theories. People will ask him what really happened. He was there—sort of. He was in stasis during the Collapse itself, but he lived in vampire society. He knew Velanna. He might have known Mordecai.

**What he can confirm**:
- The political tensions between covenants
- The Bloodweb dependency crisis
- The desperation of Mind's final years
- Velanna's role in the Vessel project

**What he can't confirm**:
- Whether the experiment was sabotaged
- Whether anyone planned the outcome
- What came through the breach
- Where Selene went

His testimony will be used by everyone to support their preferred theory. He'll be pressured to confirm this, deny that, remember conveniently.

And he'll have to decide: Does he tell what he knows, even if it damns people who are still alive? Does he protect secrets that might be protecting him?

Does he even know the truth himself?
