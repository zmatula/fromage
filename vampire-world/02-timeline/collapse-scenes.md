# The Collapse

*14th Deepwinter, Year 0 and the days that followed*
*The end of vampire civilization—lived, not summarized*

---

## Overview

The Collapse was not a moment. It was days of spreading catastrophe, experienced differently by everyone who lived through it—and the many more who didn't.

---

## Day 1: The Experiment

### Scene 1: Archon Mordecai in Apex Spire

**14th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Neth'Korath, Apex Spire Central Chamber**
**POV: Archon Mordecai, Master of the Ascension Project**

---

Four hundred years had led to this moment.

Mordecai stood at the heart of Apex Spire, surrounded by the culmination of his life's work. The Ascension Engine—a construct of dimensional gates, Bloodweb amplifiers, and technologies even he didn't fully understand—hummed with gathered power. Around him, fifty Mind savants monitored systems, adjusted flows, prepared for transcendence.

"Power levels optimal," Savant Krell reported.

"Dimensional barriers at minimum thickness," another called. "The thin point is aligned."

Mordecai closed his eyes. He could feel the Bloodweb thrumming through him—the vast network that connected every vampire in the world. Soon, it would carry them beyond flesh. Beyond mortality. Beyond the limitations that had constrained them for millennia.

He thought of Lysara. Poor, paranoid Lysara with her doomsday projections. She'd died in the mind-prisons years ago, never understanding that the Bloodweb wasn't a vulnerability—it was a gift. The means by which vampire consciousness would ascend as one.

"Initiate primary sequence," Mordecai commanded.

The Engine began to spin. Energy built—visible now, crackling between the dimensional gates, colors that didn't exist in normal space. The savants' voices became urgent, calling readings that escalated beyond predicted ranges.

"Power at 120%. 140%."

"Dimensional barrier thinning faster than projected—"

"Bloodweb amplification reaching critical—"

Mordecai felt it—the connection opening. Something on the other side. Something vast and waiting. He reached toward it, felt it reaching back—

*Not what we expected.*

The thought came from somewhere outside himself. A cold awareness. An alien intelligence that had been waiting for this door to open.

"Archon! The barriers—"

The dimensional gates shattered.

Energy exploded outward—not controlled, not directed, just raw force tearing through the Bloodweb like lightning through copper wire. Mordecai felt every vampire consciousness in the world convulse as the network they depended on inverted, reversed, became poison instead of sustenance.

His last thought was almost admiration.

*We were so close. We almost—*

He never finished it.

### Scene 2: Velanna in the Secondary Laboratory

**14th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Neth'Korath, Project Vessel Laboratory**
**POV: Velanna of Mind Covenant, Lead Researcher**

---

Velanna was monitoring the Ascension from her secondary laboratory when the readings spiked.

"That's not right," she murmured.

She was eight hundred years old. She'd seen experiments go wrong before. This was something else—something outside the boundaries of what she understood.

The displays lit up with cascading failures. Power levels impossible. Dimensional barriers collapsing. And the Bloodweb—

"Gods." She stood. "It's inverting."

She felt it a moment later—a wrongness in her body, in her connection to the network. The Bloodweb that had sustained her for centuries was now tearing at her from the inside. She had minutes. Maybe less.

But the Vessel—

She ran.

The laboratory was three corridors away. She passed other vampires in the halls—some collapsing, some convulsing, some simply staring as their bodies betrayed them. She didn't stop. Couldn't stop.

She'd spent thirty years on Project Vessel. A human subject modified with vampire physiology, designed to bridge the gap between races. Her greatest work. Her insurance policy against exactly this scenario.

The door was sealed. She keyed it open with shaking hands.

He was there. In the stasis chamber. Still alive—the monitors confirmed it. Still perfect. The experiment that Mordecai had considered secondary, that Throne had wanted terminated, that she'd hidden and protected for three decades.

"You were always the real project," she whispered.

She could feel herself dying now. The Bloodweb inversion was eating through her nervous system. Her hands were numb. Her vision was fading.

She initialized the emergency protocols. Deep stasis. Indefinite duration. He would sleep until someone woke him, and when he woke—

*He'll have to make sense of this. Whatever's left.*

She sealed the chamber. The stasis field activated.

The energy wave hit the laboratory. Velanna felt it pass through her—not pain exactly, more like absence, like every part of her that mattered was simply ceasing to be.

Her last act was to check the monitors one more time. The Vessel was stable. He would survive.

She died knowing something would remain.

### Scene 3: Mind Civilian in Neth'Korath Streets

**14th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Neth'Korath, Market District**
**POV: Telaris, Merchant of the Third Circle**

---

Telaris was closing his shop when the light appeared.

Apex Spire—he could see it from his street, the great tower where the Archons worked their mysteries. It was glowing. Not the usual functional light of operating systems, but something else. Something that made his eyes hurt to look at.

"Beautiful," his assistant murmured.

It was beautiful. Colors shifting through spectrums Telaris had never seen, dancing around the Spire's peak like aurora. He stepped into the street to see better. Others were doing the same—neighbors, customers, strangers—all staring up at the light show.

Then the pain hit.

It started in his chest—his connection point to the Bloodweb. A burning that spread outward through his veins, his nerves, his muscles. He doubled over. Around him, everyone was doing the same.

*What—*

His body wasn't working right. Signals misfiring. The Bloodweb that had kept him healthy for two centuries was doing something wrong, pushing him toward failure instead of pulling him toward function.

He fell. The cobblestones were cold against his face. He could see his neighbors falling too—everyone in the street, all at once, collapsing like puppets with cut strings.

His assistant crawled toward him. "Telaris—what's—"

She stopped moving. Her eyes went empty.

Telaris tried to stand. His legs wouldn't respond. He tried to call for help. His voice wouldn't work.

The light from Apex Spire pulsed once more, and then everything went dark.

*This isn't possible*, he thought. *We're immortal. We don't—*

He did.

## Days 2-3: The Spread

### Scene 4: Throne Commander at Northern Border

**15th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Northern Fortifications, Borderwatch Command**
**POV: Commander Vexar, Throne Covenant Military**

---

The message arrived at dawn: catastrophe in Neth'Korath. Unknown nature. All communications severed.

Vexar had commanded the northern border for sixty years. He'd seen wars, plagues, rebellions. He'd never seen anything that cut communications. The Bloodweb didn't fail. That wasn't possible.

"Get me Apex Spire," he ordered.

"Sir, we've tried. Nothing's responding."

"Then get me regional command. Provincial headquarters. Anyone."

Silence on all channels.

Vexar looked at his officers. They looked back with eyes that understood what the silence meant even if they couldn't name it.

"Rally the garrison," he said. "Full mobilization. We prepare for—"

He stopped.

One of his lieutenants was shaking. Not fear—convulsions. The man's face contorted, and he collapsed.

Then another. And another.

"Sir!" Captain Thrace grabbed Vexar's arm. "I can feel it—the Bloodweb—something's wrong with the Bloodweb—"

Vexar felt it too. A wrongness in his blood, in his connection to the network that sustained all vampires. The Bloodweb was inverting, turning from sustenance to poison. And there was nothing to fight, nothing to defend against. The enemy was inside them.

"Evacuation," he managed. "Everyone who can still move—evacuate. Head for the Twilight sanctuaries—"

"Sir, that's three hundred miles—"

"It's the only chance."

He gave the order. Maybe a hundred of his garrison could still stand. They began to move, heading west, heading for the only vampires who might have prepared for this.

Most of them collapsed within the first mile. The Bloodweb inversion was accelerating. Vampires who'd depended on the network for decades, centuries—their bodies couldn't survive the reversal.

Vexar made it two miles. He fell in a field, looking up at a sky that didn't care what was happening below it.

*We should have listened to Twilight*, he thought. *They warned us. They—*

He had time to realize he was dying, and then he was dead.

### Scene 5: Twilight Sanctuary Warden

**16th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Western Reaches, Sanctuary Seven**
**POV: Warden Kyros, Twilight Covenant**

---

They'd prepared for this. Three hundred years of preparation—independent power systems, stockpiled blood, training to survive without the Bloodweb.

Kyros had always thought they were being paranoid. Now he stood at the sanctuary gates and watched the world end, and understood they hadn't been paranoid enough.

"Another group approaching," the watchman called. "Forty. Maybe fifty."

Kyros looked. He could see them—vampires staggering toward the sanctuary, many crawling, some being dragged by others. They'd come from the east. They looked half-dead already.

"How many inside?" he asked.

"Three hundred and twelve. Capacity is four hundred."

Enough room. If these were the last, they could take them.

*If these were the last.*

High Seer Selene appeared beside him. Her face was calm, but her hands were clenched.

"We've been tracking the collapse wave," she said. "It's spreading at approximately sixty miles per hour. It will reach us within the hour."

"The sanctuary shields?"

"Untested against this. We think they'll hold. We don't know."

The approaching vampires were close now. Kyros could see their faces—terror, hope, desperation. They knew the sanctuary was their only chance.

"We have to let them in," Kyros said.

"If we open the gates when the wave hits—"

"They'll die out there."

"We'll all die in here." Selene's voice was steady. Kyros knew that tone—the tone of someone who'd already made a terrible decision. "The shields must be sealed before the wave arrives. Anyone outside when that happens..."

"You're asking me to close the gates on them."

"I'm telling you what has to happen."

Kyros looked at the approaching group. They were close enough now that he could hear them—calling out, begging, some just making sounds that weren't words. They could see the sanctuary. They could see salvation.

"How long until the wave?"

"Forty minutes. Maybe less."

"And how long until they reach us?"

"...Forty-five."

Five minutes. They would be five minutes too late.

"Then I'll go get them," Kyros said. "Organize teams—"

"If you leave the sanctuary, you won't make it back."

He knew she was right. The wave was coming. The shields had to be sealed. Every vampire who left would die outside.

He looked at the approaching group. Looked at Selene.

"Close the gates," he said.

"Kyros—"

"Close them." He walked to the control panel. His hands were shaking. "In thirty minutes. That gives them a chance."

"It's not enough time."

"Then some of them will make it and some won't." He entered the commands. "In thirty minutes, the shields seal. If I'm not inside, seal them anyway."

He ran.

---

He reached the group in fifteen minutes. They collapsed around him—exhausted, sick, dying.

"The sanctuary," one gasped. "Can you—"

"Yes. Keep moving. Follow me."

Some couldn't walk. He carried two of them, helped others drag companions. They moved as fast as anyone could move while carrying the half-dead.

The sanctuary appeared ahead. The gates were still open. Twenty minutes.

Fifteen minutes.

Ten.

People were falling behind. Too weak, too damaged. Kyros screamed at them to keep moving, threatened, begged. Some did. Some couldn't.

Five minutes.

He reached the gates with maybe thirty vampires behind him. Inside, Selene was waiting by the controls.

"Close it," he said.

"There are still—"

"Close it!"

She sealed the gates. The shields activated—a shimmer of energy that cut off the outside world.

Outside, Kyros could hear pounding. The ones who hadn't made it. The ones who were five minutes too late.

"Let them in! Please! We're so close—"

The pounding went on for a long time. Then the collapse wave arrived—Kyros felt it hit the shields, felt the shields strain and hold—and the pounding stopped.

He stood at the gate and listened to silence.

"You saved thirty-two," Selene said quietly. "There are three hundred and forty-four of us now. It's not nothing."

"No." Kyros couldn't look at her. "It's not nothing."

He could still hear the pounding. He would hear it for the rest of his life.

## Days 4-7: The Aftermath

### Scene 6: Escaped Vampire in the Ruins

**17th-20th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Eastern Wastes**
**POV: Serath of Twilight, Wanderer**

---

Serath had been outside when the wave hit.

He survived because he was Twilight—because he'd spent decades training to minimize Bloodweb dependency, because his body could function without the network in ways other vampires couldn't. He survived the initial wave, anyway.

Now he was alone in the ruins of the world, and he wasn't sure survival counted as victory.

He walked east. He didn't know why—there was nothing there. But he couldn't stay still. Couldn't stand among the bodies and do nothing.

Bodies everywhere. In the streets, in the fields, in houses and shops and temples. Vampires who'd died where they stood when the Bloodweb inverted. Some peaceful. Some frozen in agony. Thousands. Tens of thousands. He stopped counting after the first day.

On the third day, he found another survivor.

The vampire was young—maybe a hundred years old—and he was dying. Bloodweb damage, slower than the initial wave but just as fatal. His body was shutting down.

"Help me," the young one said.

Serath looked at him. "I can't."

"Please—"

"There's nothing to do. The Bloodweb is gone. You're too dependent. Your body can't survive without it."

The young one understood. Serath saw it in his eyes—the realization that he was dead, just hadn't stopped moving yet.

"How are you...?"

"Twilight training. Fifty years of learning to survive without the network." Serath knelt beside him. "I'm sorry."

The young one died within the hour. Serath buried him because there was no one else to do it.

He kept walking.

---

On the fifth day, he reached a Twilight sanctuary. The shields were still up. He could see vampires inside—alive, moving, watching him through the barrier.

He approached. A warden met him at the gate.

"You survived outside?"

"I was trained."

The warden hesitated. Then the gate opened.

Inside, maybe three hundred vampires. The last of their kind—the survivors of a civilization that had ruled the world for a thousand years.

High Seer Selene found him that evening.

"You were in the east," she said. "What did you see?"

Serath thought about it. The bodies. The silence. The total absence of vampire civilization.

"Everything's gone," he said. "Everyone's gone."

"We prepared for this."

"Yes." Serath looked around the sanctuary—the vampires eating, resting, existing. "We prepared to survive. We didn't prepare to live."

Selene didn't answer.

That night, Serath lay in his assigned quarters and stared at the ceiling. He was alive. He was safe. And there was nothing left of everything he'd known.

*What do we do now?*

He didn't know. None of them knew. They'd survived the end of the world, and none of them had any idea what came next.

### Scene 7: Human Servant in Vel'Thoras

**14th-18th Deepwinter, Year 0 | Vel'Thoras, Servant Quarters**
**POV: Mira, Household Servant**

---

Mira was cleaning when her masters started dying.

She didn't understand at first. Lord Cassian was in his study—she heard him cry out, then a crash. She ran to help and found him on the floor, convulsing, blood streaming from his nose and ears.

"Lord Cassian—"

She reached for him. He grabbed her arm—stronger than she'd ever felt, even dying he was strong—and his eyes locked on hers.

"Run," he hissed. "Something's happening. All of us. Run."

She ran.

Outside, it was everywhere. Vampires collapsing in the streets. In the gardens. On the walkways and in the plazas. Mira had to step over bodies to keep moving. She'd served vampires her entire life, feared them, hated them, accepted them as natural as the sky.

Now they were dying around her like mayflies.

She found other servants huddled in the market square. Humans, goblins, a few dwarves—all the servant races watching their masters fall.

"What's happening?" someone asked.

No one knew.

They waited. The vampires stopped moving. The city fell silent.

Mira felt something she'd never felt before. It took her a long time to identify it.

Relief.

*They're dead. They're finally dead. We're free.*

Then the horror came. This wasn't a few vampires dying in a battle. This was all of them. Every vampire in the city, probably every vampire in the world. A civilization that had ruled for a thousand years, ended in an afternoon.

*Who's going to grow the food? Who's going to keep the systems running? Who's going to protect us from—*

From what? From each other? From whatever came next?

"What do we do?" someone asked.

No one knew that either.

---

The looting started on the second day.

Mira didn't call it looting at first—she called it "finding supplies." The vampire estates had food, medicine, tools, things servants would need to survive. Wasn't that just common sense?

She found herself in Lord Cassian's study, going through his desk. Gold coins. Jewelry. A key to a vault she'd never been allowed to enter.

She took it all.

"Mira?"

She turned. Another servant—Kel, from the kitchens—stood in the doorway.

"We're supposed to share what we find," Kel said. "The council decided."

"What council?"

"The servant council. We're organizing. Deciding how to distribute resources fairly."

*Fairly.* Mira looked at the gold in her hands. She'd served Lord Cassian for thirty years. She'd been beaten, humiliated, worked until she collapsed. And now she was supposed to share his gold fairly with people who'd never suffered in his house?

"I'll bring it to the council," she said.

She didn't.

That night, she hid the gold and the jewelry and the key. The vault had more—she'd find a way to get to it. The world was chaos now. The people who survived would be the people with resources.

She'd spent her life as property. She wasn't going to spend her freedom as a beggar.

*We're free*, she thought. *Now what?*

She looked out at the dark city—the dead vampires, the scared servants, the collapsed systems and silent machines.

*Now we survive.*

---

**Historical Note**: The immediate post-Collapse period killed nearly as many people as the Collapse itself. Infrastructure failed. Food systems broke down. Servant races who'd depended on vampire organization fell into chaos. The strong took from the weak. The weak died. It would be decades before stable societies emerged, and centuries before civilization approached vampire-era levels. The Golden Age was over. The Dark Ages had begun.
