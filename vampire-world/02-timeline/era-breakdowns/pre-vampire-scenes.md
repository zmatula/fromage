# Pre-Vampire Political Scenes

*Dramatized accounts of political events before vampire conquest*
*For historical context, see [era-pre-vampire.md](era-pre-vampire.md)*

---

## Purpose

These scenes show that pre-vampire civilizations were complex, divided, and flawed—not idealized kingdoms waiting to be conquered. The internal conflicts here made vampire conquest possible.

---

## Scene 1: The Valdrian Succession Crisis

**Year -1100 | Valdris Prime, Throne Hall**
**POV: Lord Cassian Blackwood, Council Elder**

---

The braziers cast dancing shadows across the throne hall as thirty-seven lords of Valdris waited for their king to die.

Cassian Blackwood had served three kings. He knew the signs. Aldric III's breath came in rattles now, each one a little shallower than the last. The physicians had withdrawn. The priests had said their words. All that remained was the waiting, and what came after.

Marius stood at the foot of the throne, hand resting on his sword hilt. The warrior prince. Twenty-eight years old, veteran of the Border Marches, beloved by the army. His jaw was clenched, his eyes fixed on his father's face with the intensity of a man who refused to grieve until he had permission.

Valdric stood by the window, watching the city below. The scholar prince. Twenty-five years old, trained in the Academy of Letters, fluent in six languages including elvish. His hands were clasped behind his back, and he hadn't spoken in an hour.

Two sons. One throne.

Cassian felt the weight of the vote in his chest. Thirty-seven lords, thirty-seven votes, and his was the one that would break the tie. He'd known for weeks it would come to this. Half the council wanted strength—Marius's sword arm, his soldiers' loyalty, his reputation for swift judgment. Half wanted wisdom—Valdric's learning, his diplomatic connections, his understanding of trade and law.

"Lord Blackwood."

He turned. Duke Varen had crossed the hall to stand beside him, wine cup in hand. The duke's smile never reached his eyes.

"You've been quiet tonight," Varen said.

"It's not a night for speaking."

"It will be soon." Varen's gaze drifted to the dying king. "When you cast your vote."

Cassian said nothing.

"The army follows Marius," Varen continued. "The treasury follows Valdric. An interesting choice. Swords or gold." He sipped his wine. "Of course, there's a third option."

"Is there?"

"Division. A council vote this close... some might call it a wound that won't heal. A wound that certain parties might exploit."

Cassian understood. Varen had his own designs on power. A contested succession served him better than a clear one.

"The council will vote," Cassian said. "The council will decide. That's the Aurelian way."

Varen's smile widened. "Of course, my lord. The Aurelian way."

He withdrew, leaving Cassian alone with his choice.

---

The king died at midnight. The vote came at dawn.

Cassian stood before the council, feeling every one of his fifty-six years. The faces around him were exhausted, grieving, frightened. This was how kingdoms fell—not to enemies, but to themselves.

"Speak your vote, Lord Blackwood," the Councilmaster said.

Cassian looked at Marius, who met his gaze with steady confidence. He looked at Valdric, who looked away.

*Strength or wisdom. Which does Valdris need?*

He thought of the vampire clans growing bolder in the borderlands. He thought of the Merchant Principalities' economic pressure. He thought of the Holy Confederation's missionary zeal encroaching on Valdrian villages.

Enemies everywhere. Threats on all sides.

"Marius," he said.

The warrior prince closed his eyes, just for a moment.

Valdric turned from the window. His face was composed, but his hands were fists.

"Marius of House Aurelian," the Councilmaster proclaimed, "by vote of the Council of Lords, you are named King of Valdris."

The hall erupted in the ritual shouts of acclamation. But Cassian watched Valdric's supporters—fourteen lords who'd stood with him, who believed in him, who'd now been overruled by a single vote.

They weren't shouting.

*I've saved the kingdom*, Cassian told himself. *Marius will defend us.*

But as he watched Duke Varen move to Valdric's side, speaking low and earnest in the young prince's ear, he wondered what he'd also started.

---

**Historical Note**: Duke Varen's faction became the permanent opposition to King Marius II. Their undermining of royal authority weakened Valdris throughout Marius's reign. When Vykros conquered Valdris in Year -1000, Varen's grandson opened the gates of Valdris Prime in exchange for promises of power under vampire rule.

## Scene 2: The Merchant Principalities Trade War

**Year -1080 | Council of Princes, Seahaven**
**POV: Prince-Merchant Aldric Goldwater, Lord of the Banking Houses**

---

The Council of Princes met in the Glass Rotunda, a dome of Principality crystal that let in the morning sun but kept out the sea wind. Nine princes sat around the circular table: nine cities, nine interests, nine competing ambitions.

Aldric Goldwater watched the Castellan of Portmere speak and calculated the cost of every word.

"—cannot allow Valdris to dictate terms. Their tariff on Principality grain is theft. Pure theft." The Castellan's fist struck the table. "We retaliate. Full embargo. No grain, no cloth, no iron. Nothing moves east until they crawl back to us."

Murmurs around the table. Some approving, some uncertain.

Aldric let them talk. He already knew the numbers. Portmere's granaries were full—they could afford an embargo. Seahaven's shipyards were at capacity—they'd lose contracts. Goldwater's banks held Valdrian debt—if Valdris collapsed, the debt became worthless.

The Castellan wanted war. The question was whether the other princes were stupid enough to give it to him.

"The Castellan speaks sense," the Doge of Seahaven said. Ah. The Doge was already bought. "Valdris respects only strength. We show strength, they relent."

"And if they don't?" Aldric asked.

Silence. All eyes turned to him.

"If they don't relent," Aldric continued, "we have embargo. They have army. How long before King Marius decides to take what we won't sell?"

"He wouldn't dare," the Castellan said.

"He conquered the Thornmarsh tribes last year. Burned their villages because they wouldn't pay tribute." Aldric let that settle. "What makes you think he'll treat us differently?"

The Lord of the Narrow Strait leaned forward. "The banking houses could survive a war?"

"Survive? Perhaps. Profit? No." Aldric shook his head. "I've crunched the numbers. We lose forty percent of eastern trade to embargo. We lose everything to war. And for what? A point of pride?"

"Valdris needs to learn—" the Castellan began.

"Valdris needs to learn nothing," Aldric cut him off. "Valdris needs grain. We need gold. This is commerce, not honor. There's a treaty to be made here. I can make it."

"You would have us grovel," the Castellan spat.

"I would have us *profit*. Do you know what groveling buys? Ships. Warehouses. The Portmere grain monopoly broken and distributed among all nine princes equally."

That got attention. The Castellan's monopoly had enriched Portmere at everyone else's expense for two generations. The other princes had complained but never had leverage.

"You're proposing we use Valdris to break Portmere's hold," the Lord of the Strait said slowly.

"I'm proposing we think like merchants instead of lordlings. The Castellan wants to fight because fighting keeps him powerful. I want to trade because trading makes us *all* rich."

The Castellan's face was purple. "You treasonous—"

"Vote," Aldric said. "Vote on it now. Embargo, or treaty. Let's see who wants wealth and who wants pride."

---

The vote was five to four for treaty. The Castellan stormed out. His allies followed.

After, the Lord of the Strait approached Aldric in the corridor.

"You've made an enemy," he said.

"I've made four enemies. And five allies." Aldric smiled. "That's profit."

"The Castellan won't forget this."

"No. He won't." Aldric looked out at the harbor, at the ships that meant wealth, at the trade routes that meant power. "But he'll trade. Because the alternative is ruin. And Portmere is nothing if it can't trade."

"You think this holds?"

Aldric thought about it honestly. Five to four. A single vote margin. The Castellan's faction seething, waiting for weakness. The next crisis might split the other way.

"For now," he said. "It holds for now."

---

**Historical Note**: The Principality alliance never recovered its cohesion. By Year -1010, the princes were too busy fighting each other to notice vampire economic infiltration. When vampires called in their debts, the cities fell without a single battle. Aldric Goldwater's descendants served as vampire financial administrators for a thousand years. The Castellan of Portmere's family was exterminated in Year -998 for resisting.

## Scene 3: The Holy Confederation Heresy Trial

**Year -1070 | Cathedral of Sacred Dawn, Luminara**
**POV: Sister Mariel Lightseeker, Junior Priest**

---

The accused stood in the Circle of Judgment, and Sister Mariel couldn't look away.

Scholar Aldwin Truthkeeper had taught her. Three years in his class, learning to read the old texts, learning to question and reason and seek. He'd always said the Divine Light wanted understanding, not just obedience.

Now he stood accused of heresy, and High Priest Aldric Dawnward sat in judgment.

"You wrote," the High Priest said, reading from a scroll, "that the Divine Light is 'not a deity but a force, like gravity or fire, impersonal and indifferent to human prayer.' You wrote this?"

"I wrote that the evidence suggests—"

"Yes or no, Scholar."

Aldwin's hands were steady. His voice was clear. "Yes. I wrote it."

Murmurs through the cathedral. Five hundred priests and acolytes, gathered to witness judgment. Mariel felt her neighbor's elbow press against her arm in disapproval.

"You understand," the High Priest said, "that this contradicts sacred doctrine? That the Divine Light is a loving god who intervenes in human affairs?"

"The doctrine, Your Holiness, is interpretation. I am proposing alternative interpretation based on observed phenomena. Prayer does not correlate with outcomes. Miracles follow no pattern consistent with a conscious will. The Light responds to ritual in predictable ways, like—"

"Like a tool," the High Priest interrupted. "Like a machine."

"Yes."

More murmurs. Angrier now.

"And if the Light is a tool," the High Priest continued, "then it can be used. Without worship. Without faith. Without the Church."

Mariel saw it then—the fear behind the accusation. If Aldwin was right, the Church became unnecessary. Prayer became theater. The whole structure of holy authority collapsed.

*He's not being tried for error*, she realized. *He's being tried for danger.*

The defender stepped forward—Father Cornelius, assigned to speak for Aldwin even though he clearly didn't want to.

"The Scholar may be wrong," Cornelius said carefully, "but error is not heresy. He sought truth in his study. He followed evidence where it led. Is that not what the Divine Light teaches? That we should seek understanding?"

"We should seek understanding *within faith*," the High Priest said. "Understanding that strengthens. Not understanding that destroys."

Cornelius bowed his head. He had nothing else.

Mariel found herself standing before she knew she meant to speak.

"Your Holiness."

Silence. All eyes on her. *What am I doing?*

"The junior priest wishes to speak?" The High Priest's tone was ice.

"Scholar Aldwin..." She swallowed. "He taught me. He taught me to read the sacred texts more carefully than I ever had. To understand their deeper meanings. If his understanding was flawed, it was flawed in the service of understanding better. Doesn't that count for something?"

"It counts for why his error is so dangerous." The High Priest's gaze was not unkind. "A fool who stumbles serves as warning. A brilliant man who wanders leads others into darkness." He looked at Aldwin. "You led students. You published your work. How many have you corrupted?"

Aldwin looked at Mariel. For a moment, something passed between them—sorrow, maybe, or apology.

"I taught them to think," he said. "If that's corruption, I'm guilty."

---

The verdict was unanimous. Heresy. The sentence was death.

Mariel stood in the crowd as Aldwin was led away. He walked with his shoulders straight, and he didn't look back.

Beside her, Father Cornelius spoke quietly.

"He was probably wrong, you know. The Light has consciousness. I've felt it."

"Have you?" Mariel asked. "Or have you felt what you expected to feel?"

Cornelius looked at her sharply. "Be careful, Sister. You saw what happens to those who question."

She had seen. And she would be careful.

But she would never stop wondering if Aldwin had been right.

---

**Historical Note**: The Dual Nature Heresy was brutally suppressed. Dozens of scholars were executed. Thousands of texts were burned. When vampires conquered the Confederation, they found a population trained to accept dogma without question—perfect subjects. Sister Mariel survived to become Mother Superior of a small convent, where she quietly preserved Aldwin's writings. Her successor destroyed them after Mariel's death, fearing discovery.

## Scene 4: The Elven Moonspire Council

**Year -1060 | Moonspire Throne Hall, Northern Reaches**
**POV: War-Leader Aelindra Starweaver, Commander of the Eastern Reaches**

---

King Vaelin stood before the war map, and Aelindra watched him pretend to consider options he'd already rejected.

She knew her king. Brave, honorable, stubborn as stone. He'd called this council to hear advice, but he'd hear only what confirmed his instincts. The rest would wash over him like rain on granite.

"The vampire clans grow," Vaelin said. "My scouts count sixty thousand now. Perhaps more."

"Then we kill them," War-Leader Caelthor said. Predictable. Caelthor's answer to everything was killing.

"The humans count them too," Elder Silvermoon said. "The Holy Confederation has launched purification campaigns. Let them spend their blood."

"And when they fail?" Aelindra asked. "When the vampires grow stronger on human dead? What then?"

Silvermoon's lip curled. "You propose we bleed for humans? They burned Thornvale. Murdered three hundred of our people. They are not worth elvish lives."

"I propose we bleed for *ourselves*." Aelindra stepped forward to the map. "The vampires aren't choosing between us and humans. They're conquering everyone. If we don't help the humans stop them now, we fight them alone later. And we lose."

"Elves don't lose to—"

"Moonspire is seven thousand warriors. The humans number millions. If vampires conquer them, vampires have millions of slaves to throw at us. Do the arithmetic, Silvermoon."

Silence. Even Caelthor looked thoughtful.

Vaelin studied the map. "What do you propose?"

"Alliance. Real alliance, not diplomatic words. Joint command. Human armies with elvish archers and mages. Coordinate strikes on vampire strongholds. Burn them out before they consolidate."

"Humans are unreliable," Silvermoon said.

"So are we, apparently." Aelindra turned to face him. "Or have we forgotten that Lord Thornweald of Silverwood has been meeting with vampire envoys?"

That landed. Shock on several faces. Vaelin's eyes narrowed.

"Silverwood affairs are not our concern," Silvermoon said carefully.

"They will be when Thornweald sells us all to save himself."

"You speak of things you cannot prove."

"I speak of things I *know*." Aelindra held his gaze. "Do you, Elder? Do you know what Silverwood plans? Or are you guessing and hoping, like the rest of us?"

Silvermoon looked away.

Vaelin raised a hand. "Enough. We vote on the alliance."

The vote was eleven to nine in favor. A slim margin, but enough.

Aelindra allowed herself a moment of relief. It was a start. With human numbers and elvish skill combined—

"The alliance will be proposed," Vaelin said. "But not commanded. We will *offer* to coordinate. If humans wish our help, they may ask for it."

"Offer?" Aelindra stared at him. "My king, we need to *lead* this alliance, not participate in it. We need—"

"We are elves." Vaelin's voice was final. "We do not beg. We offer. They may accept or not as they choose. The council has voted. The matter is closed."

---

After, Aelindra walked the battlements alone. The forest stretched below her, ancient and beautiful and utterly vulnerable.

*Eleven to nine*, she thought. *Barely enough. And even then, we do it half-hearted. An offer, not a commitment.*

She knew how humans would see it. Elvish pride. Elvish arrogance. "They offer but do not commit. They put conditions on survival."

The alliance would be proposed. The alliance would be declined. And when the vampires came, Moonspire would fight alone.

She thought of her daughter, ten years old, training with her first bow.

*We had a chance*, she thought. *And we wasted it on pride.*

---

**Historical Note**: The alliance was proposed. It was declined. Human King Marius II called the elvish offer "an insult wrapped in condescension." Moonspire was conquered in Year -1020. King Taelin was executed, and the population enslaved. Aelindra Starweaver died defending the last fortress. Her daughter was captured and served in vampire households for three hundred years before dying in the Collapse.

## Scene 5: The Dwarven Succession Dispute

**Year -1055 | Great Hall of Ironpeak, Clan Council Chamber**
**POV: Thane Borin Ironhand, Heir to the High King**

---

Borin had his father's axe in his hands and his father's crown across the hall, and between them stood his cousin Krag.

"The Council will decide," Clan-Mother Grimstone said. She was older than both of them and patient as granite. "Present your claims."

Borin stepped forward first. By right of blood, he should speak first. By right of blood, he should be king.

"I am Borin, son of Borin Ironhand, blood of the unbroken line. My father united the Confederation. His father forged the Great Alliance. Seven generations of Ironhand kings have ruled Ironpeak. I claim by blood and tradition."

Murmurs of approval from Ironhand supporters. Five of the seven clan seats.

Krag stepped forward. He was broader than Borin, battle-scarred, famous for the defense of Deep Gate against goblin invasion.

"I am Krag Stonebreaker, nephew of Borin Ironhand, hero of Deep Gate. I claim not by blood but by merit. The High King must be strong. Must be tested. I ask the Council: what has Borin tested himself against? What battles has he won? What threats has he broken?"

Borin felt heat rise in his face. "I have trained—"

"Trained. Not fought." Krag's smile was not kind. "Dwarves need a king who has stood in blood and prevailed. Not one who has read about it."

"Reading—" Borin caught himself. *Don't defend scholarship. He'll make you sound weak.* "The High King is more than a warlord. He maintains alliances. Manages trade. Interprets law. These require study."

"Which is why High Kings have advisors." Krag spread his hands. "I'll have scholars. I'll listen to them. But when enemies come—vampires, goblins, elves—the king must lead from front. Can you do that, cousin? Can you stand in the shield wall and not flinch?"

Borin knew the answer. He'd never been tested. He didn't know.

"Can you read a treaty and know when you're being cheated?" he said instead. "Can you calculate tribute and know fair from extortion? Can you negotiate with elven envoys without starting another war?"

"I'll learn."

"So will I."

They stared at each other. Cousin against cousin. Blood against blood.

Clan-Mother Grimstone raised her hand. "The claims are heard. The Council will vote."

---

The vote went through the night. Ironhand voted for Borin. Stonebreaker voted for Krag. The other five clans deliberated, argued, threatened.

In the end, it was Goldbeard—the richest clan, always mercenary—that broke the tie.

"Stonebreaker," their Thane said. "We need strength."

Krag bowed his head in acceptance. Borin felt the crown slip away.

*Not by much*, he thought. *Four to three. One vote.*

Krag crossed to him. "Cousin. I need you."

"Do you."

"The Council is divided. Your supporters will be my enemies if I make them. I want them to be my allies." He extended his hand. "Serve as my advisor. Help me with the treaties and the tributes. We'll rule together."

Borin looked at the offered hand. The smart move was to accept. Heal the breach. Present unity.

The proud move was to refuse. Return to his hold. Nurse the grievance.

He thought of the Confederation. Of vampires growing in the dark. Of elves and humans who would probe any weakness.

"Together," he said, and clasped Krag's hand.

---

They tried. For thirty-five years, they tried.

But Krag never listened about the elven treaty. He led them into the Third Elf-Dwarf War and lost. He never understood vampire economics and let them infiltrate dwarven trade. He never trusted scholars and made decisions on instinct that a moment's research would have prevented.

And Borin, who saw these failures, who warned against them, who was proven right again and again—Borin was still the advisor, not the king.

When Krag died in Year -1030, Borin finally took the crown. He was sixty-one years old, and the vampires were at the gates.

He had six years to undo thirty-five years of damage.

It wasn't enough.

---

**Historical Note**: High King Borin II sealed the deep holds and preserved what he could. The dwarves survived the vampire era better than most races—but only as craftsmen and laborers, not as rulers. Borin died in Year -990, the last king to hold the title with real meaning. His descendants became vampire administrators, and the succession disputes that weakened the Confederation were replayed in lesser form for a thousand years.

*These five scenes show the world before vampires—not a golden age, but a fractured one. Every internal conflict, every petty rivalry, every moment of pride over pragmatism contributed to the catastrophe that followed. The vampires didn't conquer a united world. They conquered a divided one, piece by piece, using the divisions against themselves.*
