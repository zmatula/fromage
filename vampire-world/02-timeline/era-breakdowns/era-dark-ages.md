# Era: The Dark Ages (Year 50 to 300)

Two and a half centuries of struggle, loss, and slow recovery. The knowledge of the vampire age faded into legend while new civilizations fought to survive.

---

## Era Overview

**Duration**: 250 years
**Character**: Decline, superstition, warfare, gradual recovery
**Dominant Power**: Shifting regional powers; no hegemony
**Key Development**: Loss of vampire-era knowledge; new cultures crystallize
**End State**: Stable kingdoms ready for expansion; vampires are myth

---

## Characteristics of the Dark Ages

### Knowledge Loss

**What Was Lost (Year 50-150)**:
- Last vampire-language readers died
- Magical techniques degraded without texts
- Engineering knowledge reduced to basics
- Medical arts regressed to herbalism
- Historical records destroyed or indecipherable

**Why It Couldn't Be Recovered**:
- Active destruction continued (religious, political)
- No one could read vampire script
- Context for understanding was lost
- Fear of "tainted" knowledge
- Practical survival prioritized over scholarship

**What Survived**:
- Dwarven technical fragments
- Basic magical traditions
- Oral histories (distorted)
- Artifacts (without manuals)

### Living Conditions

**Population**:
- Year 50: ~15 million total (all races)
- Year 150: ~18 million (slow growth)
- Year 300: ~25 million (accelerating)

**Life Expectancy**:
- Humans: 35-45 years
- Elves: 200-400 years (reduced from vampire era)
- Dwarves: 150-250 years
- Goblins: 25-40 years

**Technology Level**:
- Iron Age equivalent
- No magical infrastructure
- Limited literacy
- Local production only

### Religious Dominance

**Church of Divine Light**:
- Dominant human religion by Year 100
- Controlled education where it existed
- Defined vampires as demons
- Restricted "dangerous" knowledge
- Justified racial hierarchies

**Counter-Traditions**:
- Mystery schools (secret knowledge preservation)
- Shamanic traditions (goblins, some humans)
- Nature worship (elves)
- Ancestor cults (dwarves, some humans)

## Failed Kingdoms of the Dark Ages

The period from Year 50 to 300 saw dozens of would-be kingdoms rise and fall. These five illustrate the challenges of the era—and why only certain political structures survived.

---

### The Kingdom of Tharos (Year 48 to Year 135)

**Location**: Eastern coastal region (later absorbed by Valdris)
**Founder**: General Tharos Ironfist, former collaborator

**Rise (Year 48-75)**: Tharos was a human general who had served vampires as a military administrator. He used his organizational knowledge to create the first post-collapse standing army. By Year 60, he controlled the entire eastern coast and threatened Valdris's eastern borders.

**Peak (Year 75-110)**: Under Tharos and his son Tharos II, the kingdom became the most militarily advanced human state. They adopted vampire-style logistics, maintained discipline that others couldn't match, and collected military artifacts. Tharos II defeated three Valdrian invasions.

**Fatal Flaw**: The Tharos dynasty's collaborator origins haunted them. They could conquer but couldn't build legitimacy. Every generation faced assassination attempts. The Church denounced them as "blood-servants" who perpetuated vampire systems.

**Fall (Year 110-135)**: Tharos III died without heirs in Year 110. His five generals divided the kingdom. Civil war lasted 25 years. Valdris picked off pieces until nothing remained. By Year 135, "Tharos" was a cautionary tale about building power without legitimacy.

**Legacy**: Valdrian military still uses Tharosian formations. The artifacts they collected are now in the Valdrian Crown Vault. The term "Tharosian" means "effective but tainted."

---

### The Sanctum of Purity (Year 88 to Year 175)

**Location**: Central hill country (later became core of Sanctified Realm)
**Founder**: High Priest Aldric the Purifier

**Rise (Year 88-120)**: After the Church of Divine Light declared vampires officially extinct (Year 100), Aldric led a faction that wanted to go further—not just defeat vampires but purify the world of all their influence. He established the Sanctum as a theocratic state where vampire artifacts were destroyed, vampire languages forbidden, and collaborator bloodlines tracked and marked.

**Peak (Year 120-150)**: The Sanctum attracted true believers from across human lands. At its height, it controlled the entire central region and demanded tribute from neighbors. Aldric's successor, High Priest Solara II, was considered a living saint. They burned thousands of artifacts and killed hundreds of suspected "vampire-touched."

**Fatal Flaw**: Purity movements consume themselves. Aldric had purged collaborators. His successors purged those who had known collaborators. Eventually they were purging anyone who had read a vampire text or touched an artifact. By Year 160, half the leadership had been declared impure.

**Fall (Year 150-175)**: The Sanctum split into three factions over doctrinal purity (Year 150). Each declared the others heretical. Civil war and mutual purges followed. The moderate faction survived and became the core of the Sanctified Realm—but only by abandoning the extreme purity doctrine that had founded the state.

**Legacy**: The Sanctified Realm inherited the Sanctum's territory but not its extremism. The term "Sanctum Purge" means self-destructive orthodoxy. Many artifacts secretly survived because even true believers couldn't bring themselves to destroy everything.

---

### The Elvish Resurgence League (Year 62 to Year 205)

**Location**: Scattered forest territories
**Founder**: Speaker Thandril Starweave (no relation to present-day Thandril)

**Rise (Year 62-120)**: After the collapse, many elves wanted to rebuild their pre-vampire domains. Speaker Thandril united twelve forest communities into the League, claiming they would restore "the glory of the First Woods" that had existed before vampire conquest.

**Peak (Year 120-165)**: The League controlled substantial forest territory and maintained internal autonomy while humans fought each other. They recovered significant elvish pre-vampire knowledge and artifacts. They refused all human contact except through designated traders.

**Fatal Flaw**: The League's founding myth was false. There had been no unified "First Woods"—pre-vampire elves had been as divided as humans. The League suppressed this truth, but it created fractures. Different communities remembered different "traditions" that couldn't all be authentic.

**Fall (Year 165-205)**: The Great Plague hit elves less hard than humans but still killed 10% of League population. This triggered a succession crisis as different communities blamed each other for "impurity." By Year 200, the League had fragmented. Silverwood isolated itself completely (Year 205). The other communities either assimilated with humans or became the scattered enclaves that persist to Year 1200.

**Legacy**: Silverwood's isolation stems from the League's failure—they decided separation was the only way to preserve elvish culture. Urban elves descend from League members who chose integration instead.

---

### The Goblin Unity Empire (Year 78 to Year 190)

**Location**: Southern swamplands and badlands
**Founder**: War-Chief Skrall Bloodfist

**Rise (Year 78-130)**: Goblins emerged from the collapse as the most traumatized race—used as experimental subjects and breeding stock, their culture erased. Skrall united the tribes by channeling this rage into a conquest ideology. "Unity" meant all goblins together, preparing to take what had been taken from them.

**Peak (Year 130-168)**: At maximum extent, the Unity Empire controlled all southern swamps and badlands and raided as far north as Valdrian borders. They had numbers—goblins bred fast and had millions of warriors. They recovered goblin artifacts specifically designed to enhance breeding and combat.

**Fatal Flaw**: The Empire was built on hate, which is exhausting. Every generation was raised to remember the vampire atrocities. But hate couldn't build farms, educate children, or create anything lasting. The Empire was always at war—with humans, with elves, and eventually with itself over who was a "true" goblin.

**Fall (Year 168-190)**: The Great Plague killed 30% of goblins—far worse than other races because of crowded conditions and poor sanitation. In the chaos, Skrall III was assassinated by a rival faction. Civil war followed. By Year 190, the Unity Empire had collapsed into the Broken Lands—the same territory but no longer unified.

**Legacy**: Goblin society never unified again. The tribes maintain the rage but not the organization. The term "Unity" among goblins now means a failed promise.

---

### The First Dwarven Confederation (Year 52 to Year 145)

**Location**: Mountain holds and undercities
**Founder**: High King Borin III (elected Year 52)

**Rise (Year 52-100)**: The dwarves attempted to re-establish their pre-vampire high kingdom system. Borin III was elected by the hold council as the first post-collapse High King. He tried to unify the holds under central authority, standardize trade, and coordinate artifact recovery.

**Peak (Year 100-130)**: The Confederation controlled all major holds and regulated all dwarven external trade. Borin III's successor, High King Thrain, expanded to reclaim abandoned holds. Dwarven engineering began recovering—they were the first race to restore magical metallurgy.

**Fatal Flaw**: The dwarven holds had never accepted real central authority. The High King was traditionally ceremonial. When Thrain tried to actually command the holds—taxing them, directing their engineering projects, controlling their artifact finds—they resisted.

**Fall (Year 130-145)**: The succession dispute of Year 145 was actually a constitutional crisis. Two candidates both had valid claims, but the real question was how much power the winner would have. When no compromise could be reached, the Confederation split. The Ironpeak holds became the Ironpeak Confederation—still calling themselves a Confederation but actually just an alliance.

**Legacy**: Dwarves never again tried to centralize. The "High King" title now exists only in the Ironpeak Confederation and is largely ceremonial. Individual holds guard their independence fiercely.

## The Great Plague (Year 168)

The most devastating single event of the Dark Ages, killing 20% of the total population and reshaping political geography for centuries.

### Origins (Disputed)

**Orthodox Church Position**: Divine punishment for recovering vampire artifacts. The plague began in ruin-explorer communities.

**Scholarly Consensus**: A natural pandemic, possibly from animals in collapsed vampire breeding facilities. Similar to diseases that appeared periodically in late vampire era.

**Conspiracy Theories**:
- Vampire survivor biological weapon
- Artifact contamination gone airborne
- Deliberate release by faction seeking to reduce competitor populations
- Goblin blood magic curse

**What's Known**: The plague appeared simultaneously in three coastal Free Cities in early Year 168. It spread inland along trade routes. Symptoms included fever, blood-coughing, and skin lesions. Death occurred within 8-12 days. No magical or herbal treatment was effective.

### Mortality by Race

| Race | Population (Year 167) | Deaths | Percentage | Notes |
|------|----------------------|--------|------------|-------|
| Humans | ~12 million | ~2.4 million | 20% | Worst in cities |
| Elves | ~3 million | ~300,000 | 10% | Forest isolation helped |
| Dwarves | ~2.5 million | ~250,000 | 10% | Sealed holds survived |
| Goblins | ~4 million | ~1.2 million | 30% | Worst conditions |
| **Total** | ~21.5 million | ~4.15 million | ~19% | |

### Consequence Chain

**Immediate (Year 168-170)**:
- Trade collapse as cities closed borders
- Food shortages from abandoned farms
- Breakdown of political authority in worst-hit regions
- Mass movement of refugees spreading plague further
- Scapegoating and pogroms against goblins, "collaborator" descendants

**Short-Term (Year 170-180)**:
- Power vacuums in human kingdoms as noble families died out
- Rise of new nobility from survivors who took vacant lands
- Church authority increases as people seek divine explanation
- Major realm boundaries shift as some regions depopulated
- Goblin Unity Empire fatally weakened; collapse begins

**Medium-Term (Year 180-220)**:
- Valdris expands into depopulated eastern territories
- Sanctified Realm absorbs weakened central kingdoms
- New merchant class emerges from those who kept trade flowing
- Scholarly tradition begins as survivors ask "why?"
- "Plague marriages" as survivors consolidate wealth/property

**Long-Term (to Year 300)**:
- Religious schism (Year 350) has roots in plague-era theodicy debates
- "Plague nobility" families still prominent in Year 1200
- Goblin population never recovered proportionally—permanently weakened
- Suspicion of goblins as plague carriers persists
- Trade regulations permanently stricter about disease control

### Political Restructuring

**Winners**:
- **Valdris Kingdom**: Absorbed depopulated Tharos remnants and eastern territories; gained buffer regions
- **Duke Aldric of Northern Marches**: United depleted northern duchies under his leadership (Year 235)
- **Free Cities League**: Survived through maritime trade and rebuilt faster than land-based economies

**Losers**:
- **Goblin Unity Empire**: Fatally weakened; collapsed by Year 190
- **Elvish Resurgence League**: Internal disputes over "impurity" led to fragmentation
- **Small inland kingdoms**: Many simply ceased to exist; land reverted to wilderness

### Named Figures

**Healer Margret of the Roads**: Human woman who organized plague hospitals along trade routes. Treated all races including goblins. Canonized by Church Year 220 despite controversy over goblin patients.

**Speaker Thandril Starweave**: League leader whose daughter died of plague. His grief turned to paranoia about racial purity, beginning the League's fatal fragmentation.

**War-Chief Skrall III**: Assassinated during plague chaos; his death ended any hope of goblin recovery.

**High Priest Solara II**: Sanctum leader who declared plague was punishment for insufficient purification. His extremism accelerated the Sanctum's self-destruction.

## Key Developments

### Formation of Secret Societies

**The Veil Keepers** (founded ~Year 100)
- Believe vampires/dimensional threats still exist
- Seek to prevent return
- Destroy or hide dangerous artifacts
- Small, paranoid, effective

**The Inheritors** (founded ~Year 180)
- Seek to recover vampire knowledge
- Believe it can elevate current races
- Collect and study artifacts
- Wealthy, scholarly, secretive

**The Order of the Dawn** (founded ~Year 220)
- Militant anti-vampire organization
- Believes vampires will return
- Hunt for survivors
- Religious, military

**Others**:
- Merchant guilds with secret purposes
- Royal intelligence services
- Criminal organizations trading in artifacts

### Magical Tradition Development

**By Year 300, magic has:**
- Fragmented into distinct schools
- Lost most vampire-era power
- Become rare but real
- Developed local variations
- Become politically sensitive

**Schools emerging:**
- Elemental traditions
- Healing arts
- Divination
- Enchantment (limited)
- Blood magic (forbidden, hidden)

### Artifact Economy

**Value Chain**:
1. Ruin exploration (dangerous)
2. Artifact recovery (valuable)
3. Identification (scholarly)
4. Sale/Trade (profitable)

## The Vampire Question

### By Year 300

**Official Position**: Vampires are extinct; they were demons destroyed by divine intervention; their works are dangerous but valuable

**Scholarly Position**: Vampires were a powerful race that destroyed themselves; their works are worthy of study; some artifacts are beneficial

**Popular Belief**: Vampires were monsters who drank blood and enslaved the ancestors; they might return; their works are cursed

**Secret Society Positions**:
- Veil Keepers: Vampires may not all be dead; they must never return
- Inheritors: Vampire knowledge is supreme; it should be recovered and used
- Order of Dawn: Vampires will return; we must be ready

**The Truth**: Perhaps 1,000-2,000 vampires survive in stasis, hiding, or isolation. None interact with the surface world. The protagonist remains in stasis in Vel'Thoras. Twilight sanctuaries may contain communities. Some have changed into something else. The world has forgotten them, but they have not ceased to exist.
