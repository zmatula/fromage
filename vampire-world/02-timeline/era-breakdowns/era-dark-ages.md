# Era: The Dark Ages (Year 50 to 300)

Two and a half centuries of struggle, loss, and slow recovery. The knowledge of the vampire age faded into legend while new civilizations fought to survive.

---

## Era Overview

**Duration**: 250 years
**Character**: Decline, superstition, warfare, gradual recovery
**Dominant Power**: Shifting regional powers; no hegemony
**Key Development**: Loss of vampire-era knowledge; new cultures crystallize
**End State**: Stable kingdoms ready for expansion; vampires are myth

---

## Characteristics of the Dark Ages

### Knowledge Loss

**What Was Lost (Year 50-150)**:
- Last vampire-language readers died
- Magical techniques degraded without texts
- Engineering knowledge reduced to basics
- Medical arts regressed to herbalism
- Historical records destroyed or indecipherable

**Why It Couldn't Be Recovered**:
- Active destruction continued (religious, political)
- No one could read vampire script
- Context for understanding was lost
- Fear of "tainted" knowledge
- Practical survival prioritized over scholarship

**What Survived**:
- Dwarven technical fragments
- Basic magical traditions
- Oral histories (distorted)
- Artifacts (without manuals)

### Living Conditions

**Population**:
- Year 50: ~15 million total (all races)
- Year 150: ~18 million (slow growth)
- Year 300: ~25 million (accelerating)

**Life Expectancy**:
- Humans: 35-45 years
- Elves: 200-400 years (reduced from vampire era)
- Dwarves: 150-250 years
- Goblins: 25-40 years

**Technology Level**:
- Iron Age equivalent
- No magical infrastructure
- Limited literacy
- Local production only

### Religious Dominance

**Church of Divine Light**:
- Dominant human religion by Year 100
- Controlled education where it existed
- Defined vampires as demons
- Restricted "dangerous" knowledge
- Justified racial hierarchies

**Counter-Traditions**:
- Mystery schools (secret knowledge preservation)
- Shamanic traditions (goblins, some humans)
- Nature worship (elves)
- Ancestor cults (dwarves, some humans)

## Century-by-Century Breakdown

### First Dark Century (Year 50-150)

**Political Developments**:
- Year 55-70: First War of Succession in Valdris
- Year 72: Sanctified Realm absorbs three smaller kingdoms
- Year 85: Goblin-Human War in southern territories
- Year 95: Elven-Human border conflicts intensify
- Year 110: Northern Marches fragment into rival duchies
- Year 130: Free Cities League civil war
- Year 145: Dwarven succession crisis; High King election disputed

**Cultural Developments**:
- Year 60: Last fluent vampire-script reader dies
- Year 75: Church of Divine Light declares vampire artifacts "demonic"
- Year 90: First "History of the Liberation" written (heavily biased)
- Year 100: Vampires officially declared extinct by Church
- Year 120: Goblin shamanic traditions driven underground
- Year 140: Mystery schools begin codifying preserved knowledge

**Artifact Events**:
- Year 52: First major artifact activation (accident; 200 dead)
- Year 68: Valdris royal family acquires collection
- Year 88: Artifact causes plague in Free City (magical contamination)
- Year 105: Church demands artifact destruction; mostly ignored
- Year 125: First artifact dealer guild formed
- Year 148: Artifact found that still functions; major political crisis

**Vampire Survivor Status**:
- Fewer than 5,000 estimated
- Almost all in deep hiding or stasis
- Occasional sightings dismissed as superstition
- Last public vampire killing: Year 87

### Second Dark Century (Year 150-250)

**Political Developments**:
- Year 155: Valdris Kingdom reaches maximum early extent
- Year 168: Great Plague reduces populations by 20%
- Year 175: Sanctified Realm internal schism
- Year 190: Goblin Great Migration (pushed from southern territories)
- Year 205: Elven Silverwood closes borders completely
- Year 220: Free Cities League reforms after civil war
- Year 235: Northern Marches reunified under Duke Aldric
- Year 248: Dwarven-Human alliance against goblin raiders

**Cultural Developments**:
- Year 160: First generation with no living memory of vampires
- Year 175: "Vampire" becomes generic term for monster/demon
- Year 188: Scholarly tradition begins among coastal humans
- Year 200: Artistic renaissance in Free Cities
- Year 225: Dwarven engineering revival
- Year 240: First systematic artifact study (secret; Free Cities)

**Artifact Events**:
- Year 158: Major ruin entrance discovered; expedition lost
- Year 172: Functioning artifact used in battle; decisive victory
- Year 195: Artifact heals plague survivors; creates cult following
- Year 210: Secret society forms around artifact collection
- Year 230: Ruin discovered with intact library (unreadable)
- Year 245: Artifact trade becomes major economic factor

**Vampire Survivor Status**:
- Fewer than 2,000 estimated
- No confirmed sightings in decades
- Completely mythologized
- Some may be waking from long stasis cycles

### Third Dark Century (Year 250-300)

**Political Developments**:
- Year 255: Alliance system develops between kingdoms
- Year 268: Religious wars between Church factions
- Year 275: Goblin territories consolidated under new leadership
- Year 285: Elven-Human treaty (first formal recognition)
- Year 292: Major border war reshapes northern territories
- Year 298: Period of relative peace; trade expansion

**Cultural Developments**:
- Year 258: First university founded (Free Cities)
- Year 270: Standardized calendar adopted
- Year 282: Historical society begins collecting records
- Year 290: Literary tradition flourishes
- Year 295: Religious reform movement in Church
- Year 300: "Modern" era considered to begin

**Artifact Events**:
- Year 252: Map to major ruin discovered; rush begins
- Year 265: Artifact collection becomes royal hobby
- Year 278: First successful translation of vampire text fragment
- Year 288: Major ruin expedition returns with treasures
- Year 295: Artifact guild gains political recognition
- Year 300: Systematic ruin exploration begins

## Major Powers (Year 300)

### Human Kingdoms

**Valdris Kingdom**
- Strongest single human power
- Eastern territories
- Royal artifact collection
- Supports limited scholarship

**Sanctified Realm**
- Theocratic government
- Central-southern territories
- Church of Divine Light headquarters
- Anti-magic, anti-artifact officially (secretly collects)

**Free Cities League**
- Merchant republic federation
- Coastal territories
- Wealthiest region
- Most tolerant of scholarship and magic

**Northern Marches**
- Military aristocracy
- Northern territories (former Throne lands)
- Rich in ruins
- Warrior culture

**Numerous smaller kingdoms and duchies**

### Dwarven Holds

**Ironpeak Confederation**
- Dominant dwarven power
- Controls most mountains
- Industrial base for region
- Conservative, isolationist

**Other holds**:
- Deep Throne Remnant (ancient, mysterious)
- Surface dwarf communities (integrated with humans)

### Elven Domains

**Silverwood Domain**
- Largest elven territory
- Completely isolationist since Year 205
- Rumored to preserve vampire-era knowledge
- Mysterious to outsiders

**Scattered enclaves**:
- Urban elves in human cities
- Wild elf tribes
- Scholar communities

### Goblin Territories

**Broken Lands Confederation**
- Former Great Swamp Dominion reorganized
- Marginal territories
- Constant raiding conflicts
- Underestimated by others

## Key Developments

### Formation of Secret Societies

**The Veil Keepers** (founded ~Year 100)
- Believe vampires/dimensional threats still exist
- Seek to prevent return
- Destroy or hide dangerous artifacts
- Small, paranoid, effective

**The Inheritors** (founded ~Year 180)
- Seek to recover vampire knowledge
- Believe it can elevate current races
- Collect and study artifacts
- Wealthy, scholarly, secretive

**The Order of the Dawn** (founded ~Year 220)
- Militant anti-vampire organization
- Believes vampires will return
- Hunt for survivors
- Religious, military

**Others**:
- Merchant guilds with secret purposes
- Royal intelligence services
- Criminal organizations trading in artifacts

### Magical Tradition Development

**By Year 300, magic has:**
- Fragmented into distinct schools
- Lost most vampire-era power
- Become rare but real
- Developed local variations
- Become politically sensitive

**Schools emerging:**
- Elemental traditions
- Healing arts
- Divination
- Enchantment (limited)
- Blood magic (forbidden, hidden)

### Artifact Economy

**Value Chain**:
1. Ruin exploration (dangerous)
2. Artifact recovery (valuable)
3. Identification (scholarly)
4. Sale/Trade (profitable)
5. Collection/Use (political power)

**Effects**:
- Created wealth outside traditional aristocracy
- Funded scholarship
- Drove exploration
- Created conflicts
- Kept vampire legacy relevant

## The Vampire Question

### By Year 300

**Official Position**: Vampires are extinct; they were demons destroyed by divine intervention; their works are dangerous but valuable

**Scholarly Position**: Vampires were a powerful race that destroyed themselves; their works are worthy of study; some artifacts are beneficial

**Popular Belief**: Vampires were monsters who drank blood and enslaved the ancestors; they might return; their works are cursed

**Secret Society Positions**:
- Veil Keepers: Vampires may not all be dead; they must never return
- Inheritors: Vampire knowledge is supreme; it should be recovered and used
- Order of Dawn: Vampires will return; we must be ready

**The Truth**: Perhaps 1,000-2,000 vampires survive in stasis, hiding, or isolation. None interact with the surface world. The protagonist remains in stasis in Vel'Thoras. Twilight sanctuaries may contain communities. Some have changed into something else. The world has forgotten them, but they have not ceased to exist.
