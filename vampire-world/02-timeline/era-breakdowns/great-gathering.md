# The Great Gathering

*Year -1050 | The Last Free Goblin Council*
*The moment that sealed goblin fate for two thousand years*

---

## Part 1: The Decision

### Scene 1: Shaman-King Grist Blood-Eye's Council

**Year -1051 | Blood Hollow, Great Swamp Confederation**
**POV: Grist Blood-Eye, Shaman-King**

---

The spirits were silent. That was what frightened Grist most.

He sat in the hollow tree that had served shamans for nine generations, blood-smoke curling around him, and called to the ancestors. Nothing answered. He called to the swamp spirits. Nothing. He called to the old powers, the ones with names he was forbidden to speak aloud.

Silence.

In forty years as Shaman-King, Grist had never known the spirits to abandon him. They warned of floods and droughts, of goblin-hunts and disease. They guided his people through the dark. And now, when he needed them most, they were gone.

*Or hiding*, he thought. *Even spirits fear what comes.*

He emerged from the hollow to find his council waiting.

Twelve war-chiefs, their skin painted for battle though no battle had been called. Beside them, the clan-mothers, old beyond reckoning, keepers of birth-lore and death-rite. And standing apart, seven shamans from the allied tribes, watching him with eyes that knew what the silence meant.

"Well?" War-Chief Skag Tooth-Keeper demanded. "What do the spirits say?"

Grist looked at each of them. These were the leaders of the Great Swamp Confederation, the largest goblin nation in the world. Sixty thousand warriors. Three hundred thousand goblins in all.

It wasn't enough.

"The spirits are silent," he said. "They fear to speak of what comes."

Murmurs. Fear. Skag's face twisted.

"Then we fight without them. We've fought without spirits before."

"Against goblin-hunters. Against human raiders." Grist shook his head. "Not against this."

He gestured, and a young runner brought forward the scout's report. Grist unrolled it, though he knew its contents by heart.

"Vampire clans in the east: eighty thousand. More coming. They've begun taking human cities. Enslaving populations. Building toward something—the scouts don't know what. But the vampires are organized now. They don't just hunt. They *rule*."

"Then we fight harder," Skag said.

"We lose. Numbers don't lie, Skag. Sixty thousand against eighty thousand, and theirs are faster, stronger, and they don't die when you wound them." Grist rolled up the report. "I've run the calculations. In open battle, we break in an hour. In guerrilla war, we last maybe two years. Either way, we die."

"Then what?" Clan-Mother Vrix spoke now, ancient, wizened, voice like rustling leaves. "If fighting is death and running is death, what remains?"

Grist had thought about this for days. Prayed for another answer. Received none.

"Alliance," he said.

The silence that followed was worse than the spirits'.

"You mean beg," Skag said finally.

"I mean *ask*. We ask humans to fight with us. Elves. Dwarves. All of us together against the vampires."

"They'll never agree."

"They might. They fear vampires too. And together—" Grist did the numbers again in his head. "Together, we can win. Human armies, elven archers, dwarven fortifications, goblin shamanism. The vampires can't stand against all of us."

"Humans hunt us," Skag said. "Elves kill us on sight. Dwarves war with us underground. And you want to *ally* with them?"

"I want to survive." Grist stepped forward, into the center of the council circle. "I want my son to survive. I want *your* children to survive. If that means asking help from those who hate us, then we ask. Pride is worth nothing if we're dead."

"They'll refuse," Skag said. "They'll laugh. They'll send back our heads."

"Maybe." Grist looked at them—war-chiefs, clan-mothers, shamans, all the leaders his people had. "Or maybe they'll see what we see. That this is bigger than old hatreds. That we survive together or die alone."

He waited.

Clan-Mother Vrix spoke first. "I'm old. I remember the last time goblins were nearly destroyed. We didn't ask for help then. We should have." She placed her hand on the council stone. "I vote to send envoys."

One by one, they voted. War-Chief Skag last, hands clenched, shame on his face.

"We'll look weak," he said.

"We are weak," Grist answered. "That's why we need allies."

Skag placed his hand on the stone.

### Scene 2: Selecting the Envoys

**Year -1051 | Grist's Hall, Blood Hollow**
**POV: Vren Blood-Eye, Son of the Shaman-King**

---

Vren knew before his father spoke.

The council had called for envoys. Three goblins to approach three races. The most important mission in goblin history—begging enemies for help.

And his father was looking at him with that expression. The one that meant duty.

"No," Vren's mother said.

Grist didn't look at her. "Vren. I need you to go to the humans."

"Father—"

"The Valdrian king is young. Ambitious. He might listen to reason if it's presented well. You speak their language. You've studied their customs. You know how to—"

"He's your son." Vren's mother, Thira, stepped between them. Her eyes were wild. "Your *only* son. Send someone else."

"There is no one else. Skag would start a fight. The other war-chiefs would be worse. The shamans frighten humans. It has to be someone who can speak to them as equals."

"Equals." Thira's voice cracked. "You think humans will see any goblin as equal? They'll kill him the moment he arrives."

"That's possible."

"Then don't send him!"

Grist finally looked at her. Vren saw the pain in his father's face, the cost of what he was asking. It didn't make him feel better.

"If I don't send him," Grist said quietly, "we all die. If I do send him, we might live." He turned to Vren. "I won't order you. I'm asking. As your father, as your king. Will you go?"

Vren thought of his daughter, three years old, barely old enough to remember him if he didn't come back. He thought of his wife, pregnant with their second. He thought of the swamps that had raised him, that he'd thought would always be home.

He thought of what would happen to all of them if the vampires came.

"Yes," he said.

His mother made a sound like something breaking.

---

**The Three Envoys**:
- **Vren Blood-Eye** to the Kingdom of Valdris (humans)
- **Skaela Marsh-Walker** to Silverwood Throne (elves)
- **Threk Stone-Friend** to Ironpeak Confederation (dwarves)

Vren chose Skaela himself. She was calm, patient, better with elves than anyone. She might actually get a hearing.

Threk volunteered. He'd traded with dwarves before, knew their customs. "They're practical," he said. "Show them the numbers. They'll understand."

Three envoys. Three white banners. Three chances.

The night before they left, Grist called Vren to his chambers.

"You understand the danger," Grist said.

"Yes."

"You understand I wouldn't ask if there was any other way."

"I know."

Grist put his hand on Vren's shoulder. His grip was strong—still strong, despite the age. "If they refuse... come home. Don't argue. Don't beg. Just come home. We'll find another way."

"What other way?"

Grist didn't answer.

Vren embraced his father. He held his wife and kissed his daughter. He told himself he would see them again.

He walked south with the white banner and the words he'd prepared, and he didn't look back.

## Part 2: The Missions

### Scene 3: Vren's Arrival at Valdrian Court

**Year -1050 | Valdris Prime, Throne Hall**
**POV: Vren Blood-Eye**

---

They let him in. That was the first surprise.

Vren had expected arrows at the gate. Spears in the street. Instead, the guards stared, muttered, but let him pass. The white banner meant something, even to humans.

*Or they want to see the monster up close*, he thought.

Valdris Prime was the largest city Vren had ever seen. Towers of stone, streets of cobble, thousands of humans packed together in ways goblins would find unbearable. The smell was overwhelming—meat and smoke and perfume and filth, all mixed together.

They led him to the throne hall. More guards there. More stares. A crowd of nobles, watching him like he was an animal in a cage.

King Marius II sat on the throne. Young—barely thirty, Vren guessed. Strong jaw, hard eyes. A warrior king, they said. A conqueror.

*Maybe he'll understand*, Vren thought. *Warriors respect strength. I'll show him the strength we could have together.*

"Speak," the king said.

Vren had prepared this. He'd practiced the words in human tongue, made sure to soften the goblin accent. He stepped forward and bowed—lower than a goblin would bow to anyone, a gesture of respect that cost him.

"Your Majesty. I am Vren Blood-Eye, envoy of the Great Swamp Confederation. I come with a proposal of alliance."

Murmurs through the court. Not all hostile—some curious.

"Alliance." The king's voice was flat. "Against what?"

"Against the vampires, Majesty. The clans grow. Eighty thousand now, maybe more. They've taken cities in the east. They're organizing. They're building toward conquest."

"We've noticed."

"Then you know they won't stop with the east. They'll come here. They'll come for everyone." Vren took a breath. "Alone, neither of us can stop them. Humans have numbers but struggle in night warfare. Goblins have shamanism and know the dark, but we lack numbers. Together—"

"Together." The king leaned forward. "You want us to fight alongside goblins."

"Yes, Majesty."

"The same goblins who raid our villages? Who kill our farmers? Who eat human flesh in your blood-rites?"

"Some tribes have raided. Some have—" Vren stopped himself. Defending goblin customs would only confirm human fears. "The vampire threat is greater than old grievances. If we don't unite, we both fall."

"And if we do unite? What then? Goblins in our cities? Goblins equal to humans?"

"Allies, Majesty. Fighting together. After, we return to our swamps. We don't want your lands. We want to survive."

The king studied him. Vren couldn't read human faces well, but he thought he saw consideration. Calculation. Maybe the beginning of agreement.

Then the advisor spoke.

He was old, gray-bearded, standing at the king's right hand. Duke something. Vren hadn't caught the name.

"Your Majesty," the advisor murmured, leaning close. "Goblins are monsters. This is a trick. They want us to commit our armies, then they'll turn on us. It's in their nature."

Vren heard it. The advisor meant him to hear it.

"It's not a trick," Vren said. "I swear on my father's blood, on my children's lives—"

"Goblin oaths." The advisor smiled. "Worth nothing."

"I've come under white banner. I've bowed to your king. I've offered alliance that costs goblin pride as much as human. What more proof do you need?"

"Monsters don't change their nature."

"I'm not asking you to love us." Vren was losing control now, hearing his voice rise. "I'm asking you to *survive*. The vampires will come. They'll enslave you. They'll turn your children into cattle. And you'll die knowing you could have stopped them but you were too proud to accept help from—"

"Enough."

The king's voice cut through. Vren stopped.

Marius II stood. He was tall—taller than Vren had expected. His hand rested on his sword.

"I've heard your proposal," the king said. "Here is my answer."

He drew the sword.

Guards seized Vren before he could move. Two on each arm, twisting until his joints screamed.

"Majesty—" Vren gasped. "White banner—"

"White banner protects envoys. Monsters are not envoys." The king stepped down from the throne, sword in hand. "You came here to deceive us. To make us lower our guard. It won't work."

"Please—I have a daughter—"

The king paused. For a moment—just a moment—Vren saw something that might have been doubt.

Then the advisor whispered again, too low to hear.

The doubt vanished.

"Execute him," the king said. "Send his head to the swamps. Let the goblins know our answer."

Vren struggled. It did nothing. The guards dragged him toward the door.

"You doom us both!" he screamed. "You doom us both! Remember—when they come for you—remember that we *asked*—"

The doors closed behind him.

### Scene 4: Skaela at Silverwood Border

**Year -1050 | Edge of Silverwood Forest**
**POV: Skaela Marsh-Walker**

---

She waited three days.

The elves knew she was there. She'd seen them in the trees—shadows that moved when shadows shouldn't, watching, judging. She'd shown the white banner. She'd called out in the few elven words she knew, begging for audience.

Nothing.

On the third day, a message came. Not a messenger—just an arrow, driven into the ground at her feet. Wrapped around it, a strip of paper.

*Silverwood does not treat with goblins. Leave or die.*

Skaela looked up at the trees. She could see them now, not hiding anymore. A dozen elven archers with arrows nocked.

She could argue. She could beg. She could try to explain that the vampires would come for elves too, that Silverwood's walls wouldn't save them forever.

But the arrows were real and her words were wind.

She turned and walked away.

*We asked*, she thought. *Remember that we asked.*

---

### Scene 5: Threk at Ironpeak Gates

**Year -1050 | Ironpeak Confederation, Great Hall**
**POV: Threk Stone-Friend**

---

The dwarves listened. That was more than Threk had expected.

High King Krag Stonebreaker sat on the stone throne, clan-lords around him. They didn't offer Threk a seat—goblins stood before dwarves—but they let him speak.

He showed them the numbers. Vampire population. Growth rates. Territory taken. Projected conquest timeline.

"Three years," Threk said. "Maybe four. They take the human lands, then the elven forests. Then they come underground. For all of us."

Krag studied the figures. His advisors muttered among themselves.

"Your numbers are sound," Krag said finally.

"They are, High King."

"And you propose... joint defense? Dwarven fortifications, goblin shamans?"

"Yes. And human armies, elven archers. All of us together. It's the only way."

Krag nodded slowly. "Perhaps." He looked at his advisors. "What say the clans?"

They debated. Threk waited, heart pounding. This was closer than he'd dared hope. Dwarves were practical. They could see the threat.

Finally, Krag raised his hand.

"Here is our answer, goblin. We will defend our holds. We will seal our gates. We will wait for the storm to pass." He met Threk's eyes. "We will not join your alliance."

Threk felt the hope drain away. "High King—"

"The vampires will come for humans first. Then elves. Then, perhaps, us. But our holds are strong. We can outlast them."

"You can't outlast a thousand years."

"We'll find another way."

*There is no other way*, Threk wanted to scream. But he could see the decision was made. Dwarves didn't change their minds once set.

"Then I'll take my leave," Threk said. "When they come for you, High King, remember that we asked."

Krag's jaw tightened. "I'll remember."

## Part 3: The Return

### Scene 6: Grist Receives News

**Year -1050 | Blood Hollow, Great Swamp Confederation**
**POV: Grist Blood-Eye**

---

Skaela came first. She didn't need to speak—her face said everything.

Then Threk. Same story. The dwarves wouldn't help. Would defend themselves and wait.

"Vren?" Grist asked.

Threk shook his head. "No word yet."

Grist waited. He sat in his hollow tree and called to the spirits and received nothing. He watched the edge of the swamp where the southern path emerged.

Three days.

On the fourth day, a runner came. Human soldiers had been spotted approaching the border. Carrying something.

Grist went to meet them.

---

There were twenty soldiers. They stopped at the swamp's edge—human soldiers feared goblin territory, even with numbers. Their leader held something wrapped in cloth.

"King Marius sends his answer," the leader called.

He threw the bundle.

It landed in the mud at Grist's feet. He knew what it was before he unwrapped it. He knew from the shape, the weight, the smell.

His son's head stared up at him. Eyes open. Mouth frozen in a word he'd never finish.

Grist made a sound. Not a scream—he wouldn't give humans that. Something lower. Something that came from the bottom of him.

He picked up his son's head. He held it to his chest.

The soldiers watched. Some looked uncomfortable. Some laughed.

Grist looked at them through vision that blurred and cleared and blurred again.

"Go," he said.

"King Marius says—"

"*GO.*"

They went.

---

He carried Vren home himself. He wouldn't let anyone else touch him.

The council gathered. The tribe gathered. His wife Thira saw him coming and began to scream, and didn't stop for an hour.

Grist sat with what remained of his son and felt something inside him change. The grief was there—it would always be there—but something else was growing around it. Something cold. Something patient.

He stood.

"Hear me," he said.

The Confederation fell silent.

"We asked. We lowered ourselves. We begged for help to save our people." Grist held up his son's head. "This was their answer."

Silence. Grief. Rage building.

"Remember this. Remember that we asked. Remember that they answered with my son's head." He looked at them—every goblin in Blood Hollow, every leader of every tribe. "We will never ask again. We will never forget. And one day—if it takes a thousand years—they will answer for this."

He raised his voice.

"Swear it. On blood and bone and spirit. Swear that we never forget."

They swore. Every goblin there. The Oath that would echo for two thousand years.

*We will never ask again. We will never forget.*

## Part 4: The Consequence

### The Conquest (Year -1020)

Goblins fought the vampires alone. Thirty years after the Great Gathering, vampire armies swept through the swamps.

The Confederation resisted longer than anyone expected—shamanic magic, guerrilla warfare, knowledge of terrain. But the numbers were too great. Tribe by tribe, they fell.

Grist Blood-Eye died in the final battle of Blood Hollow. He killed four vampires before they took him down. His last words were a curse on humans and elves.

### The Vampire Era (Year -1000 to Year 0)

Goblins were treated worst of all races. They were breeding stock, experimental subjects, blood cattle. The vampires knew the other races despised goblins. No one would speak for them.

For a thousand years, goblins suffered. And remembered.

### The Preservation (Year 0-100)

After the Collapse, goblin shamans preserved what they'd learned from vampires. Blood magic. Dimensional techniques. Weapons.

"Why do we use their magic?" young goblins asked.

"Because it's the one thing they gave us that can hurt them. And there are others who deserve to hurt."

Rage became tradition. The Oath passed down through generations.

### Year 1200: Gritha Bloodsinger

A young goblin shaman sits in council. She has never known a world where goblins weren't hunted. She has never known humans or elves who didn't treat her kind as monsters.

But she knows the stories. Vren Blood-Eye who went to sue for peace and was sent back in pieces. The Great Gathering where her people lowered themselves, and were answered with murder.

She looks at humans and sees the race that killed Vren.

She practices blood magic and feels the old rage rise.

*We will never ask again. We will never forget.*

## Historical Note

The Great Gathering is remembered differently by each race:

**Goblins**: The ultimate betrayal. Proof that other races are monsters who deserve everything that happens to them.

**Humans**: Barely remembered. A footnote about goblin envoys being executed for espionage or trickery. Most humans don't know it happened.

**Elves**: Not officially recorded. Silverwood's refusal to meet goblins was standard policy, not a deliberate choice.

**Dwarves**: Remembered as practical decision. "We couldn't have won anyway. Better to defend what we could."

**Scholars**: Recognize the Great Gathering as a turning point. Had the alliance formed, vampires might have been stopped. Instead, divided races fell one by one.

Only goblins remember who asked for help.

Only goblins remember who answered with murder.
