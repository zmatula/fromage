# The Immediate Aftermath: Years 0-10

The true horror of the collapse's first decade. This is not a timeline—it's a chronicle of apocalypse.

---

## The Numbers

**Before the Collapse**
- Total population (all servant races): ~50 million
- Vampires: ~15 million

**Year 1**
- Servants: ~40 million (10 million dead in collapse itself)
- Vampires: <1,000 (scattered survivors)

**Year 5**
- Population: ~20 million (30 million dead)
- Vampires: <100 (being hunted)

**Year 10**
- Population: ~10 million (40 million dead)
- Vampires: ~0 (officially extinct)

**Half the world died in ten years.**

---

## Year 0: The Collapse

### Day 1-7: The Wave

**What Happened**
- Sundering Wave killed everything in its path
- Capital region: total death
- Expanding outward for weeks
- Not just vampires—every living thing

**The Sound**
- Survivors described a sound
- Not an explosion—a tearing
- Like reality itself screaming
- Heard hundreds of miles away
- Some went mad from it

### The Bloodweb Death

**What It Meant**
- Not just vampire power—infrastructure
- Food distribution
- Water purification
- Heating systems
- Communication
- All gone instantly

**The Immediate Effect**
- Cities without food
- No way to move supplies
- No way to coordinate
- No one in charge

### Week 1-4: The Chaos

**What Happened**
- Mass panic
- Looting, violence
- People fleeing cities without knowing where to go
- Vampire overseers abandoning posts or being killed
- No authority, no information, no hope

---

## Year 1: The Dying

### Starvation

**The Logistics**
- Bloodweb had distributed food to all cities
- Without it, cities had days of supplies
- No infrastructure for mass food movement
- No one organized transport
- No one knew how

**The Death**
- Cities starved first
- Populations that couldn't forage
- The young, the old, the sick
- Then everyone else

**The Cannibalism**
- Not myth—documented
- Siege conditions in major cities
- Parents eating children
- Children eating parents
- The living eating the dead

### Disease

**Without Vampire Systems**
- No water purification
- No waste removal
- No medical care
- Corpses piling up

**The Plagues**
- Multiple outbreaks
- Cholera, typhus, others unnamed
- Spread through refugee columns
- No way to treat, no way to stop

**Year 1 Plague Deaths**: ~5 million

### Violence

**Who Fought Whom**
- Servants against vampires (where vampires remained)
- Servants against collaborators
- Servants against servants
- Warlords against warlords
- Everyone against everyone

**The Purifier Bands**
- Formed to hunt vampires
- Often hunted anyone they wanted
- Rape, murder, robbery under "purification"
- Some were heroes; some were monsters

---

## Year 2-3: The Purges

### Who Were Collaborators?

**True Collaborators**
- Administrators who ran vampire systems
- Informants who reported resistance
- Overseers who brutalized fellow servants
- Those who prospered under vampire rule

**Accused Collaborators**
- Anyone with nice things
- Anyone who could read vampire script
- Anyone with vampire knowledge
- Neighbors you hated
- People with something you wanted

**No Distinction Made**

### How It Worked

**The Accusation**
- Public denunciation
- Often anonymous
- Sometimes in exchange for food
- Sometimes from grudges decades old

**The Trial**
- No trial
- Or trial by mob
- Verdict predetermined
- Defense meaningless

**The Punishment**
- Burning (most common)
- Hanging
- Drowning
- Being torn apart
- Being buried alive

### The Numbers

**Estimated Purge Deaths**: 3-5 million
**Percentage Actually Guilty**: Unknown—probably small

### The Stories

**The Healer of Valdris (Year 2)**
- A woman who knew vampire medicine
- Saved hundreds of lives after collapse
- When they learned where she learned it
- Burned alive by people she'd healed

**The Librarian of Ironmount (Year 3)**
- A dwarf who could read vampire script
- Warned destruction would lose vital knowledge
- Accused of vampire sympathy
- Thrown into a mine shaft

**The Teacher (Year 2)**
- Taught children mathematics
- Using methods learned under vampires
- Parents burned down the school
- With children inside
- "To kill the corruption at the root"

---

## Year 4-5: The Warlords

### Why Warlords?

**Power Vacuum**
- No authority survived the collapse
- Those with weapons had power
- Those with food had leverage
- Those willing to kill had both

**The Math**
- Food enough for 10 million
- Population of 20 million
- Someone had to die

### The Types

**The Survivor**
- Just trying to feed their people
- Did terrible things because necessary
- Sometimes became good rulers
- Sometimes became monsters anyway

**The Opportunist**
- Saw chaos as opportunity
- Gathered power for its own sake
- Used protection rackets
- "Serve me or starve"

**The Ideologue**
- Believed in a vision
- Would kill to achieve it
- Often killed more than opportunists
- "For the greater good"

### The Wars

**Year 4-10**: Constant warfare
- Over food, territory, resources
- Over ideology, grudges, nothing
- Armies were starving men with weapons
- Civilians were prey

**Casualties**: 5-10 million

### The Stories

**The Battle of Three Rivers (Year 5)**
- Two warlords fighting for farmland
- Third warlord arrived during battle
- All three armies destroyed each other
- Farmland burned
- 40,000 dead
- No one won

**The Siege of Oldgate (Year 6)**
- City held out for six months
- Defenders ate rats, then leather, then dead
- When the walls fell
- No one left alive to conquer

---

## Year 6-8: The Orphans

### The Children

**Who Survived**
- Parents died—children didn't
- Millions of children without families
- Some taken in by strangers
- Most weren't

**Life as an Orphan**
- Scavenging, begging, stealing
- Trading anything for food
- Many died
- Many worse than died

**The Gangs**
- Children organized for survival
- Some became bandits
- Some became worse
- By Year 10, some were warlords themselves

### The Stories

**The Children's March (Year 7)**
- 2,000 orphans traveling together
- Heard there was food in the south
- No adults would help them
- Made it 200 miles
- Winter killed them
- Found in spring, huddled together

**The Boy King of Ashford (Year 8)**
- 14-year-old who took over a town
- Smart, ruthless, effective
- Ruled for three years
- Killed by an adult who wanted his territory
- His child soldiers executed

---

## Year 8-10: The Stabilization

### What "Stabilization" Meant

**Not Peace**
- Just less chaos
- Warlords becoming kings
- Territory becoming kingdoms
- Laws (of a sort)
- But still brutal

**The Cost**
- 40 million dead in ten years
- 4/5 of the population
- Most knowledge lost
- Most culture destroyed
- Generation traumatized beyond recovery

### The New Order

**Who Ruled**
- Whoever survived
- Whoever was ruthless enough
- Not the wise or the good
- The strong and the lucky

**How They Ruled**
- By fear
- By force
- By controlling food
- By promising security
- By pointing at an enemy

### What Was Lost

**Knowledge**
- Vampire technology destroyed or seized
- Anyone who understood it killed or fled
- Libraries burned as "tainted"
- Language suppressed
- History rewritten

**People**
- 40 million dead
- Families destroyed
- Communities scattered
- Generations missing

**Trust**
- Everyone betrayed someone
- Everyone was betrayed
- Neighbor had killed neighbor
- Brother had denounced brother
- How do you rebuild from that?

---

## What People Remembered

### The Survivors' Stories

**Every survivor had stories.**
- What they saw
- What they did
- What they couldn't prevent
- What they can't forget

**They didn't tell them.**
- Shame
- Trauma
- The children wouldn't understand
- Better forgotten

**But the children knew anyway.**
- Nightmares
- Silences
- Things not discussed
- The way adults looked at each other

### The Generational Trauma

**First Generation (Year 0-50)**
- Lived through it
- Did what they had to do
- Carried the guilt
- Died early, died hard

**Second Generation (Year 50-100)**
- Raised by traumatized parents
- Inherited the fear
- Didn't understand why
- Angry at everything

**Third Generation (Year 100-150)**
- Grandparents dead
- Parents wouldn't talk
- Just knew something was wrong
- Started asking questions

**Every generation since has been shaped by what happened in those ten years.**

## The Specific Horrors

### The Healer's Choice (Year 2)

*Refugees from a collapsed city arrived at a village. 2,000 people. The village had food for 100.*

The healer—a woman named Mira—had to choose.

**Option A**: Share food with all. Everyone starves slowly.
**Option B**: Keep food for village. Refugees die.
**Option C**: Triage. Decide who lives.

She chose C.

She examined every refugee. Chose 200 who were healthy enough to contribute. Fed them. The rest—1,800 people—she turned away. She knew they would die.

She saved her village. She lived to 90. She never slept peacefully again.

*Her descendants still live in that village. They don't talk about grandmother.*

---

### The Collaboration Document (Year 3)

*A woman named Elena had worked as a translator for vampire administrators. She knew the systems. After the collapse, she used that knowledge to help organize food distribution.*

*Someone found her work papers.*

The mob came for her. She tried to explain—she had saved lives, she was helping, her vampire work was long ago.

They didn't care. Collaborator was collaborator.

Her daughter was 8. She watched the burning. She grew up to lead a Purifier band.

*"I understood why they did it. That's what haunts me."*

---

### The River Town (Year 5)

*A town of 3,000 built on a river. Controlled a bridge—valuable territory.*

Two warlords wanted it. Instead of fighting each other, they agreed to share it. The town thought they were saved.

The warlords used the town as a base. Ate their food. Took their women. Conscripted their men. When the town was used up, they fought over what remained.

700 survivors. They fled. Town abandoned.

*The town is still there. Still empty. Locals won't go near it.*

---

### The Children's Crusade (Year 7)

*A priest named Father Marcus gathered orphans and told them God would protect them if they had faith. He led them toward a "holy place" where they would be safe.*

500 children followed him.

It wasn't a holy place. It was a ruin—nothing there. The children had no food. Father Marcus had no plan. He'd had a vision. The vision was wrong.

Half died before help came. Father Marcus survived. He spent the rest of his life caring for orphans.

*Was he a saint or a fool? People still argue.*

## Legacy

### What It Created

**Fear of Vampires**
- Not rational—visceral
- The collapse proved vampires were dangerous
- Association: vampires = death of everything

**Fear of Knowledge**
- Those who understood vampires = suspect
- Knowledge itself = potential corruption
- Ignorance = safety

**Fear of Order**
- Vampires had order—it killed everyone
- Organization = potential tyranny
- Better chaos than control

**Fear of Each Other**
- Neighbors betrayed neighbors
- Trust died with the purges
- Still haven't recovered

### What It Destroyed

**Population**
- 40 million dead
- Demographic devastation
- Took 500 years to recover

**Knowledge**
- Vampire technology
- Vampire language
- Vampire history
- Any knowledge associated with vampires

**Trust**
- Between neighbors
- Between races
- Between regions
- Between generations

### What It Explains

**Why the Church is feared and followed**
- They provided answers in the chaos
- They said vampires were demons
- People wanted to believe

**Why knowledge is dangerous**
- Those who understood vampires died
- Understanding = suspicion
- Better not to know

**Why vampires are monsters in memory**
- The collapse killed everyone
- Vampires caused the collapse
- Therefore vampires = absolute evil

**Why Vessel Seven will be feared**
- He is what the collapse came from
- He represents everything that went wrong
- His existence threatens to bring it back
