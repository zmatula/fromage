# The Immediate Aftermath: Year 0 to 8

The ground-level chaos before the first kingdoms. Warlords, purifiers, and survivors in a world without rules.

---

## The First Days (Year 0, Months 7-12)

### The Vacuum

When the vampires died, they took order with them. No government. No law. No food distribution. No protection.

**What Remained**:
- Millions of former servants with nothing
- Weapons from vampire armories
- Blood farms full of dying livestock
- Infrastructure that no one knew how to run
- Hatred for anything associated with vampires

### The First Warlords

Within weeks, strong individuals began claiming territory. They weren't kings. They weren't even leaders. They were just the ones who could kill anyone who disagreed.

---

#### Thresh of the Eastern Mines

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Overseer in a vampire mine
**Territory**: Eastern mining region
**Peak Power**: Month 9
**Controlled**: ~5,000 people

**Who He Was**:
Thresh had been a collaborator—a human who managed other humans for vampire masters. When the vampires died, he had keys to the weapons vault. He also knew where the food was.

**His Method**:
"Work the mines. I'll feed you. Refuse, and I won't." Simple. Brutal. Effective.

**His End**:
Month 11. His own workers killed him. They'd found other food. They didn't need him anymore. His body was thrown into a mine shaft.

**Legacy**:
None. He was the first and worst kind of warlord—pure opportunism. Hundreds like him rose and fell in those months. None are remembered by name except as examples.

---

#### Mara of the Healing Hands

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Medical servant in vampire household
**Territory**: River valley in central region
**Peak Power**: Year 1
**Controlled**: ~8,000 people

**Who She Was**:
Mara knew medicine—vampire medicine, which was far beyond what humans had. When plague hit the refugee camps in Month 8, she was the only one who could help.

**Her Method**:
"I'll heal your sick if you defend my hospitals." She traded medical care for loyalty and protection. People came to her, not because she conquered them, but because they needed her.

**Her End**:
Year 3. Assassinated by a rival warlord who wanted her territory. Her hospitals were looted. The medical knowledge was lost—no one else could read the vampire texts.

**Legacy**:
Later called "the First Saint." The Church of Divine Light claimed her as a holy figure, though she never followed any religion. Her actual methods—pragmatic trading—were forgotten. Only the myth remained.

---

#### General Koss

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Commander of vampire auxiliary forces
**Territory**: Northern plains
**Peak Power**: Year 2
**Controlled**: ~15,000 people

**Who He Was**:
Koss had been a soldier, commanding humans who fought for vampires. When the vampires died, he had 2,000 trained troops and nowhere to go.

**His Method**:
Military discipline. Organized supply lines. Defended territory rather than raiding it. He was building a kingdom before anyone knew what a kingdom was.

**His Approach**:
"The strong protect the weak. The weak work for the strong. Everyone eats." Almost civilized.

**His End**:
Year 5. His own officers staged a coup. They wanted to raid and loot, not build and defend. Koss was executed by his second-in-command.

**Legacy**:
His organizational methods survived his death. The officers who killed him used his systems to build the Northern Marches—precursor to the Northern Kingdom. They erased his name from history. He deserved better.

---

### The Blood Farms

**The Problem**: Vampires had maintained enormous blood farms—facilities where blood was extracted from prisoners or livestock for vampire consumption. When vampires died, these farms became flashpoints.

**What Was Inside**:
- Thousands of prisoners, often dying
- Blood stores (valuable for medicine and preservation)
- Records of who had been harvested
- Evidence of which humans had helped

**The Pattern**:
1. Liberation (someone frees the prisoners)
2. Revelation (records show who collaborated)
3. Revenge (collaborators hunted)
4. Looting (blood stores seized)
5. Destruction (farm burned)

**The First Farm Liberator**:

#### Sister Keth

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Temple servant who escaped vampire household
**Territory**: Central blood farm region
**Actions**: Years 0-2

Sister Keth led a group of escaped servants to three blood farms in the first year. She freed prisoners. She burned records (to prevent revenge killings, she said). She destroyed the facilities.

**The Controversy**:
Her record-burning was later condemned. Those records proved who had collaborated. Without them, guilty people escaped. But they also proved who had been imprisoned—some survivors needed that evidence.

**Her End**:
Year 2. Killed at the fourth farm. The prisoners she freed had been there so long they didn't recognize liberation. They attacked her.

**Legacy**:
Mixed. Hero for freeing thousands. Villain for burning evidence. She was trying to prevent a cycle of revenge. She may have just delayed it.

## The Purifier Bands (Year 1-10)

### Origins

The purifiers started as vampire hunters—bands that tracked down survivors and killed them. They became something else.

### The First Band

#### The Righteous Flame

**Founded**: Year 1
**Founder**: Caldris the Accuser
**Original Purpose**: Hunt vampires
**What They Became**: Hunt anyone associated with vampires

**Caldris the Accuser**:
- Former prisoner in blood farm
- Freed in collapse
- Watched his family bled to death over ten years
- Had no interest in forgiveness

**His Creed**: "Blood guilt cannot be washed away. It must be burned out."

**What He Did**:
Year 1-3: Hunted actual vampire survivors. Killed perhaps 200.
Year 3-5: Ran out of vampires. Started hunting collaborators.
Year 5-8: Ran out of proven collaborators. Started accepting accusations.

**The Problem**:
By Year 5, the Righteous Flame was burning people on testimony alone. Accusation became execution. Neighbors settled scores. Children condemned parents for food rewards.

**Caldris's End**:
Year 8. He was accused by his own lieutenant—supposedly for hiding vampire sympathies. Almost certainly a power play. Caldris died on a pyre he'd helped build.

**Legacy**:
The Righteous Flame continued after his death. They killed for another 30 years before finally disbanding. Total deaths attributed to them: approximately 15,000. Vampires they killed: maybe 500.

---

### The Collaboration Trials

**The Question**: Who deserved to die for serving vampires?

**The Categories**:

1. **Administrators**: Managed vampire estates and households
2. **Overseers**: Supervised other servants
3. **Soldiers**: Fought in vampire auxiliary forces
4. **Specialists**: Healers, scribes, craftsmen who worked directly for vampires
5. **Informants**: Reported resistance to vampire masters

**The Problems**:

- Many "collaborators" had no choice—serve or die
- Some collaborators helped other servants survive
- Some resisters got innocent people killed
- The accusers often had their own agendas
- There were no courts, no evidence rules, no appeals

**The Results**:

Approximately 40,000 people killed as collaborators in the first ten years. How many were actually guilty? How many were guilty but shouldn't have died? How many were innocent?

No one knows. No one kept fair records.

---

### The Falsely Accused

#### Kovan Marsh

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Scribe in vampire archive
**Accused Of**: Informing on resistance members
**Actual Role**: Smuggled resistance messages hidden in archive records

Kovan was the resistance's most valuable spy. He copied vampire documents showing troop movements, prisoner locations, security weaknesses. He passed them through a network of servants.

When the collapse came, the network members were scattered. No one could confirm his role. His vampire masters' records showed him as a trusted servant. That was the cover that kept him alive—and the evidence that killed him.

**His Trial**:
Three witnesses said he informed. All three had personal grudges. No witnesses defended him. The resistance contacts who could have saved him were dead or fled.

**His End**:
Burned, Year 3. He didn't beg. He said, "I did what I did. The truth is in the archives." But the archives were already destroyed.

**Legacy**:
Years later, a survivor from his network wrote the truth. It was dismissed as forgery. Kovan's grandchildren still live with the family shame—for treason he never committed.

---

#### The Ashford Children

**Names**: Mira (12), Kell (10), Sera (8)
**Race**: Human
**Accused Of**: Vampire blood—claims their mother was vampire-touched
**Actual Status**: Completely human children of a collaborator administrator

Their father managed a vampire estate. When the collapse came, he fled with whatever he could carry. He was caught and executed. The children were left in a refugee camp.

**The Accusation**:
A woman claimed to have seen the children drink blood. She was probably lying—she wanted their supplies. But the claim spread: vampire spawn, hidden among humans, waiting to grow into monsters.

**The Mob**:
Came for them on the third night. The camp leader tried to stop it. He was beaten. The children were dragged out.

**What Happened**:
Unknown. Their bodies were never found. Some say they were killed. Some say they escaped and changed their names. The name "Ashford" became cursed—associated with vampire taint.

**Legacy**:
Centuries later, a "Mira Ashford" appears in Maritime Republic records—a merchant captain. Coincidence? Descendant? The actual child? The name keeps appearing in history, always at the margins, always moving on.

---

### The Hidden Collaborator

#### Vera Goldveil

**Race**: Human
**Background**: Administrator of vampire financial accounts
**Actual Role**: Managed more wealth than most vampire lords
**Public Story**: Minor household servant

Vera was brilliant—and careful. She had managed vampire finances, moved assets, tracked debts. She knew where every treasure was buried, every payment hidden.

When the collapse came, she burned her records, changed her name, and walked into a refugee camp as just another survivor.

**What She Did**:
Year 0-5: Lay low. Survived. Watched.
Year 5-10: Began recovering vampire assets she knew about.
Year 10-40: Built the Goldveil Banking House in the Free Cities.

**The Truth**:
House Goldveil's fortune was built on vampire gold that Vera knew how to find because she'd hidden it in the first place. The oldest banking house in the Maritime Republic was founded by a collaborator who never faced justice.

**Legacy**:
House Goldveil exists in Year 1200—among the most powerful banks. The family claims descent from "refugee merchants." The truth is in Vera's private journals, locked in the family vault. No one outside the family has read them in 1,200 years.

## The Religious Movements

### Before Sister Mercy

Sister Mercy founded the Church of Divine Light in Year 1, but she wasn't the first religious leader of the post-collapse.

#### Prophet Kavel of the Burned God

**Race**: Human
**Appeared**: Year 0, Month 8
**Disappeared**: Year 1, Month 3
**Followers**: ~3,000 at peak

**His Teaching**:
The collapse was the death of the old god (vampires' god) and birth of the new (a god of fire and cleansing). Salvation came through fire. His followers burned vampire artifacts, vampire buildings, and eventually themselves.

**His End**:
Led 200 followers into a vampire ruin, sealed the entrance, and set it ablaze. "We rise as smoke to the Burned God." No survivors.

**Legacy**:
Dismissed as a madman, but his ideas influenced later Church doctrine: fire as purification, vampire artifacts as spiritually dangerous, martyrdom as holy.

---

#### The Silent Congregation

**Founded**: Year 1
**Founder**: Unknown (deliberately)
**Belief**: The divine spoke only through silence; words were corruption

Members took vows of silence. They communicated through gesture. They helped anyone who asked, without question or record.

**Their Work**:
They buried the dead. Thousands and thousands of dead. They asked no payment. They left no names.

**Their End**:
Year 15. Cassandra the Burner decided they were vampire cultists (they weren't) and destroyed them. Their burial sites became mass graves' only markers.

**Legacy**:
Some say the Silent Congregation never ended—just went deeper into silence. Monks in certain remote monasteries don't speak. They might be descendants. Or just quiet.

## The First Marriages

### Cross-Race Unions

Before the collapse, vampires had forbidden intermarriage between servant races. After, it happened anyway.

#### Borin and Elara

**Races**: Dwarf and human
**Year**: 3
**Location**: Eastern borderlands

Borin was a mining engineer. Elara was a farmer. They met in a refugee camp. They married within a year.

**The Reaction**:
Dwarves were scandalized—Borin was polluting his bloodline. Humans were confused—why would anyone marry down (dwarves were associated with labor). Neither community accepted them.

**What They Did**:
Founded a homestead in unclaimed territory. Had three children (human-presenting but long-lived). Built a community of other outcasts.

**Legacy**:
The borderland community they founded still exists—called "Borin's Rest." It's the oldest continuously inhabited cross-race settlement. Their descendants are the region's leading family.

---

#### Kess and Thornwood

**Races**: Goblin and elf
**Year**: 5
**Location**: Forest edge

This shouldn't have happened. Goblins and elves hated each other—ancient enemies before vampires, worse enemies during. But Kess and Thornwood found each other in the chaos and didn't care about history.

**The Reaction**:
Both races rejected them completely. They were expelled from all communities. Even mixed-race settlements wouldn't take them.

**What They Did**:
Lived alone in the forest for decades. Had no children (the races aren't compatible). Died within a year of each other.

**Legacy**:
None, except as a curiosity. But they proved something: even the oldest hatreds could be overcome by two people who chose each other over their peoples. Whether that matters depends on what you value.

## The Path to Roderick

### Year 8: The State of Things

**Political**: Dozens of warlords controlling territories from a few hundred to 20,000 people. No stable borders. Constant fighting.

**Social**: Purifier bands still hunting. Collaborator trials still killing. Cross-race tensions rising. Slavery returning (in different forms).

**Economic**: Trade routes forming but dangerous. Blood stores running out. Vampire infrastructure failing. Starvation in many regions.

**Religious**: Sister Mercy's church growing. Competing cults declining. Doctrine forming around anti-vampire ideology.

### Roderick's Emergence

Year 3: Sergeant in a warlord's army (eastern region)
Year 5: Takes over his unit after the warlord dies
Year 8: Controls three territories through conquest and alliance
Year 12: Declares himself King of Valdris

He was not the first warlord, not the best person, and not the most powerful in Year 8. But he was the one who lasted—the one who built something that outlived him.

Why him and not the others? Partly luck. Partly skill. Partly the fact that by Year 8, people were tired of chaos. They wanted order. They would accept a tyrant if the tyrant would make the killing stop.

Roderick made it stop. In his territories, anyway. Everywhere else, it continued.

*The eight years between collapse and Roderick were the worst in human history. Forty million dead—more than the vampires ever killed in a thousand years. Not from vampire violence but from human chaos. The vampires built an evil system that held order. When it fell, the order fell too, and people killed each other in the void. Remember that before you celebrate liberation. Freedom without structure is just another kind of death.*
