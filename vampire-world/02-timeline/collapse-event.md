# The Collapse Event (Year 0)

The pivot point of all history. This document details what actually happened, how different groups remember it, and the consequences that cascaded through the following 1,200 years.

---

## Part I: What Actually Happened (Shadow Truth)

### The Multi-Causal Breakdown

The collapse was not a single event but a cascade of failures that occurred over approximately 18 months (Year -1 to Year 0). No single cause was sufficient alone; the combination was lethal.

#### Primary Causes (Immediate Triggers)

**1. The Sanguine Ascension Experiment (The Triggering Event)**

The **Crimson Covenant faction** initiated Project Sanguine Ascension in Year -3, an attempt to create a new form of vampire that could transcend their inherent limitations:
- Goal: Eliminate vulnerability to sunlight, reduce blood dependency, extend already-long lifespans
- Method: Binding mortal souls to vampire essence through a blood-magic matrix
- Scale: 144 subjects in the primary cohort (the protagonist was Subject 73)
- Location: The Vermillion Spire complex beneath the capital city of **Kaelthros**

**What Went Wrong:**
- The soul-binding process created resonance feedback through the blood-magic network
- This network connected all major vampire cities for communication and power
- The feedback didn't destroy the network—it *inverted* it
- Instead of channeling power, it began *draining* it from every connected node
- Vampires experienced it as a sudden, horrifying weakness—their magic failing, their bodies aging rapidly

**Timeline of Experiment Failure:**
- Day 1: Experiment initiated, initial subjects show promising results
- Day 3: First resonance fluctuations detected, dismissed as calibration issues
- Day 7: Network instability spreads to three connected cities
- Day 12: Full cascade begins, irreversible
- Day 30: 60% of vampire population dead or dying

**2. The Blood Tithe Rebellion**

For centuries, vampires had maintained the **Blood Tithe**—a system where other races provided blood in exchange for protection and magical services. This was not slavery exactly, but it was not freedom either.

As the Sanguine Ascension cascade weakened vampires:
- The subjugated races sensed vulnerability
- Decades of resentment exploded into coordinated uprisings
- The **Goblin Warrens** revolted first (they had the best intelligence networks)
- Humans and dwarves followed within weeks
- Elves were divided—some joined the rebellion, some remained loyal

**3. Factional Civil War**

Vampire society was already fractured into competing factions:
- **The Crimson Covenant**: Progressives who initiated the experiment
- **The Obsidian Court**: Traditionalists who opposed it
- **The Pallid Hand**: Militarists who wanted to crush the rebellions
- **The Veiled Ones**: Isolationists who wanted to retreat underground

As the crisis deepened, these factions turned on each other:
- Obsidian Court blamed Crimson Covenant and attacked their holdings
- Pallid Hand attempted a military coup to "restore order"
- Veiled Ones sealed their cities and let others die
- What remained of central authority collapsed entirely

#### Secondary Causes (Systemic Weaknesses)

**1. Infrastructure Dependency**

Vampire civilization had become critically dependent on interconnected systems:
- The blood-magic network powered everything
- Cities couldn't function independently
- When the network failed, so did sanitation, food preservation, defensive wards
- Diseases spread through populations unused to illness

**2. Population Vulnerability**

Vampires had low birth rates and long lifespans:
- Total vampire population at peak: approximately 2 million
- Average age: 400 years
- Very few children (perhaps 50,000 under age 50)
- When adults died rapidly, there was no next generation to rebuild

**3. Knowledge Concentration**

Critical knowledge was held by small numbers of specialists:
- Master artificers, network engineers, blood-mages
- These individuals were primary targets in faction fighting
- When they died, irreplaceable knowledge died with them
- No written records existed for the most sensitive techniques (security measure)

**4. Other Race Dependency**

Vampires had outsourced many functions to other races:
- Dwarves built and maintained physical infrastructure
- Humans provided food production and blood
- Elves served as administrators and scholars
- Goblins handled waste management and underground maintenance
- When these races rebelled, essential services stopped

### The Cascade Timeline

**Year -1 (Pre-Collapse)**
- Month 1-6: Sanguine Ascension project in development
- Month 7: Project receives final approval from Crimson Covenant leadership
- Month 8-11: Subject selection and preparation
- Month 12: Final preparations, network calibrations

**Year 0 (Collapse Year)**

*First Quarter (Months 1-3)*
- Week 1: Experiment begins. 144 subjects enter the Vermillion Spire.
- Week 2: Initial transformations show promise. Subjects display enhanced abilities.
- Week 3: First anomalies. Network fluctuations detected in three cities.
- Week 4: Fluctuations spread. Crimson Covenant insists it's controllable.
- Week 5-6: Cascade accelerates. Blood-magic begins failing across the network.
- Week 7-8: Mass vampire die-offs begin. Panic spreads.
- Week 9-12: Full societal breakdown initiates.

*Second Quarter (Months 4-6)*
- Goblin Warrens rebel. Goblin intelligence reveals vampire weakness.
- Human uprisings in the agricultural regions.
- Dwarf fortress-cities seal themselves and declare independence.
- Elven population splits—half rebel, half remain loyal.
- Factional fighting among vampires intensifies.

*Third Quarter (Months 7-9)*
- Pallid Hand military coup fails spectacularly.
- Obsidian Court and Crimson Covenant engage in open warfare.
- Veiled Ones seal their underground cities.
- Surface vampire cities begin falling to combined rebellions.
- Blood-magic network completely collapses.

*Fourth Quarter (Months 10-12)*
- Last major surface city (Kaelthros) falls.
- Remaining vampires scatter—some underground, some into wilderness.
- Experimental subjects in Vermillion Spire: most die, some are sealed in stasis (including protagonist).
- Estimated vampire survivors: fewer than 10,000 worldwide.
- Year ends with vampire civilization effectively extinct.

### Who Survived and How

**Group 1: The Sealed (Including Protagonist)**

- Approximately 500-1,000 vampires
- Entered stasis chambers, hibernation vaults, or sealed sanctuaries
- Includes 12 surviving Sanguine Ascension subjects
- Most will never wake (chambers failed, locations lost)
- Protagonist's chamber: deep in Vermillion Spire, separate from main complex

**Group 2: The Hidden**

- Approximately 3,000-5,000 vampires
- Fled to remote locations: mountains, deep forests, island chains
- Survived by hiding, not fighting
- Gradually died out over following centuries (isolation, hunting, attrition)
- Some bloodlines may have persisted through interbreeding with other races

**Group 3: The Veiled**

- Approximately 2,000-3,000 vampires
- The Veiled Ones faction who sealed their underground cities
- Most sophisticated survival attempt
- Failed due to lack of blood supply and internal conflicts
- Last Veiled city fell around Year 200

**Group 4: The Feral**

- Approximately 1,000-2,000 vampires
- Those who devolved into bestial hunters
- Lost higher cognition due to network disconnection and trauma
- Became the "monsters" of later legends
- Hunted to extinction by Year 500

**Current Survivors (Year 1200):**
- Protagonist: In stasis for 1,200 years, recently awakened
- Unknown: Possibly other Sealed individuals in undiscovered chambers
- Possible: Hybrid descendants with diluted vampire blood
- Rumored: Veiled Ones survivors in deepest underground

### Regional Variations

The collapse did not happen uniformly. Different regions experienced different patterns:

**The Heartland (Kaelthros Region)**
- Most severe collapse
- Network hub created strongest feedback
- Complete destruction of vampire presence
- Now: Contested territory between human kingdoms

**The Northern Mountains (Dwarf Territories)**
- Least affected initially
- Dwarves had maintained more independence
- Vampires in this region fled or were killed
- Now: Dwarf kingdoms, some with sealed vampire vaults

**The Eastern Forests (Elf Territories)**
- Complicated collapse
- Many elves remained loyal, died with vampires
- Mixed vampire-elf ruins throughout
- Now: Elven lands, some cooperation with vampire history

**The Southern Plains (Human Territories)**
- Violent rebellion
- Blood Tithe system was harshest here
- Most complete destruction of vampire presence
- Now: Human kingdoms, strong anti-vampire sentiment

**The Underground (Goblin Territories)**
- Goblins seized vampire underground infrastructure
- Some cities intact but goblin-controlled
- Mixture of goblin culture and vampire remnants
- Now: Goblin warrens with vampire artifacts

**The Western Coast (Veiled Ones Territory)**
- Sealed cities remained intact longest
- Gradual failure over 200 years
- Most preserved vampire sites
- Now: Mixed settlement, major relic hunting grounds

### The Triggering Experiment in Detail

#### Project Sanguine Ascension

**Initiating Faction**: The Crimson Covenant

**Leadership**:
- **Archon Veltharion** (project director, died Day 30)
- **Magister Seraphine** (lead researcher, died Day 45)
- **Commander Kaelis** (military liaison, died in factional fighting)

**Purpose**:
The Crimson Covenant believed vampires had reached a developmental plateau. Despite their long lives and power, they faced inherent limitations:
- Sunlight vulnerability (limited surface activity)
- Blood dependency (tied to other races)
- Slow reproduction (population stagnation)
- Network dependency (centralization weakness)

They sought to create a new form of vampire that transcended these limits—the **Sanguine Ascended**.

**Method**:
The soul-binding matrix attempted to:
1. Anchor a mortal soul to vampire essence (providing mortality's adaptability)
2. Use the blood-magic network to stabilize the transformation
3. Create beings that could survive without blood, walk in sunlight, reproduce normally

**Subjects**:
- 144 volunteers/selectees from various backgrounds
- Mix of young vampires seeking advancement and older ones seeking renewal
- **Subject 73 (Protagonist)**: Selected for [specific criteria to be developed]

**What Actually Happened**:
The soul-binding created an unexpected resonance with the blood-magic network. Instead of stabilizing the subjects, the process:
1. Drew power from the network to fuel transformation
2. Created feedback loops as the network tried to compensate
3. Inverted the network's flow—instead of distributing power, it began collecting it
4. Drained magical energy from every vampire connected to the network

The subjects themselves became conduits for this drain. Most died as their bodies couldn't handle the power surge. A few (including the protagonist) achieved a partial transformation before the process was interrupted.

**The Protagonist's Transformation**:
Subject 73 underwent partial Sanguine Ascension:
- Reduced but not eliminated blood dependency
- Reduced but not eliminated sunlight vulnerability
- Enhanced magical capacity (but not vampire peak)
- Partially preserved memories (fragmented, some false)
- Placed in emergency stasis when facility began failing
- Unique among survivors due to specific circumstances of his transformation [to be detailed]

## Part II: Contradictory Historical Interpretations

How different groups remember and interpret the collapse. None are completely right; none are completely wrong.

### Official Version (What Most People Believe)

**The Narrative:**
The vampire civilization was a dark age of demonic tyranny. Vampires were monsters who enslaved the other races and fed on their blood. The gods heard the prayers of the oppressed and struck down the vampires with divine wrath. The world entered a period of chaos but eventually emerged into the current age of freedom.

**Key Elements:**
- Vampires were evil demons or corrupted beings
- The collapse was divine punishment
- Other races were innocent victims who fought for freedom
- The dark age was unfortunate but necessary
- Current civilization is morally superior

**Who Believes This:**
- Common people of all races
- Most religious institutions
- Anyone without access to scholarly sources
- Those who benefit from anti-vampire sentiment

**What It Gets Right:**
- Vampires did exploit other races
- Other races did rebel
- The collapse was catastrophic
- Vampires are effectively extinct

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- The divine punishment narrative
- Vampires as uniformly evil
- Other races as innocent victims
- Simplification of causes
- Denial of any positive vampire contributions

### Scholarly Interpretation (What Evidence Suggests)

**The Narrative:**
The vampire civilization was advanced and complex—neither utopia nor dystopia. The collapse resulted from internal failures combined with external pressures. Evidence suggests a specific catastrophic event triggered a cascade of system failures. The other races took advantage of vampire weakness but didn't cause the initial collapse.

**Key Elements:**
- Vampires were a sophisticated civilization with flaws
- Collapse was multi-causal, not divine
- A specific event triggered the cascade
- Other races were both victims and aggressors
- Much vampire knowledge was genuinely lost

**Who Believes This:**
- University scholars
- Some noble houses with historical interests
- Secret societies studying vampire history
- The more educated clergy

**What It Gets Right:**
- Multi-causal analysis
- Vampire complexity
- Specific trigger event
- Loss of knowledge
- Other race agency

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- Details of the triggering event
- Full extent of vampire capabilities
- True nature of race relations
- What knowledge remains vs. what's lost
- Underestimates intentional suppression

### Elven Memory

**The Narrative:**
We were partners, not slaves. We served the vampires but were valued and respected. When they fell, we lost our greatest allies and protectors. The rebellion was a mistake made by younger races who didn't understand what they were destroying. We mourn the collapse as a tragedy.

**Key Elements:**
- Elves had special status in vampire society
- Partnership not servitude
- The collapse was a tragedy
- Other races were short-sighted
- We preserved what we could

**Who Believes This:**
- Traditional elven nobility
- Elven historians
- High elven culture

**What It Gets Right:**
- Elves did have privileged status
- Some elves did support vampires
- Significant knowledge was lost
- Some elves tried to preserve

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- Downplays exploitation
- Ignores elves who rebelled
- Romanticizes the relationship
- Overestimates their own role
- Suppresses negative memories

### Dwarven Memory

**The Narrative:**
We built their cities and maintained their machines. We were skilled workers, not slaves, and we were paid fairly in resources and knowledge. When the vampires weakened, we simply took back our independence—no hatred, just practicality. Their civilization failed because they built too much on magic instead of solid stone.

**Key Elements:**
- Practical, transactional relationship
- Dwarves as skilled contractors
- Independence seized when possible
- Collapse due to over-reliance on magic
- No strong feelings either way

**Who Believes This:**
- Dwarven engineers and builders
- Dwarven historians
- Dwarven leadership

**What It Gets Right:**
- Dwarves did have technical roles
- Relationship was somewhat transactional
- They did seize independence opportunistically
- Infrastructure failure was real

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- Underplays coercion
- Overestimates their independence
- Downplays their role in rebellion
- Misses the magic-technology integration
- Forgets dwarves who were harshly treated

### Goblin Memory

**The Narrative:**
We were the first to see they were dying. We worked in their sewers, their tunnels, their secret places. We knew things. When we saw weakness, we struck first and struck hard. Every other race followed our lead. We freed ourselves; no gods helped us.

**Key Elements:**
- Goblins were in lowest positions but gathered intelligence
- They recognized the weakness first
- Led the rebellion through action
- No divine intervention—they freed themselves
- Pride in their role

**Who Believes This:**
- All goblins
- This is core to goblin identity
- Passed down in oral traditions

**What It Gets Right:**
- Goblins did have extensive intelligence networks
- They did recognize weakness early
- They did rebel first
- They did inspire others
- No gods were involved

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- Overestimates their centrality
- Underestimates other races' agency
- Doesn't acknowledge what they lost
- Glorifies violence
- Misses complexity of goblin-vampire relationships

### Human Memory

**The Narrative:**
We were cattle to them. They drank our blood and stole our lives. We rose up because we had no choice—better to die fighting than live as food. The gods blessed our rebellion. Now we must never allow such tyranny again.

**Key Elements:**
- Humans were blood-slaves
- Complete exploitation
- Desperate rebellion
- Divine support
- Must prevent recurrence

**Who Believes This:**
- Most humans
- Human religious institutions
- Human kingdoms
- Human-supremacist factions

**What It Gets Right:**
- Humans did provide blood
- It was exploitative
- Humans did rebel
- They should be wary of tyrants

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- Relationship was more complex
- Some humans had status
- Divine intervention narrative
- All vampires as identical monsters
- Ignores human collaboration with vampires

### Secret Society (Veil Keepers) Theory

**The Narrative:**
The collapse was caused by a specific experiment in the capital that went catastrophically wrong. This experiment involved binding souls and opened a breach into something that shouldn't be accessed. Some subjects survived the experiment and may still exist. We must find and control all vampire relics before they can be misused to repeat the catastrophe.

**Key Elements:**
- Specific experiment triggered collapse
- Soul-binding was involved
- Something was "opened"
- Survivors may exist
- Relics are dangerous

**Who Believes This:**
- The Veil Keepers secret society
- Some other secret organizations
- A few scholars with access to forbidden texts

**What It Gets Right:**
- Most accurate about triggering event
- Soul-binding involved
- Experiment location
- Survivors exist
- Relics are dangerous

**What It Gets Wrong:**
- The "breach" interpretation
- Details of the experiment
- Number and nature of survivors
- What relics can actually do
- Believes they can "control" it

## Part III: Consequence Cascade

How the collapse shaped the next 1,200 years.

### Immediate Aftermath (Year 0-10)

**Year 0-1: Chaos**
- Complete breakdown of vampire authority
- Mass celebrations and mass violence
- Looting of vampire cities and estates
- Destruction of vampire records and symbols
- Revenge killings of vampire sympathizers
- Food shortages as distribution systems collapse
- Disease outbreaks from sanitation failures

**Year 1-5: Power Vacuums**
- Local strongmen seize control
- No central authority in any region
- Thousands of small territories
- Constant small-scale warfare
- Each race splinters into factions
- Elven civil war (loyalists vs. rebels)
- Dwarven clan conflicts
- Goblin territorial wars
- Human warlord period

**Year 5-10: First Consolidation**
- Strongest leaders absorb neighbors
- Emergence of first kingdoms (still small)
- Trade routes reestablished (limited)
- First attempts at inter-racial diplomacy
- Vampire hunting becomes organized
- Records begin being kept again
- Religious movements emerge explaining the collapse

**Key Figures:**
- **Gorath Iron-Arm** (Human): First major warlord, united three regions
- **Queen Silvael** (Elf): Led loyalist faction, eventually accepted rebel victory
- **Throdin Deep-Delver** (Dwarf): Sealed dwarf mountain homes, established isolation policy
- **Skreech the Cunning** (Goblin): United five warrens, established first goblin kingdom

**Critical Events:**
- **Year 3**: Last confirmed vampire sighting on surface
- **Year 5**: First treaty between human warlords
- **Year 7**: Elven civil war ends
- **Year 8**: Dwarven isolation policy begins
- **Year 10**: First goblin-human trade agreement

### Power Vacuum Effects (Year 10-50)

**Political Development:**
- Warlord territories coalesce into kingdoms
- First multi-racial alliances form
- Religious institutions gain power as unifying forces
- Noble classes emerge from successful warriors
- Borders remain extremely fluid

**Knowledge Impact:**
- Most vampire sites thoroughly looted
- Some records preserved by collectors
- Practical vampire knowledge largely lost
- Myths and legends begin replacing history
- First deliberate suppressions of truth

**Social Changes:**
- Racial identities crystallize
- Humans develop strong anti-vampire religions
- Elves retreat into insularity
- Dwarves fortify mountains
- Goblins expand underground
- Inter-racial distrust solidifies

**Key Events:**
- **Year 12**: First human kingdom formally declared
- **Year 18**: Founding of first anti-vampire religious order
- **Year 25**: First major human-elf war
- **Year 30**: Discovery of first major vampire ruin (Kael's Deep)
- **Year 35**: Goblin expansion into former vampire tunnels
- **Year 40**: Dwarven emergence from isolation
- **Year 45**: First scholarly institution founded (to study relics)
- **Year 50**: Three human kingdoms dominate former heartland

### Long-Term Impacts (Year 50-1200)

#### Political Evolution

**Year 50-300: Warring Kingdoms Era**
- Constant warfare between emerging states
- Borders shift continuously
- Alliances form and break
- Major kingdoms absorb minor ones
- First empires emerge

**Year 300-700: Imperial Age**
- Large empires dominate
- More stable borders
- Increased trade and communication
- Cultural flourishing
- Religious institutions peak power

**Year 700-1100: Consolidation and Reform**
- Empire fragmentation into stable kingdoms
- Current political map takes shape
- Secret societies formalize
- Magic schools establish traditions
- Vampire relics become political tools

**Year 1100-1200: Recent Past**
- Current political tensions develop
- Relic hunting intensifies
- Secret society conflicts escalate
- Signs of protagonist's awakening
- Stage set for main story

#### Knowledge Evolution

**Year 50-300:**
- Vampire knowledge largely mythologized
- Practical applications lost
- Relics treated as magical treasures
- First secret societies form to preserve truth

**Year 300-700:**
- Scholarly study of relics begins
- Some techniques rediscovered
- Universities founded
- Historical records compiled (inaccurate)

**Year 700-1100:**
- Magic system codified (based on fragments)
- Vampire ruins mapped (partially)
- Secret societies compete for relics
- Some truths actively suppressed

**Year 1100-1200:**
- Renewed interest in vampire history
- Better archaeological methods
- Political use of relics increases
- Competition intensifies

#### Cultural Evolution

**Humans:**
- Develop strong civilizations
- Anti-vampire religion dominant
- Internal conflicts frequent
- Multiple kingdoms/empires

**Elves:**
- Retreat, emerge, retreat again
- Preserve some vampire knowledge
- Internal split (traditional vs. progressive)
- Declining population

**Dwarves:**
- Mountain kingdoms flourish
- Master engineering (with vampire influences)
- Emerge as trading power
- Conservative, suspicious of surface

**Goblins:**
- Underground empires expand
- Information brokers and traders

- Distrust but work with all races
