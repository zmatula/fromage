# The Collapse Event - Year 0

This is the pivot point of all history. Everything before led to this moment; everything after stems from it.

---

## Executive Summary

The vampire civilization fell in Year 0 through a cascading systems failure triggered by the Ascension Experiment—an attempt to transcend vampiric limitations that instead tore holes in the dimensional fabric, severed their magical infrastructure, and unleashed energies that killed 94% of vampires within six months. The collapse was neither instant nor purely accidental; it was the culmination of centuries of internal conflict, overextended infrastructure, and hubristic experimentation. Different factions responded differently, some fighting, some fleeing, some choosing to die. The other races, suddenly masterless, descended into chaos before eventually building the current world on vampire bones.

## Part I: What Actually Happened (Shadow Truth)

### The Triggering Event: The Ascension Experiment

**Date**: 14th of Deepwinter, Year 0

**Location**: The Apex Spire, Neth'Korath (Capital of the Eternal Dominion)

**What It Was**:
The Ascension Experiment was the culmination of the Transcendence Program—a 200-year research initiative by the Covenant of the Eternal Mind to overcome vampiric weaknesses (sunlight vulnerability, blood dependency, limited reproduction) while enhancing their strengths. The program had produced numerous breakthroughs, including the protagonist.

**The Goal**:
To create a "dimensional anchor"—a being that could exist simultaneously across multiple planes of existence, theoretically allowing vampires to draw power directly from other dimensions without the costs and limitations of conventional magic.

**What Went Wrong**:

1. **The Resonance Breach**: The experiment succeeded in opening dimensional conduits, but the energy patterns resonated with—and disrupted—the Bloodweb, the magical infrastructure that connected all vampire cities and powered their civilization.

2. **Cascade Failure**: The Bloodweb didn't just fail; it inverted. Instead of channeling ambient magical energy to vampire nodes, it began pulling life force FROM vampires into the dimensional breaches.

3. **The Feedback Loop**: Vampires who died fed more energy into the breaches, which pulled harder, killing more vampires. This cascade completed in approximately 47 hours in the capital region.

4. **The Sundering Wave**: When the Apex Spire finally collapsed, it released a shockwave of unbound dimensional energy that spread outward at approximately 100 miles per day, disrupting Bloodweb connections and killing vampires who were tied into the network.

### The Protagonist's Creation

**Project Name**: The Sanguine Vessels Initiative

**Timeline**: Year -12 to Year 0

**Purpose**: To create vampires who could survive outside the Bloodweb—insurance against infrastructure failure and advance scouts for dimensional exploration.

**The Protagonist** (designation: Vessel Seven):
- Created Year -7
- One of twelve experimental subjects
- Modified to draw power from internal reserves rather than the Bloodweb
- Given enhanced regeneration and magical capacity
- Implanted with memory engrams from senior researchers (hence his fragmented knowledge)
- Considered a partial success—survived independence from Bloodweb but showed "emotional instability" and "excessive attachment to subjects of study"

**Why He Survived**:
1. Was not connected to the Bloodweb when it inverted
2. Was in a shielded laboratory in Vel'Thoras (secondary city, 400 miles from capital)
3. His enhanced regeneration allowed him to survive the Sundering Wave
4. The laboratory's containment systems placed him in stasis when systems detected catastrophic failure

**What Happened to Other Vessels**:
- Vessels 1-4: Died in earlier experimental stages
- Vessels 5-6: Destroyed as "failures" (too unstable)
- Vessels 8-10: Connected to Bloodweb for "optimization testing" when collapse occurred—died
- Vessel 11: Location unknown—possibly survived
- Vessel 12: Never completed—embryonic stage when collapse occurred

### The Six-Month Cascade

**Phase 1: The First 48 Hours (The Burning)**
- Apex Spire explosion visible 200 miles away
- Capital Neth'Korath destroyed—estimated 2 million vampire deaths
- Bloodweb connections in central region severed
- Dimensional breaches open across capital region
- Communication network fails instantly

**Phase 2: Days 3-30 (The Spreading)**
- Sundering Wave expands outward
- Vampires in outlying cities begin dying as wave reaches them
- Those who disconnect from Bloodweb in time survive initially
- Civil order collapses as leadership structure evaporates
- Different factions blame each other
- Fighting breaks out between survivors
- Servant races begin uprisings in power vacuum

**Phase 3: Months 2-3 (The Dying)**
- Wave reaches furthest vampire settlements
- Vampires who survived initial wave begin dying from blood shortage (normal blood no longer sustains them properly—Bloodweb had been enhancing blood potency for centuries)
- Mass starvation among vampires
- Some vampire factions attempt to flee underground/into pocket dimensions
- Servant races fully revolt across all regions
- Dimensional breaches begin collapsing, trapping some vampires in other dimensions

**Phase 4: Months 4-6 (The Silence)**
- Fewer than 100,000 vampires remain of estimated 15 million
- Most survivors are either: isolated, fled to prepared refuges, or in stasis like protagonist
- Surface world descends into chaos
- Infrastructure decay accelerates without vampire maintenance
- Former servant races begin fighting each other for resources

## Part II: Contributing Factors (The Deep Roots)

The Ascension Experiment was the trigger, but the collapse was enabled by centuries of structural weakness.

### Factor 1: Bloodweb Dependency

**The Problem**:
Over 800 years, vampires had become increasingly dependent on the Bloodweb for basic survival. What began as a power enhancement became a life support system.

**How It Happened**:
- Year -800: Bloodweb created as communication/power network
- Year -600: Enhanced to provide ambient magical energy to all vampires
- Year -400: Blood potency enhancement added—natural blood no longer sufficient
- Year -200: Individual vampires began losing ability to survive without connection
- Year -50: Fewer than 5% of vampires could survive disconnection for more than a week

**Why It Wasn't Fixed**:
- The enhancement felt good—why would you remove it?
- Each generation was weaker without it
- Dependency happened gradually, imperceptibly
- Those who warned were dismissed as alarmists
- The Covenant of the Eternal Mind suppressed research into the problem

### Factor 2: Political Fragmentation

**The Three Great Covenants** (by Year -100):

1. **Covenant of the Eternal Mind**: Controlled the Bloodweb and most research; believed in transcendence through knowledge; based in Neth'Korath. Ran the Ascension Experiment.

2. **Covenant of the Crimson Throne**: Military and governance; believed in expansion and control; resented Mind's monopoly on power; based in Vel'Krath.

3. **Covenant of the Silver Twilight**: Spiritualists and traditionalists; believed vampires had lost their way; wanted to return to "pure" vampirism; based in Sel'Naroth.

**The Conflict**:
- Mind and Throne had been in cold war for 200 years
- Twilight played both sides while secretly hoping both would fall
- Mind proceeded with Ascension Experiment partly to gain advantage over Throne
- Throne was planning military coup for Year 1
- Twilight had already begun preparations to survive catastrophe they saw coming

**Why This Mattered**:
- No unified response to collapse was possible
- Factions fought each other instead of working together
- Each faction's survival preparations were secret from others
- Some factions actively sabotaged others during collapse

### Factor 3: Servant Race Tensions

**The Situation by Year -100**:
- Humans: 40% of population, primary laborers, some given minor magical training
- Elves: 20% of population, craftspeople and magical assistants, treated "better" but still property
- Dwarves: 15% of population, miners and engineers, allowed more autonomy in underground works
- Goblins: 25% of population, menial laborers and experiment subjects, treated worst

**Growing Unrest**:
- Year -50: Human rebellion in eastern provinces, brutally suppressed
- Year -30: Elven crafters guild strikes, leaders executed
- Year -20: Dwarven tunnel collapses (sabotage?) kill thousands of vampires
- Year -10: Goblin breeding programs halted due to "quality concerns" (actually resistance)

**Why This Mattered**:
- When vampires began dying, servant races saw opportunity
- No loyalty to masters meant no help during crisis
- Active sabotage accelerated collapse in some regions
- Knowledge of vampire weaknesses used against survivors

### Factor 4: Infrastructure Age

**The Problem**:
Core vampire infrastructure was 800-1000 years old. Maintenance had been deferred for centuries.

**Specific Vulnerabilities**:
- Bloodweb nodes hadn't been inspected in 200+ years
- Power crystals in many cities were at 40% efficiency
- Transportation portals increasingly unstable
- Food production systems failing (blood farms operating at minimal capacity)
- Defensive wards degraded and full of gaps

**Why It Wasn't Fixed**:
- The old systems "still worked"
- New construction was more prestigious than maintenance
- Knowledge of how to repair old systems was being lost
- Each Covenant blamed others for decay in shared systems

### Factor 5: Magical Pollution

**The Problem**:
Centuries of magical experimentation had polluted the dimensional barriers around vampire territory. This made the Ascension Experiment far more dangerous than its designers realized.

**The Evidence** (discovered only after collapse):
- Dimensional "thin spots" throughout capital region
- Accumulated magical waste from failed experiments
- Echo patterns from previous dimensional research
- The Apex Spire was built on one of the worst thin spots

**Why They Didn't Know**:
- Pollution was invisible to standard detection
- Those who studied it were in Twilight Covenant (enemies of Mind)
- Mind dismissed Twilight's warnings as superstition
- The instruments that could have detected it were classified and unused

## Part III: Timeline of Collapse (Detailed)

### Year 0, Month 1: The Breaking

**Day 1 (14th Deepwinter)**
- 03:00 - Ascension Experiment begins
- 07:14 - First dimensional breach opens
- 07:16 - Bloodweb disruption begins
- 07:18 - Cascade failure initiates
- 09:00 - Capital evacuation attempts begin
- 12:00 - Apex Spire begins to destabilize
- 18:30 - Apex Spire collapses
- 18:31 - Sundering Wave released

**Days 2-7**
- Capital region entirely dead
- Wave expands at 100 miles/day
- Secondary cities lose Bloodweb connection
- Communication blackout across civilization
- Military mobilization impossible without communications
- Isolated outposts don't know what's happening

**Days 8-14**
- Wave reaches first secondary cities
- Vel'Thoras (protagonist's location) struck Day 11
- Mass deaths in all connected cities
- Survivors begin fleeing but don't know where to go
- First servant race uprisings

**Days 15-30**
- Wave reaches outer territories
- Covenant of Crimson Throne mobilizes remnants
- Covenant of Silver Twilight retreats to prepared sanctuaries
- Covenant of Eternal Mind effectively destroyed (centered in capital)
- Fighting between Throne and fleeing Mind refugees

### Year 0, Months 2-3: The Dying Time

- Vampires not killed by wave begin starving
- Enhanced blood dependency means normal feeding doesn't work
- Survivors need 10x normal blood intake
- Human blood supply revolts/flees
- Vampire turns on vampire for blood
- Twilight sanctuaries seal themselves
- Throne attempts to establish order, mostly fails
- Underground refuges fill up, close to newcomers
- Surface infrastructure begins rapid decay without magical maintenance

### Year 0, Months 4-6: The Silence

- Fewer than 100,000 vampires survive
- Most in stasis, hiding, or isolated
- Servant races now control surface
- Fighting between human/elf/dwarf/goblin factions
- Vampire ruins begin to be looted
- First attempts to destroy vampire remnants
- Weather patterns disrupted by magical pollution
- Crops fail across former vampire territories

### Year 0, Months 7-12: The First Year After

- Vampire survivors further reduced to perhaps 50,000
- Most concentrated in hidden refuges
- Some emerge to feed, are hunted
- Surface races form first alliances against vampire survivors
- Major vampire ruins systematically destroyed
- Knowledge destruction begins (burning of records, killing of collaborators)
- New power structures begin forming among free races

## Part IV: Regional Variations

### Central Region (Neth'Korath and Surroundings)
- **Population**: 5 million vampires
- **Survival Rate**: <0.1%
- **What Happened**: Ground zero of collapse. Dimensional breaches remained open for months. Entire region became uninhabitable wasteland. Even today (Year 1200), central region is blasted landscape called "The Scar" that no one enters.

### Northern Territories (Vel'Krath - Crimson Throne)
- **Population**: 3 million vampires
- **Survival Rate**: 2%
- **What Happened**: Military organization allowed partial evacuation. Throne leaders attempted to establish order among survivors. Eventually overwhelmed by combination of starvation and servant uprisings. Ruins relatively intact due to strong construction.

### Eastern Provinces (Vel'Thoras - Research Centers)
- **Population**: 2 million vampires
- **Survival Rate**: 1%
- **What Happened**: Research facilities had better shielding. Some experiments (including protagonist) survived in stasis. Heavy looting in immediate aftermath due to valuable materials. Now primarily underground ruins.

### Western Reaches (Sel'Naroth - Silver Twilight)
- **Population**: 2 million vampires
- **Survival Rate**: 5%
- **What Happened**: Twilight had prepared. Sealed sanctuaries preserved several thousand vampires. These may still exist in Year 1200. Region is now heavily forested, ruins overgrown and hidden.

### Southern Expanse (Mining and Agricultural)
- **Population**: 3 million vampires
- **Survival Rate**: 0.5%
- **What Happened**: Least shielded, most dependent on Bloodweb for harsh environment survival. Nearly total death. Dwarven slaves took over mines immediately. Minimal ruins, maximum resource extraction by successor races.

## Part V: Who Survived and Why

### Survival Category 1: Disconnected (2%)
Vampires not connected to Bloodweb when it inverted.
- Experimental subjects like protagonist
- Exiles cut off as punishment
- Hermits who rejected society
- Some prisoners

**Fate**: Most died later from starvation or hunting. A few adapted to reduced feeding, went into long hibernation cycles.

### Survival Category 2: Shielded (3%)
Vampires in locations with strong magical protection.
- Deep laboratory facilities
- Twilight sanctuaries
- Military bunkers
- Pocket dimensions

**Fate**: Many still alive in Year 1200, either in stasis or isolated communities. Do not interact with surface world.

### Survival Category 3: Fled (1%)
Vampires who escaped to other dimensions or extreme isolation.
- Dimensional travelers
- Deep underground refugees
- Those with personal pocket realms

**Fate**: Unknown. Some may have established civilizations elsewhere. Some are certainly dead. Some may return.

### Survival Category 4: Changed (<0.1%)
Vampires who survived by becoming something else.
- Merged with other entities
- Became undead in different way
- Transcended physicality

**Fate**: These are the monsters in deep ruins, the things that are not quite vampires anymore.

### The Non-Survivors

**Why Most Died**:
1. Bloodweb dependency (instant death when it inverted)
2. Starvation (couldn't feed properly without enhancement)
3. Hunted by servant races
4. Killed by other vampires (competition for resources)
5. Suicide (couldn't face the loss)
6. Dimensional breaches (pulled into other realms)
7. Magical pollution (slow poisoning in affected areas)

## Part VI: What Was Lost

### Immediately Lost (Within 6 Months)

**Population**: 99%+ of vampires
**Leadership**: All three Covenant heads, most council members
**Communication**: Entire Bloodweb network
**Transportation**: Portal system completely collapsed
**Energy**: Power crystals drained or destroyed
**Food Production**: Blood farms destroyed/abandoned
**Military**: Command structure gone, most forces dead
**Knowledge**: Living experts in most fields

### Lost Over Time (6 Months - 100 Years)

**Magical Knowledge**: Without practitioners, techniques forgotten
**Engineering**: Vampire construction methods lost
**Medicine**: Advanced healing arts gone
**Agriculture**: Magical farming collapsed to basic methods
**Metalworking**: Enchanted forges grew cold
**Records**: Libraries looted, burned, or sealed
**Language**: Vampire high speech became dead language
**Art**: Cultural works destroyed or hidden

### What Remains (Year 1200)

**Physical**:
- Ruins (mostly underground, dangerous)
- Artifacts (most don't work without Bloodweb)
- Bones and preserved corpses
- Some intact sealed facilities
- Degraded infrastructure

**Knowledge**:
- Fragments in other races' records
- Oral traditions (corrupted)
- Artifacts that can be studied
- What protagonist remembers
- What hidden survivors know

**Functional**:
- Some artifacts still work (those with internal power)
- Certain locations still have residual magic
- Sealed facilities can potentially be reactivated
- Protagonist's abilities

## Part VII: The Evidence (What Remains in Present Day)

### Physical Evidence

**The Scar**: Blasted wasteland where capital was. Still magically contaminated. Nothing grows. Occasional dimensional instabilities. Avoided by all.

**Underground Ruins**: Thousands of vampire structures underground. Some explored, most not. Dangerous due to traps, guardians, instability, and worse things.

**Artifacts**: Thousands scattered across world. Most inert without Bloodweb. Some functional. Highly valued, poorly understood.

**Preserved Sites**: Some facilities in stasis or sealed. Contain intact knowledge but nearly impossible to access.

**Corpses**: Vampire remains found regularly. Some preserved by magic. Source of study and superstition.

### Documentary Evidence

**Vampire Records**: Fragments survived. Mostly indecipherable (language loss, context loss). Scholars study them. Much misinformation.

**Servant Records**: Accounts from humans/elves/dwarves/goblins who lived through collapse. Biased but informative. Form basis of most modern understanding.

**Archaeological Evidence**: Excavations reveal physical culture. Interpretations vary wildly. Each generation reinterprets.

**Living Witnesses**: If any vampires survive (beyond protagonist), they would be evidence. Most people don't believe any exist.

### Magical Evidence

**Residual Energies**: Collapse left magical traces. Can be detected by skilled practitioners. Tell partial story.

**Dimensional Scars**: Weak points in reality from the breaches. Occasionally cause problems. Proof something catastrophic happened.

**Magical Artifacts**: Functioning artifacts prove vampire capabilities. Non-functioning artifacts raise questions.

**The Protagonist**: Living evidence of vampire magical achievement. His abilities demonstrate what was possible.

## Part VIII: Contradictory Interpretations

### Version 1: Divine Punishment (Human Popular Belief)

**The Narrative**:
"The vampires were demons who defied the gods. They drank blood and enslaved the righteous races. In their arrogance, they tried to become gods themselves. The Divines struck them down with holy fire, freeing the enslaved and destroying the wicked. The Scar is the mark of divine judgment. The ruins are cursed places where evil lingers. Vampires were destroyed completely, and any who remain are abominations to be destroyed."

**What This Gets Right**:
- Vampires did push too far
- There was an experimental trigger
- The collapse was catastrophic
- Most vampires did die

**What This Gets Wrong**:
- No divine intervention occurred
- Vampires weren't demons, just another species
- Collapse was self-inflicted, not external punishment
- Survivors exist
- Ruins contain knowledge, not curses

**Who Benefits from This Story**:
- Human religious institutions (validates their theology)
- Human nobility (justifies their rule as divinely sanctioned)
- Anyone who wants vampire ruins left alone

### Version 2: Internal Civil War (Elven Scholarly Consensus)

**The Narrative**:
"The vampire covenants warred among themselves. The Covenant of the Eternal Mind created a weapon of mass destruction and used it on their rivals, but it backfired. The collapse was the result of mutually assured destruction. The different regions show different damage patterns because each covenant had different defenses. The war had been building for centuries, and the final battle was the result. Vampires destroyed themselves through inability to cooperate."

**What This Gets Right**:
- Covenants were in conflict
- Mind covenant did trigger collapse
- There was intentional experimentation
- Cooperation failure contributed to totality of collapse

**What This Gets Wrong**:
- It wasn't a weapon, it was a transcendence attempt
- The damage wasn't from vampire-on-vampire violence
- Other covenants were victims, not combatants in this specific event
- The Bloodweb dependency was the key factor, not warfare

**Who Benefits from This Story**:
- Elven academics (makes them the sophisticated analysts)
- Anyone who wants to study ruins (removes divine curse concern)
- Political factions (parallels to current conflicts, lesson about cooperation)

### Version 3: Infrastructure Collapse (Dwarven Practical View)

**The Narrative**:
"The vampires built too much too fast on foundations they didn't maintain. Their whole civilization depended on magical infrastructure that was failing. When one part broke, it all broke. Classic overextension and deferred maintenance. We see it in their construction—impressive but not built to last. They had all that power but forgot the basics. Their cities are full of bad engineering covered up by magic that doesn't work anymore."

**What This Gets Right**:
- Infrastructure was failing
- Bloodweb dependency was critical
- Maintenance had been deferred
- Magic covered up fundamental problems

**What This Gets Wrong**:
- The Ascension Experiment was the trigger, not random failure
- Infrastructure could have lasted longer without that trigger
- Vampire engineering was actually excellent—the magic failing revealed it, not bad fundamentals
- This wasn't gradual decay but catastrophic triggered event

**Who Benefits from This Story**:
- Dwarven engineers (positions them as the practical ones)
- Anyone who wants to salvage vampire technology
- Merchants (pragmatic view supports commercial archaeology)

### Version 4: Slave Rebellion (Goblin Oral Tradition)

**The Narrative**:
"The slaves rose up. For a thousand years the blood-drinkers used us—fed on us, experimented on us, treated us as things. But we remembered. We planned. When the time came, we struck. We poisoned their blood. We sabotaged their machines. We killed them in their sleep. The fire from the sky was their own magic, turned against them by our shamans. They fled underground where some still hide, afraid of us. We are the ones who ended them."

**What This Gets Right**:
- Slaves did rebel during collapse
- Sabotage did occur and contributed
- Vampires did flee underground
- Some may still be hiding

**What This Gets Wrong**:
- Rebellion was result of collapse, not cause
- Poisoning didn't work that way
- The fire wasn't redirected slave magic
- Slaves struck after vampires were already dying
- Goblins couldn't have actually caused the Bloodweb failure

**Who Benefits from This Story**:
- Goblin cultural identity (agency and power)
- Goblin political movements (justifies claims to former vampire territories)
- Anyone resisting current power structures (inspiration)

### Version 5: Forbidden Knowledge (The Veil Keepers Secret Society)

**The Narrative**:
"The vampires opened a door that should have stayed closed. The Ascension Experiment breached the walls between worlds, and something came through. The collapse wasn't just system failure—it was an invasion from beyond reality. The vampires didn't all die; some were taken. The dimensional breaches weren't accidents; they were opened from the other side. Something is still out there, waiting. The ruins are not just dangerous; they are doorways that must stay sealed. The protagonist isn't a survivor; he's a vessel prepared for something that hasn't arrived yet."

**What This Gets Right**:
- Dimensional breach was the trigger
- Something did come through (energy, not entities)
- The experiment opened connections that shouldn't exist
- Ruins can be doorways/dangerous
- Protagonist was created as a "vessel"

**What This Gets Wrong**:
- No intelligent invasion occurred
- The breaches were outgoing, not incoming
- Vampires died, weren't "taken"
- Protagonist isn't waiting for possession
- The Veil Keepers' fear has outpaced reality... probably

**Who Benefits from This Story**:
- The Veil Keepers (justifies their existence and secrecy)
- Anyone who wants ruins quarantined
- Those who fear magic and want it limited

### The Shadow Truth

All versions contain pieces of reality:
- Divine punishment? No, but the collapse did stem from hubris
- Civil war? The covenants' inability to cooperate sealed their fate
- Infrastructure? The Bloodweb dependency was the key vulnerability
- Slave rebellion? The servant races did contribute to the totality of collapse
- Forbidden knowledge? The Ascension Experiment did open dangerous doors

The truth is that the collapse was **multi-causal**, **self-inflicted**, and **both sudden and long-building**. It was a perfect storm of:
- A specific triggering event (Ascension Experiment)
- Critical infrastructure vulnerability (Bloodweb dependency)
- Political dysfunction (covenant conflict)
- Social instability (servant race tensions)
- Environmental degradation (magical pollution)
- Maintenance failure (aging systems)

Any ONE of these might have been survived. All together were fatal.

## Part IX: Consequence Cascade

### Immediate Aftermath: Year 0-10

**Year 0-1**: The Dying
- 99%+ vampire population loss
- Complete infrastructure collapse
- Servant race liberation/chaos
- Initial looting and destruction
- First inter-race conflicts
- Starvation and displacement
- Destruction of vampire knowledge

**Year 1-5**: The Scramble
- Territories claimed by former servants
- Warlords rise and fall rapidly
- Mass migrations
- Plague outbreaks (magical medical knowledge lost)
- Famine (magical agriculture lost)
- Religious movements emerge
- Vampire hunting becomes organized

**Year 5-10**: First Consolidation
- Strongest warlords establish domains
- Initial borders form
- Trade routes reestablish
- Deliberate destruction of vampire records
- "Collaborator" purges (anyone with vampire knowledge)
- First generation born free
- Memory begins to shift

### Power Vacuum Effects: Year 10-50

**Political**:
- Proto-kingdoms emerge
- Constant warfare between aspiring rulers
- Racial tensions (who was favored under vampires? who suffered most?)
- No single power can dominate like vampires did
- Alliances shift constantly

**Economic**:
- Trade networks reform on non-magical basis
- Resource conflicts (especially former vampire mines/farms)
- Technology regression as magical tools fail
- New crafting traditions emerge
- Slavery continues in different forms

**Cultural**:
- Racial identities solidify
- Origin myths form
- Religions codify
- Vampire past increasingly mythologized
- Literate tradition nearly lost

**Knowledge**:
- Vampire libraries destroyed or sealed
- New scholars must start nearly from scratch
- Magical traditions fragment
- Medical knowledge regresses significantly
- Engineering limited to non-magical methods

### Long-Term Impacts: Year 50-1200

**By Year 100**:
- Stable kingdoms exist but constantly warring
- Vampires are historical/mythological
- Ruins are known but feared
- Artifacts occasionally found, rarely understood
- Magic is rare and weakened
- Technology is medieval

**By Year 500**:
- Modern empires' predecessors established
- Vampire history heavily mythologized
- Ruins become treasure hunting sites
- Secret societies form around vampire knowledge
- Magic traditions diverge significantly
- Religions incorporate vampire narrative

**By Year 1000**:
- Current political structure emerging
- Vampire past is ancient history
- Scholarly study of ruins begins
- Artifacts more systematically collected
- Magic stabilizes at current (lower) level
- The collapse is a moral lesson, not lived memory

**By Year 1200 (Present)**:
- Vampires are legend to most people
- Ruins are valuable and dangerous
- Artifacts are rare treasures
- Secret societies have significant power
- No one knows a vampire survivor exists
- The truth is buried under 1200 years of narrative
